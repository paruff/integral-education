## AQAL Glossary

Plain-language definitions for AQAL and developmental psychology terms used throughout the module library. Each definition is a starting point — module content provides the full context.

---

### AQAL

Integral framework developed by Ken Wilber. Stands for All Quadrants, All Levels, All Lines, All States, All Types. A map of human experience and development across five dimensions.

### Centre of Gravity

The developmental stage where a person consistently operates — their default way of making meaning, not a score or a label. A person may temporarily access higher stages while their centre of gravity remains at an earlier level.

### Causal State

A state of consciousness characterised by pure awareness without objects — the experience of being a witness to experience rather than being identified with any particular thought, feeling, or sensation. Also called the Witness.

### Conventional

In developmental psychology: reasoning and meaning-making that operates within the norms, roles, and authority structures of one's community or culture. Kohlberg's conventional morality and Kegan's interpersonal stage are examples.

### Construct-Aware

A post-conventional stage (Cook-Greuter) where a person becomes aware that meaning is constructed — that their own worldview is a lens, not direct access to reality. This awareness can be destabilising before it becomes generative.

### Developmental Line

A specific domain of development (cognitive, emotional, moral, interpersonal, somatic, spiritual, self) that grows through predictable stages. Lines develop at different rates — someone can be highly developed cognitively while their emotional line lags behind.

### Ego Development

The growth of the self's capacity to make meaning, regulate emotion, and relate to others. Measured empirically through instruments like Loevinger's Sentence Completion Test. Cook-Greuter extended this model into post-conventional stages.

### Emotional Line

The capacity to feel, differentiate, and regulate emotions — from basic emotional awareness through emotional granularity to integrated emotional intelligence.

### First-Person Perspective

The interior, subjective view — your direct experience of thoughts, feelings, sensations. The "I" quadrant in AQAL. Contrast with second-person (relational) and third-person (objective) perspectives.

### Flow State

A state of complete absorption in an activity where self-consciousness disappears, time perception shifts, and performance peaks. Described by Csikszentmihalyi. A temporary state, not a permanent stage.

### Gross State

The ordinary waking state of consciousness — what most people experience during daily life. The baseline against which more refined states (subtle, causal, nondual) are distinguished.

### Holon

A term from Arthur Koestler adopted by Wilber: something that is simultaneously a whole and a part. A cell is a whole in itself and a part of an organ. Every developmental stage is a holon — it transcends and includes the previous stage.

### ILP

Integral Life Practice. A cross-training approach that applies AQAL's multiple dimensions to personal growth: simultaneous practice across body, mind, spirit, and shadow rather than focusing on a single domain.

### Integral

In developmental theory: a stage of development (tier 2 / teal) that integrates multiple perspectives into a coherent whole. More broadly: a framework or approach that attempts to include rather than exclude viewpoints from different domains, disciplines, or cultures.

### Integral Life Practice

See ILP.

### Interpersonal Line

The capacity to relate to others — including empathy, perspective-taking, conflict navigation, and collaborative meaning-making. This line is distinct from the emotional line but develops in close relation to it.

### Intersubjective

The "We" quadrant in AQAL: shared meaning, culture, and mutual understanding. What exists between people rather than inside a single person (subjective) or outside as observable behaviour (objective).

### Level

In AQAL: a developmental stage — a relatively stable structure of meaning-making, values, and identity. Each level transcends and includes the previous one. See also: Centre of Gravity.

### Meaning-Making

The process by which humans construct understanding from experience. Central to Kegan's constructive-developmental theory: how we make meaning changes in predictable ways as we develop.

### Metasystematic

A postformal cognitive stage where a person can think about systems of systems — coordinating multiple frameworks, recognising that each framework has its own assumptions, and integrating across them. Also called vision-logic.

### Moral Line

The capacity for ethical reasoning and moral judgment — from obedience to external rules through principled reasoning to post-conventional integration of care and justice.

### Nondual

A state of consciousness where the subject-object distinction dissolves — there is no separate observer watching experience, just experience unfolding. Not a stage but a state that can be accessed temporarily through practice.

### Perspective-Taking

The capacity to see a situation from another person's point of view — not just intellectually understanding their position but genuinely inhabiting their frame of reference. A core skill in the interpersonal and cognitive lines.

### Post-Conventional

Developmental stages beyond the conventional level — where a person no longer takes their community's norms and values as given but constructs their own framework for evaluating them. Includes individualist, strategist, construct-aware, and unitive stages.

### Postformal

Cognitive stages beyond Piaget's formal operations — the capacity to think in systems, hold paradox, and integrate multiple frameworks rather than applying a single correct method.

### Pre/Trans Fallacy

The error of confusing pre-rational states (regression, infantile fusion) with trans-rational states (genuine integration, nondual awareness). Both can look similar from the outside — intense affect, non-ordinary experience — but are structurally opposite in terms of developmental capacity.

### Projection

The unconscious act of attributing your own disowned feelings, traits, or impulses to someone else. A core mechanism in shadow formation. If you react strongly to a quality in another person, ask: does this quality exist in me?

### Quadrant

One of four irreducible perspectives in AQAL: I (subjective-interior), We (intersubjective-cultural), It (objective-exterior), Its (interobjective-systems). Every situation can be viewed through all four quadrants.

### Second-Person Perspective

The relational, dialogical view — the "We" space between self and other. Not just what I experience (first-person) or what I observe (third-person), but what we create together.

### Self-Authorship

Kegan's term for the capacity to construct one's own values, beliefs, and identity rather than having them supplied by external authority. The defining achievement of the transition from the socialised mind (stage 3) to the self-authoring mind (stage 4).

### Self Line

The developmental line concerned with identity — your sense of who you are, how you understand yourself, and how that self-understanding evolves through stages.

### Shadow

Disowned aspects of the self — qualities, impulses, and capacities pushed out of conscious awareness and often projected onto others. Shadow includes both negative qualities (the "dark" shadow) and positive qualities (the "golden" shadow).

### Somatic Line

The capacity for body-based knowing — accurate interoception (sensing internal body states), somatic pattern recognition, and somatic attunement to others. Distinct from cognitive emotional intelligence.

### Spiral Dynamics

A developmental model by Don Beck and Chris Cowan (based on Clare Graves' research) that maps value systems as "vMEMEs" emerging in response to changing life conditions. Wilber adopted Spiral Dynamics' colour-coding for his levels of development.

### Spiritual Line

The developmental line concerned with ultimate concerns — how a person relates to meaning, purpose, transcendence, and the sacred. This line develops through stages (Fowler's stages of faith) independently of other lines.

### Subject-Object

In Kegan's constructive-developmental theory: what a person can take as "object" (look at, reflect on) vs. what they are "subject to" (embedded in, cannot see). Development means converting subject into object — what controlled you becomes something you can examine.

### Subtle State

A state of consciousness characterised by interior quiet, spaciousness, or felt energy — different from ordinary waking. Often accessed through meditation, breathwork, or contemplative practice.

### Third-Person Perspective

The objective, external view — observable behaviour, measurable data, systems analysis. The "It" and "Its" quadrants in AQAL. Contrast with first-person (interior) and second-person (relational) perspectives.

### Transcend and Include

A core AQAL principle: each developmental stage transcends (goes beyond) the limitations of the previous stage while including (preserving) its essential capacities. Growth does not discard earlier stages — it builds on them. A cell is transcended and included by an organ, which is transcended and included by an organism.

### Vision-Logic

See Metasystematic.

### vMEME

In Spiral Dynamics: a value system or "attractor" that shapes how individuals and cultures interpret the world and organise their priorities. Each vMEME emerges in response to specific life conditions.

### Witness

See Causal State.
