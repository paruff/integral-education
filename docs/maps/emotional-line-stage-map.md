## 🗺️ Emotional Line Stage Map (Amber through Integral)

## Overview

The **Emotional developmental line** tracks the growth of affective differentiation, regulation capacity, emotional vocabulary, and relational emotional intelligence across developmental stages. Like all developmental lines in the AQAL framework, the Emotional line follows its own arc — it can advance or lag relative to other lines, producing characteristic patterns of asynchrony. A learner may hold a sophisticated cognitive map of their emotional architecture while still enacting an earlier stage's regulation patterns under pressure, or may possess rich felt-sense differentiation without the interpersonal vocabulary to communicate it across difference.

This map uses the **stage axis** employed throughout the platform — Amber/Mythic, Rational/Orange, Pluralistic/Green, and Integral/Teal — corresponding to the developmental altitudes most commonly active in adult learning populations. These stage labels describe **centre-of-gravity patterns**, not fixed types. Real individuals show variation across contexts, and emotional development rarely moves in a straight line; regression under stress is normal and does not indicate developmental failure.

The map is anchored in constructive-developmentalist research (Cook-Greuter, Loevinger, Kegan) and affective science frameworks (Barrett's constructed-emotion theory, Gross's process model of emotion regulation, the RULER framework). Each stage characterisation is a **modal description**, not a diagnostic category. Readers are encouraged to hold these descriptions with curiosity rather than identification or self-judgment.

**How to use this map:**
- Locate a characteristic pattern in the table without treating it as a complete portrait of any individual.
- Use the shadow table to identify which shadow pattern is generating friction in a current relational or professional context.
- Use the growth edge column as a compass rather than a prescription — not "I should be doing this" but "this is the direction available from here."
- Return to this map after completing the [Emotional Line Overview & Orientation](../modules/emotional-line-overview-orientation) module and the stage orientation modules listed in the cross-references below.

This map should be reviewed by a subject-matter expert before use in any facilitated or clinical context. Stage characterisations reflect cultural and linguistic patterns that may not generalise across all cultural contexts without adaptation.

## Stage × Emotional Line Matrix

The table below characterises emotional experience across seven dimensions at each of the four principal developmental altitudes addressed by this platform. Each cell contains a descriptive pattern. Individual experience is always more varied and contextually nuanced than any map cell can represent; hold these characterisations as starting points for inquiry, not as labels.

| Stage | Characteristic Emotional Experience | Dominant Regulation Style | Emotional Vocabulary Range | Relational Emotional Pattern | Characteristic Shadow | Growth Edge |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Amber / Mythic** (Conventional) | Emotion is experienced as cosmically or divinely caused — feelings arrive as messages from God, fate, moral order, or the collective. The individual does not yet fully distinguish personal emotional experience from collective emotional truth; what the group feels is what *is* real. Intense affect (grief, joy, fear, anger) is typically channelled into shared ritual containers: prayer, ceremony, mourning rites, collective celebration. Emotions that fit the group's moral narrative are experienced as righteous and real; emotions that violate it are experienced as temptation, sin, or shameful weakness. | Regulation through ritual, prayer, group conformity, and external moral authority. The primary strategy is alignment with collective norms: "feel what the group sanctions, suppress what it forbids." Religious and cultural scripts provide ready-made containers for affect. Regulatory capacity is genuinely protective at this stage — ritual holds experience that might otherwise be unbearable — but breaks down when the approved container does not fit the actual emotional reality. | Vocabulary is limited to culturally sanctioned emotion categories. Broad terms (glad, sad, afraid, angry, grateful) are used with sincerity. Nuanced or ambiguous emotional states — shame that also contains grief, love mixed with resentment, pride that contains fear — tend to be resolved into the available sanctioned category rather than held in their complexity. Emotion words carry strong moral valence: some emotions are "good" (devotion, reverence, gratitude) and others are "bad" (envy, lust, doubt). | Shared group emotion and emotional conformity. Relational health is maintained through aligned feeling: celebrating the same victories, grieving the same losses, fearing the same threats. Emotional divergence from the group threatens belonging and is experienced as disloyalty or spiritual failure. Empathy operates most readily within the in-group; emotional imaginative reach to those outside the group narrative is limited. | Suppression of emotions that violate group norms, leading to emotional splitting: sanctioned feelings are amplified and performed; unsanctioned feelings are denied, projected onto out-groups, or expressed indirectly through somatic symptoms, passive aggression, or sudden explosive discharge. The suppressed affect is not absent — it is simply not owned as *one's own*. | Differentiation of personal from collective emotional experience. Beginning to name and tolerate the feeling that arises *before* the cultural script arrives — noticing "I feel something here that doesn't fit the approved category" — is the growth edge available from Amber. This requires sufficient psychological safety and a container outside the primary group. |
| **Rational / Orange** (Achievement) | Emotion is experienced as information to be managed for performance, efficiency, and goal achievement. Feelings are acknowledged instrumentally — "I notice frustration, which signals I need to adjust my strategy" — rather than inhabited as the intrinsic signal they carry. Strong emotional activation is experienced as interference; the ideal state is composed, focused, motivated arousal directed toward outcomes. Success emotions (pride, enthusiasm, competitive energy) are valued; vulnerability emotions (grief, fear, longing) are treated as liabilities to be contained. | Regulation through suppression, cognitive reappraisal toward productivity, and strategic deployment. The dominant strategy is "get the emotion under control so I can perform." Reappraisal is real — Orange can genuinely shift the meaning of an emotional event — but the reappraisal target is performance utility rather than emotional truth. Emotional discomfort is processed privately and efficiently; seeking emotional support from others is experienced as dependency and therefore weakness. Mindfulness and biofeedback tools may be adopted at this stage, but typically in service of performance optimisation rather than emotional integration. | Vocabulary is technically adequate — Orange often acquires a sophisticated emotional lexicon through therapy, coaching, or self-help — but it is deployed instrumentally rather than as authentic felt-sense expression. Emotion words function as diagnostic categories: naming the feeling is a step toward managing it, not an end in itself. Emotional vocabulary is richer for achievement-valenced emotions (motivation, confidence, frustration, focus) than for relational or vulnerability-valenced emotions (longing, shame, grief, awe). | Emotional efficiency and discomfort with emotional ambiguity. Relationships are managed with the same competence orientation applied to projects. Emotional conversations are most comfortable when they are goal-directed: "what do we need to work out so we can move forward?" Open-ended emotional exploration that does not resolve into actionable insight is experienced as inefficient or self-indulgent. Vulnerability in others may be received with genuine problem-solving intent — "how can I help you fix this?" — that inadvertently forecloses emotional presence. | Emotional bypassing in service of performance. Affective material that does not contribute to goal achievement is suppressed, redirected, or pathologised as "overthinking." Accumulated emotional data — grief, longing, existential dissatisfaction — builds without adequate processing, and eventually expresses through burnout, psychosomatic symptoms, relationship breakdown, or a sudden mid-life reorientation when the performance frame no longer holds. The shadow is not absence of feeling but absence of permission to feel. | Recognising emotion as data rather than interference. The growth edge from Orange is to discover that emotional experience is not the enemy of effective action but one of its richest information sources. This typically requires experiencing the cost of sustained emotional bypassing — burnout, relational disconnection, motivational collapse — before the performance frame loosens enough to allow emotional data in. |
| **Pluralistic / Green** (Relational-Egalitarian) | Strong emotional validation and expressiveness; high tolerance for emotional complexity and contradictory simultaneous feelings. Emotional experience is celebrated as authentic self-expression and treated as intrinsically valuable. Feelings are neither cosmically caused nor instrumentally managed — they arise from *here*, in this body, in this relationship, as real and worthy of full attention. The full affective spectrum is welcomed in principle; vulnerability is honoured rather than pathologised. | High tolerance for emotional complexity but sometimes without adequate regulation structure. Green's expanded emotional range may outpace the development of felt-sense differentiation (emotional granularity) and distress tolerance, resulting in states of emotional flooding or overwhelm that feel meaningful but are difficult to navigate. The regulation strategies available are relational and community-based: processing feelings in dialogue, receiving witness and validation, participating in emotionally safe group containers. Private emotional management skills developed at Orange may be abandoned without being replaced by something more developmentally coherent. | Rich and expansive emotional vocabulary — often incorporating somatic, relational, cultural, and spiritual frames. Affective precision is a genuine Green value; communities at this stage create shared vocabulary for nuanced feeling states. The vocabulary range is among the broadest available at first-tier altitudes. However, vocabulary fluency does not always track regulation capacity: a person can have precise words for an emotional state while still being unable to modulate it or maintain perspective within it. | Emotional merging and empathic flooding. Green's commitment to emotional availability and empathic presence can dissolve appropriate self-other distinction, leading to secondary trauma, compassion fatigue, and empathic flooding — taking on others' emotional states as one's own without a stable witnessing position. Boundaries are experienced as relational violations rather than acts of care; saying "no" to another's emotional need activates guilt and threatens belonging. At the collective level, this pattern may produce emotional tyranny disguised as inclusivity — the implicit rule that emotional intensity grants moral authority in the group. | Undifferentiated empathy leading to secondary trauma, emotional exhaustion, and the use of emotional intensity as a substitute for emotional intelligence. Emotional flooding can masquerade as depth; emotional reactivity can masquerade as passion and authenticity. The shadow includes a subtle entitlement around emotional expression ("my feelings matter and you must hold them") that can suppress the emotional autonomy of those in relationship with Green. Additionally, the consensus-validation dynamic of pluralistic communities can reinforce emotional stories that need examining rather than validating. | Healthy boundaries with full emotional attunement. The growth edge from Green is to develop a stable witnessing position from which emotional experience can be fully felt *without* being identical to it — maintaining self-other distinction, practising self-regulation alongside co-regulation, and discovering that healthy emotional boundaries deepen rather than diminish genuine empathy. |
| **Integral / Teal** (Systemic-Evolutionary) | Emotional experience as developmental data: feelings are held as multi-perspectival informants — about the self, the relationship, the cultural context, and the developmental moment. The full affective range is genuinely welcomed without the emotional merging characteristic of Green; there is both felt presence in the emotion and perspective on it simultaneously. Emotional experience is understood as constructed (in Barrett's sense), culturally shaped, developmentally patterned, and intrinsically real — none of these framings excludes the others. Strong affects (grief, joy, anger, fear) can be inhabited fully without flooding, and subtle affects (anticipatory dread, ambient shame, quiet longing) are tracked with precision. | Sophisticated regulation with contextual flexibility. The Teal practitioner can shift between first-person inhabiting, second-person witnessing, and third-person pattern-recognition within a single emotional episode — choosing the appropriate stance for the relational or developmental context. Regulation strategies are multiple and selected with discernment: somatic grounding for body-held activation, cognitive reappraisal for meaning-level distortions, relational co-regulation for interpersonal activation, contemplative presence for existential affect. The goal is neither management for performance nor immersion for authenticity but calibrated response that honours the emotion's signal while maintaining functional agency. | Vocabulary is precise, culturally informed, and developmentally aware. Teal can name affective states at multiple levels of granularity — broad categories, nuanced sub-types, somatic correlates, appraisal structures, developmental stage origins — and can translate between emotional vocabularies to communicate across stage differences. The practitioner knows that emotion words are culturally and developmentally shaped, and holds them as useful pointers rather than transparent windows. Vocabulary precision extends to developmental meta-language: distinguishing a stage-specific emotion (Green's empathic flooding) from a universal human feeling (grief) without collapsing one into the other. | Co-regulation across significant difference. Teal can remain emotionally present to people and patterns at very different developmental altitudes without fusing, managing, or pathologising. This includes holding the emotional distress of someone at Amber without performing Amber's emotional logic, receiving Orange's affective bypassing without being recruited into it, and resourcing Green's empathic flooding without merging or collapsing. The characteristic relational quality is warm differentiation — genuine care expressed within maintained self-other clarity. | Subtle emotional superiority or developmental hierarchy in relationships. The Teal practitioner's developmental awareness can silently organise relational emotion around stage comparisons: feeling a faint impatience with Amber's rigidity, a mild contempt for Orange's bypassing, a residual condescension toward Green's flooding — even when these reactions are not consciously acknowledged. The shadow also includes using emotional precision as a performance of development: deploying refined affective vocabulary as a marker of stage altitude rather than as genuine communication. At its subtlest, Teal shadow involves staying in a witnessing meta-position as a way of avoiding the full force of an emotion — treating emotional intelligence as a skill to be practised rather than a dimension of life to be fully inhabited. | Full embodiment of emotional development without the self-monitoring overlay. The growth edge from Teal is to allow emotional experience to arise and move with full developmental sophistication and without constant internal commentary on its stage characteristics — to feel deeply without needing to simultaneously map the feeling. This requires letting the affective intelligence that has been developed function automatically, trusting the regulation and vocabulary capacity that is genuinely available without turning each emotional moment into a developmental exercise. |

## Emotional Line Shadow at Each Stage

The following table focuses specifically on the characteristic shadow of the Emotional line at each stage and describes its typical behavioural expression in learning, relational, and professional contexts. This table should be read alongside the [Shadow & Developmental Lines Integration Map](./shadow-developmental-lines-map).

| Stage | Core Shadow Pattern | Typical Behavioural Expression | Triggering Context | Practice Entry Point |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Amber / Mythic** | Emotional splitting and projection: the suppression of emotions that violate group norms produces a splitting dynamic in which disowned feeling is projected outward onto "other" individuals or groups who are then experienced as the source of threat or moral corruption. | Sudden explosive emotional discharge after prolonged suppression; intense moral outrage toward perceived norm violators; somatic symptoms (tension, illness) that carry unexpressed affect; passive-aggressive expression of emotion that cannot be named directly; collective scapegoating of members who express unsanctioned feelings. | Situations in which personal emotional reality violates the group's emotional script — when personal grief, doubt, anger, or longing does not fit the approved collective feeling. Also triggered by contact with members of out-groups who express unfamiliar emotional repertoires. | Supported, gentle inquiry into the felt experience *before* the group script arrives. Reflective journalling in private, with a trusted companion outside the primary group. Somatic practices that restore sensation as a trustworthy signal. |
| **Rational / Orange** | Emotional bypassing and instrumental management: affect is treated as a productivity variable to be optimised, leading to systematic under-attendance to emotional data that does not serve goal achievement. The shadow of accumulated, unprocessed affect expresses in delayed and displaced ways. | Burnout; somatic symptoms with no identified physical cause; relationship breakdown attributed to "communication problems" that are actually unprocessed emotional distance; sudden motivational collapse after sustained peak performance; irritability and disproportionate reactions to minor obstacles; intellectualised emotional vocabulary that describes feelings accurately without making contact with them. | Performance pressure, high-stakes evaluation, sustained goal pursuit, and situations in which emotional needs conflict directly with task achievement. Also triggered by proximity to others who express vulnerability, which activates contempt or discomfort in Orange. | Psychoeducation about emotion as information: introducing the concept that emotional data improves decision quality and risk awareness rather than impairing it. Body-scan practices that introduce somatic signals as legitimate data. Motivational interviewing approaches that surface the cost of emotional bypassing without requiring vulnerability before trust is established. |
| **Pluralistic / Green** | Empathic flooding and emotional merging: the collapse of self-other differentiation in emotional experience leads to secondary trauma, compassion fatigue, loss of personal emotional continuity, and the unconscious use of emotional intensity as a source of moral authority in group settings. | Persistent fatigue after extended emotional contact with others; compulsive reassurance-seeking or care-giving that leaves the person resourced at their own expense; difficulty distinguishing personal feelings from absorbed relational feelings; use of emotional distress as an implicit claim to moral priority in group processes; discomfort or confusion when healthy emotional limits are encouraged; group dynamics where agreement and emotional safety are maintained by suppressing honest feedback. | Close relational contact, community emotional processing events, conflict in group contexts, and situations requiring emotional self-differentiation from a person in distress. Also triggered by requests to maintain personal emotional limits, which Green experiences as abandonment or hardness. | [Emotional Granularity](../modules/emotional-granularity) practices that build self-other differentiation from the inside. Body-anchor practices that distinguish "my emotion" from "absorbed emotion" through somatic location. Facilitated boundary work that reframes limits as care rather than abandonment. |
| **Integral / Teal** | Subtle developmental superiority and self-monitoring overlay: the extensive development of emotional intelligence and vocabulary paradoxically generates a new shadow — the use of emotional precision as a developmental performance, and the maintenance of a meta-observing stance that prevents full contact with raw affective experience. | Describing emotional experience in ways that display developmental altitude rather than communicate genuine feeling; using stage language to subtly contextualise (and thereby contain) others' emotional responses rather than fully receiving them; persistent self-commentary during emotional experience that functions as distancing rather than insight; impatience with others' emotional "inefficiency" expressed as gentle pedagogical framing rather than honest irritation; treating emotional integration as a project rather than a dimension of lived experience. | Contexts involving significant emotional intensity (grief, rage, fear, profound joy) in which the practitioner's reflexive move is to map the experience rather than inhabit it. Also triggered by close relational contact with highly skilled emotional intelligences at the same stage who cannot easily be contained by developmental framing. | Advanced contemplative practice that deliberately suspends the meta-commentary layer — Zen koan work, somatic floor practice, grief ritual, and relational processes that explicitly disallow developmental interpretation during the experience. [Self-line integration work](../modules/self-line-integration-practice) that targets the self-monitoring function specifically. |

<Admonition type="note" title="🧑‍🏫 Facilitator Note: Applying This Map in Coaching and Group Learning">

**Stage as centre of gravity, not type.** The stage columns describe modal patterns, not fixed categories. Learners will recognise themselves in multiple columns depending on context, stress level, relational proximity, and the specific emotional content involved. Use the map to open inquiry, not to assign labels.

**Honour the shadow without pathologising.** When working with the shadow table, frame shadow patterns as the predictable cost of a stage's genuine strengths — not as deficits or failures. Amber's emotional conformity is the cost of its profound communal belonging. Orange's bypassing is the cost of its genuine achievement capability. Green's empathic flooding is the cost of its extraordinary empathic reach. Teal's self-monitoring overlay is the cost of its hard-won developmental awareness.

**The growth edge is directional, not prescriptive.** The growth edge column describes the developmental direction available from each altitude — not a target the person should be working toward by the end of a session. Growth edges unfold on developmental timescales; facilitators who push them as immediate goals typically produce performance rather than development.

**Use this map alongside, not instead of, lived emotional inquiry.** No map can substitute for the direct experience of working with emotional material in a safe, well-boundaried relational context. This map is a compass, not a destination.

</Admonition>
