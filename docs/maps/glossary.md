# AQAL Glossary

Plain-language definitions for terms used across the platform. Click any term's link to jump to its definition. Each term has a unique anchor ID that can be used as a deep link (e.g., `glossary.md#aqal`).

---

### AQAL {#aqal}

**AQAL** stands for **A**ll **Q**uadrants, **A**ll **L**evels, **A**ll **L**ines, **A**ll **S**tates, **A**ll **T**ypes. It is the comprehensive map of human experience and development developed by Ken Wilber. The platform uses AQAL as its organising framework to ensure no aspect of development is overlooked.

→ See [AQAL Overview](../maps/aqal-overview)

---

### Quadrant {#quadrant}

One of the four fundamental perspectives in AQAL: **Interior-Individual** (I, subjective experience), **Exterior-Individual** (It, observable behaviour), **Interior-Collective** (We, shared values and culture), and **Exterior-Collective** (Its, systems and structures). Every event or phenomenon can be viewed from all four quadrants.

---

### Level / Stage {#stage}

A distinct level of development within any line — a coherent worldview with its own strengths, limitations, and ways of making meaning. Stages unfold in a sequence (e.g., Amber → Orange → Green → Teal) and are also referred to by colour names from Spiral Dynamics. No stage is "bad" — each is a necessary step in a larger developmental journey.

---

### Line {#line}

A specific domain of development that grows through stages relatively independently. Major lines include: **Cognitive** (how we think), **Emotional** (how we feel), **Interpersonal** (how we relate), **Moral** (how we judge right and wrong), **Somatic** (body awareness), **Spiritual** (ultimate concern), and **Self** (identity and ego development). A person may be highly developed in one line and less developed in another.

---

### State {#state}

A temporary condition of consciousness — what you are experiencing *right now*. States include waking (gross), dreaming (subtle), deep sleep (causal), and nondual (witness). Unlike stages (which are enduring), states come and go. However, repeated state access can be stabilised into stage development.

---

### Type {#type}

A relatively stable characteristic that does not necessarily develop through stages. Types include personality styles (e.g., Enneagram, MBTI), learning preferences, gender orientations, and cultural conditioning. AQAL includes types to ensure the map accounts for individual difference, not just developmental sequence.

---

### ILP (Integral Life Practice) {#ilp}

A set of practices designed to develop all major lines across all four quadrants simultaneously. Common ILPs include: meditation (I quadrant), physical exercise (It quadrant), relationship work (We quadrant), and systems thinking (Its quadrant). The principle is "practice in all quadrants" for balanced development.

---

### Tier 1 {#tier-1}

The first major grouping of developmental stages — from Beige/Archaic through Green/Pluralistic. Tier 1 stages are characterised by the belief that *my* worldview is the correct or most advanced one. Each Tier 1 stage tends to see earlier stages as less developed and later stages as threatening or incomprehensible.

---

### Tier 2 {#tier-2}

The second major grouping of developmental stages — beginning with Teal/Integral and beyond. Tier 2 stages are characterised by the capacity to hold multiple worldviews as partially valid while recognising the developmental logic that orders them. Tier 2 does not reject Tier 1 stages but *includes and transcends* them.

---

### Spiral Dynamics {#spiral-dynamics}

A colour-coded model of value systems (memes) that emerge as human conditions change. Each colour represents a distinct way of thinking, valuing, and organising. The model is integrated into AQAL as one way of describing Levels/Stages, using colours for shorthand: Beige, Purple, Red, Blue (Amber), Orange, Green, Yellow (Teal), Turquoise.

---

### Beige / Archaic {#beige}

The earliest value system — focused on basic survival: food, water, warmth, safety. Individual automatic reflexes drive behaviour. Rarely seen as a primary adult value system in modern contexts, but may activate under extreme threat.

---

### Purple / Magical {#purple}

A magical-animistic value system where the world is understood through spirits, rituals, and tribal bonds. Safety is found in belonging to the group. Common in early childhood and traditional societies.

---

### Red / Impulsive {#red}

An impulsive, egocentric value system focused on power, action, and immediate gratification. The self is asserted against rules and constraints. "Might makes right." Often emerges in adolescence or under conditions of breakdown.

---

### Blue / Amber {#amber}

A mythic-order value system organised around absolute truths, rules, and stable hierarchies. Meaning comes from serving a higher purpose or established authority. Provides order, discipline, and meaning — but can struggle with ambiguity and complexity.

---

### Orange / Rational {#orange}

A modern-rational value system driven by achievement, science, autonomy, and merit. Truth is discovered through evidence and reason. Emphasises individual freedom, innovation, and material success. A powerful engine for progress that can overlook systemic and relational dimensions.

---

### Green / Pluralistic {#green}

A pluralistic value system centred on community, equality, dialogue, and sensitivity to marginalisation. Seeks to include all voices and honour multiple perspectives. Places high value on authenticity, empathy, and belonging — but can struggle with decision-making and authority when consensus is required.

---

### Teal / Integral {#teal}

An integral value system that can hold multiple perspectives simultaneously, recognise developmental logic, and flexibly adapt responses to context. Sees the strengths and limitations of each earlier stage. Focuses on flow, integration, and continuous development rather than any fixed worldview.

---

### Turquoise {#turquoise}

A holistic value system that experiences reality as a single, interconnected, dynamic whole. Moves beyond individual perspective into global-systemic awareness. Rarely attained as a stable worldview — more often experienced as peak or state access.

---

### Shadow {#shadow}

The parts of ourselves that we have disowned, repressed, or projected onto others. Shadow content includes qualities we consider unacceptable (anger, weakness, greed) as well as positive qualities we cannot own (strength, talent, beauty). Shadow work is the practice of reclaiming these disowned parts with compassion and awareness.

---

### Projection {#projection}

The unconscious act of attributing one's own disowned feelings, traits, or motives to someone else. If you find yourself having a strong emotional reaction to a quality in another person, it is worth asking: "Do I have this quality myself?" Recognising projections is a core skill in shadow work.

---

### Mastery Loop {#mastery-loop}

The five-step learning cycle used across all platform modules: **Learn** (concept), **Practice** (embodied exercise), **Reflect** (guided self-inquiry), **Assess** (self-assessment rubric), **Integrate** (real-life application). This loop is designed to move knowledge from intellectual understanding into embodied capability.

---

### Anki {#anki}

A spaced-repetition flashcard application used on the platform for retrieval practice. Key concepts from modules are turned into Anki cards to help transfer learning from short-term to long-term memory. The platform provides Anki card decks for each module.

---

### Pre/Trans Fallacy {#pretrans-fallacy}

A conceptual error in which pre-rational states (e.g., infantile fusion, magical thinking) are confused with trans-rational states (e.g., nondual awareness, mystical unity). The fallacy arises because both pre- and trans-rational states appear "non-rational" from a rational perspective, but they are developmentally opposite — one is *before* rationality, the other is *beyond* it.
