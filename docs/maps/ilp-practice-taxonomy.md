## ILP Practice Taxonomy

This taxonomy defines Integral Life Practice (ILP) categories, taxonomy dimensions, progression ladders, fallback alternatives, and safety constraints for implementation in learning pathways and curriculum design.

Canonical references:

- [AQAL Label Crosswalk](../maps/aqal-label-crosswalk)

## 1. Practice Families Overview

| Family | Primary Aim | Typical Formats | AQAL Primary Quadrant |
|---|---|---|---|
| Body | Regulation, vitality, embodiment | Breathwork, mobility, posture resets, walking practice | It (Upper-Right) |
| Somatic | Interoceptive awareness, nervous system regulation, embodied presence | Grounding, body scan, breath awareness, co-regulation | I / It (Upper-Left + Upper-Right) |
| Mind | Attention and cognition | Focus drills, bias checks, metacognitive journaling | I (Upper-Left) |
| Spirit/Meaning | Perspective widening and meaning coherence | Contemplative sits, gratitude, values reflection | I (Upper-Left) |
| Shadow | Projection awareness and integration | Trigger logs, role reversal, parts dialogue | I / We |
| Ethics | Values-in-action and responsibility | Dilemma analysis, commitment contracts | We (Lower-Left) |
| Relationships | Empathy and conflict skill | Dialogue protocols, feedback exchange | We (Lower-Left) |
| Service | Contribution beyond self-interest | Community projects, mentorship micro-actions | Its (Lower-Right) |
| Emotional | Affective differentiation and regulation | Emotion journalling, body tracking, conversational labelling, cognitive reappraisal | I (Upper-Left) |
| Interpersonal | Relational skill and connection quality | Dialogue protocols, partner exercises, self-audit, written repair | We (Lower-Left) |

## 2. Taxonomy Dimensions

All practices are described along five standard dimensions.

| Dimension | Allowed Values |
|---|---|
| Duration band | Short (3–5 min), Medium (10–15 min), Long (20–30 min), Extended (30+ min) |
| Intensity | Low, Moderate, High |
| Mode | Solo, Pair, Group, Facilitated |
| Evidence type | Self-report, peer feedback, performance artifact, facilitator observation |
| Risk profile | Low, Medium, Elevated |

### 2.1 Body

| Dimension | Values |
|---|---|
| Duration bands | Short (3–5 min): micro-resets, breath anchors; Medium (10–15 min): mobility flows, walking practice; Long (20–30 min): yoga, structured movement |
| Intensity levels | Low: seated breath or gentle stretch; Moderate: sustained movement or bodyweight sequence; High: vigorous aerobic or advanced postural work |
| Contraindications | Recent injury or surgery; chronic pain without practitioner guidance; cardiovascular contraindications for high-intensity formats |
| Setup requirements | Quiet space with room to move; comfortable clothing; no equipment required for Low/Medium; mat recommended for Long |

### 2.1b Somatic

**Definition:** Practices that develop interoceptive awareness, nervous system regulation, and embodied presence — distinct from Body practices (physical conditioning) and Mind practices (cognitive reflection). The somatic line is the infrastructure all other developmental lines rest on.

| Dimension | Values |
|---|---|
| Duration bands | Short (2–5 min): grounding, single breath exercise; Medium (10–15 min): body scan, progressive relaxation; Long (20–30 min): full somatic practice session with integration time |
| Intensity levels | Low: seated check-in (name one sensation); Moderate: guided body scan or movement-based regulation; High: trauma-adjacent material, partner co-regulation, or extended interoceptive tracking |
| Contraindications | Active trauma history without practitioner support; dissociation or depersonalisation patterns; body-image disorders; recent somatic practice-related destabilisation. Label these practices as facilitator-recommended minimum (Tier 2). |
| Setup requirements | Quiet space with option to sit or lie down; comfortable, non-restrictive clothing; facilitator present for Tier 2 practices; grounding resources immediately accessible |
| Stage/Tier requirements | **Tier 1 (solo-safe):** grounding, breath awareness (without prescribed ratios — see SAFE-03), naming one body sensation. **Tier 2 (facilitator-recommended minimum):** body scan, progressive relaxation, co-regulation partner exercise. **Tier 3 (facilitator-required):** trauma-adjacent somatic work, extended interoceptive tracking with history of dissociation. |
| Practice catalogue | Grounding (feet on floor, name 5 things visible, slow breath); Breath awareness (notice natural breath rhythm, no prescribed counts); Body scan (guided, region-by-region — see SOM-01 Practice section); Movement-based regulation (gentle shaking, stretching, walking meditation); Co-regulation practice (partner breathing, shared grounding — facilitator present for initial sessions) |
| Cross-references | [Somatic Line Overview](../modules/somatic-line-overview) (SOM-01); [Shadowwork Safety Standard](/internal/safety/shadowwork-safety-standard); SAFE-03 (no prescribed breath-count ratios) |

### 2.2 Mind

| Dimension | Values |
|---|---|
| Duration bands | Short (3–5 min): single-focus drill, bias prompt; Medium (10–15 min): metacognitive journal, systems mapping; Long (20–30 min): in-depth reflection or argument analysis |
| Intensity levels | Low: reading or single-prompt response; Moderate: multi-step analysis or debate review; High: extended cognitive reframing or belief examination |
| Contraindications | Acute cognitive overload or burnout states; neurological conditions affecting sustained attention (modify pacing, do not exclude) |
| Setup requirements | Quiet environment; writing surface or digital note space; distraction-free window |

### 2.3 Spirit/Meaning

| Dimension | Values |
|---|---|
| Duration bands | Short (3–5 min): gratitude noting, values word; Medium (10–15 min): contemplative sit, loving-kindness; Long (20–30 min): extended sit, nature immersion, meaning mapping |
| Intensity levels | Low: informal gratitude or appreciation; Moderate: structured contemplative sit with breath focus; High: open-awareness sit, inquiry practices, extended silence |
| Contraindications | Acute anxiety or panic history (modify high-intensity entry; begin with eyes-open or movement-based forms); dissociative tendencies (require facilitator guidance for High); recent significant loss (offer grief-adapted alternatives) |
| Setup requirements | Comfortable seated position; optional timer or guidance audio; limited external distraction; written reflection prompt available |

### 2.4 Shadow

| Dimension | Values |
|---|---|
| Duration bands | Short (3–5 min): trigger noting, reactive emotion log; Medium (10–15 min): trigger pattern journaling, projection check; Long (20–30 min): structured parts dialogue, role reversal |
| Intensity levels | Low: noticing and naming a reactive emotion without exploration; Moderate: structured journaling to identify projection or assumption; High: active parts dialogue, role-reversal, or inner critic confrontation |
| Contraindications | Active trauma response or crisis state; unmanaged PTSD symptoms; no active support system; inability to self-regulate during reflective activities |
| Setup requirements | Private, safe physical environment; written prompt sheet; grounding script on hand; facilitator access for Moderate/High; explicit consent completed before practice |

### 2.5 Ethics

| Dimension | Values |
|---|---|
| Duration bands | Short (3–5 min): single-value check-in; Medium (10–15 min): dilemma analysis; Long (20–30 min): commitment contract, stakeholder impact mapping |
| Intensity levels | Low: personal values recall; Moderate: structured ethical dilemma analysis; High: live commitment declaration or accountability partnership |
| Contraindications | Contexts with active interpersonal conflicts that require mediation beyond peer scope; high-stakes legal or employment situations (defer to qualified guidance) |
| Setup requirements | Scenario prompt or real-life dilemma description; writing space; optional partner for accountability formats |

### 2.6 Relationships

| Dimension | Values |
|---|---|
| Duration bands | Short (3–5 min): appreciation or acknowledgment exchange; Medium (10–15 min): structured dialogue or feedback session; Long (20–30 min): interpersonal repair protocol, perspective-taking exercise |
| Intensity levels | Low: one-way appreciation or written reflection on a relationship; Moderate: structured dialogue with a partner using an agreed protocol; High: conflict repair or difficult conversation with facilitator framing |
| Contraindications | Active abusive or unsafe relationships (refer to qualified support, do not use platform practices as substitute); power imbalances that undermine voluntary participation |
| Setup requirements | Willing partner or peer; agreed dialogue ground rules; optional facilitator presence for High-intensity formats; private or semi-private environment |

### 2.7 Service

| Dimension | Values |
|---|---|
| Duration bands | Short (3–5 min): micro-action planning (identify one contribution); Medium (10–15 min): mentorship exchange or community contribution reflection; Long (20–30 min): community project session or service learning debrief |
| Intensity levels | Low: planning a small contribution; Moderate: completing a defined service task and reflecting; High: sustained community role or public commitment |
| Contraindications | Overextension or burnout risk—learners in high-demand life circumstances should start at Low; avoid high-stakes public commitments without adequate preparation |
| Setup requirements | Identified contribution context; reflection prompt; optional partner or community group; Low intensity requires no external resources |

### 2.8 Emotional

**Definition:** Practices that develop affective differentiation, emotional vocabulary, regulation capacity, and the ability to use emotional data as information rather than interference. The Emotional line is distinct from the Somatic line (body-based awareness) and the Relationships family (interpersonal skill) — it targets the interior capacity to name, understand, and modulate emotional experience.

| Dimension | Values |
|---|---|
| Duration bands | Short (3–5 min): emotion wheel check-in, single reappraisal; Medium (10–15 min): structured journalling (ABC chain), body scan for emotional data; Long (20–30 min): extended journalling, multi-session emotion pattern tracking |
| Intensity levels | Low: naming emotions from a wheel without exploration; Moderate: structured journalling with cause-and-effect analysis or body-based tracking; High: real-time affect labelling during emotionally charged conversation or extended somatic tracking |
| Contraindications | Active trauma history without practitioner support (somatic emotion tracking rises to Tier 2); dissociation or depersonalisation patterns (avoid body-based tracking without facilitator); recent emotional destabilisation during reflective practice; Alexithymia spectrum — start with emotion wheel only, do not push body-based formats |
| Setup requirements | Quiet, private environment for journalling practices; emotion wheel or vocabulary list accessible; writing surface or digital note space; facilitator present for Tier 2 somatic tracking; grounding resources immediately accessible |
| Stage/Tier requirements | **Tier 1 (solo-safe):** emotion wheel journalling, ABC chain analysis, reappraisal micro-practice, affect labelling in low-stakes conversation. **Tier 2 (facilitator-recommended):** somatic emotion tracking (body scan for emotional data) — per Emotional & Interpersonal Safety Addendum, body-focused attention can activate body-held trauma. |
| Practice catalogue | Emotion wheel journalling (daily, 5–10 min); ABC chain analysis (Activating event → Belief → Consequence, 10–15 min); Reappraisal micro-practice (real-time cognitive reframe, 3–5 min); Somatic emotion tracking (body scan for emotional location/quality, 10–15 min, Tier 2); Affect labelling in conversation (naming affect during live dialogue, low intensity) |
| Cross-references | [Emotional Line Stage Map](./emotional-line-stage-map); [Emotional Line Overview & Orientation](../modules/emotional-line-overview-orientation) (EI-01); [Emotional Granularity](../modules/emotional-granularity) (EI-02); [Emotional Appraisal & Meaning-Making](../modules/emotional-appraisal-meaning-making) (EI-05); [Emotional Intelligence & the Somatic Line](../modules/emotional-intelligence-somatic-line) (EI-06); [Emotional & Interpersonal Safety Addendum](/internal/safety/emotional-interpersonal-safety-addendum) |

### 2.9 Interpersonal

**Definition:** Practices that develop relational skill — perspective-taking accuracy, attuned listening, conflict navigation, repair capacity, and the ability to maintain contact across meaningful difference. The Interpersonal line is distinct from the Relationships family, which focuses on empathy and conflict skill as general competencies; Interpersonal practices target the developmental line specifically, with stage-appropriate progression and Kegan-informed structural awareness.

| Dimension | Values |
|---|---|
| Duration bands | Short (3–5 min): perspective check (verify one assumption about another's experience); Medium (10–15 min): structured listening exercise, Four Horsemen self-audit; Long (20–30 min): cross-difference dialogue, full repair protocol drafting |
| Intensity levels | Low: solo reflective audit or perspective check; Moderate: structured partner listening or written repair drafting; High: live cross-difference dialogue or facilitated repair conversation |
| Contraindications | Active abusive or unsafe relationships (refer to qualified support; platform practices are not a substitute); power imbalances that undermine voluntary participation (e.g., supervisor-subordinate dyads in workplace settings); active relational trauma without support system; recent interpersonal crisis without stabilisation period |
| Setup requirements | Willing partner or peer for dialogue practices; private or semi-private environment; agreed ground rules for partner exercises; facilitator present for Tier 2 cross-difference dialogue; solo audit practices require only reflection prompt and writing space |
| Stage/Tier requirements | **Tier 1 (solo-safe):** perspective accuracy verification, Four Horsemen self-audit, NVC repair attempt drafting (written only). **Tier 2 (facilitator-recommended):** PACE attunement listening (partner exercise — attachment-related distress risk), cross-difference dialogue (identity threat/minority stress risk) — per Emotional & Interpersonal Safety Addendum. |
| Practice catalogue | Perspective accuracy verification (check assumption against partner's actual experience, 3–5 min); PACE attunement listening (Playful-Accepting-Curious-Empathic, 10–15 min, Tier 2); Four Horsemen self-audit (criticism/contempt/defensiveness/stonewalling review, 10–15 min); NVC repair attempt drafting (Observation-Feeling-Need-Request format, 15–20 min); Cross-difference dialogue (structured dialogue across identity difference, 20–30 min, Tier 2) |
| Cross-references | [Interpersonal Line Stage Map](./interpersonal-line-stage-map); [Interpersonal Line Overview & Orientation](../modules/interpersonal-line-overview-orientation) (EI-08); [Perspective-Taking & Empathic Accuracy](../modules/perspective-taking-empathic-accuracy) (EI-09); [Relational Repair & Conflict Navigation](../modules/relational-repair-conflict-navigation) (EI-10); [Cross-Difference Dialogue & Trust-Building](../modules/cross-difference-dialogue-trust-building) (EI-11); [Emotional & Interpersonal Safety Addendum](/internal/safety/emotional-interpersonal-safety-addendum) |

## 3. Progression Ladders and Fallback Alternatives

Each ladder covers **Levels 1–4** (Beginner → Advanced). Criteria for advancing between levels are explicit. Fallback alternatives are provided for learners with constraints.

### 3.1 Body

| Level | Name | Criteria to Enter | Example Practices | Developmental Line Focus |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Foundation | No prior somatic practice needed | 3-min seated breath reset, gentle neck rolls | Somatic awareness |
| 2 | Consistent Mover | Completed Level 1 practices for 5+ sessions | 10-min walking meditation, basic mobility flow | Somatic + Emotional regulation |
| 3 | Embodied Practitioner | Consistent 10-min practice for 3+ weeks | 20-min yoga or bodyweight sequence, breath-body linking | Somatic + Cognitive integration |
| 4 | Integrated Practice | Spontaneous somatic self-regulation reported | 30-min vigorous movement, advanced postural or aerobic work | Somatic + Interpersonal presence |

**Criteria for advancing:** Demonstrate the target practice independently for 5+ sessions before progressing. Self-reported skill and reflection artifact required.

**Fallback alternatives:** Learners with mobility limitations use seated or reclined breath-based practices at any level. Reduce duration before intensity.

---

### 3.1b Somatic

| Level | Name | Criteria to Enter | Example Practices | Developmental Line Focus |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Body-Aware | No prior practice needed | Grounding (feet on floor, slow breath, name 5 things visible); name one body sensation | Somatic awareness |
| 2 | Self-Regulating | Completed Level 1 for 5+ sessions; can identify regulation state (regulated/hyper/hypo) | Guided body scan (10 min); breath awareness without prescribed ratios; gentle movement-based regulation | Somatic + Emotional regulation |
| 3 | Somatically Integrated | Consistent body scan practice for 3+ weeks; uses body signals during decisions | Progressive relaxation; interoceptive tracking during conversation; co-regulation with partner | Somatic + Cognitive + Interpersonal |
| 4 | Embodied Facilitator | Spontaneous somatic self-regulation; teaches or designs body-informed protocols | Leading group grounding; designing body check-in protocols; evaluating participant somatic change | Somatic + Facilitation + Systemic design |

**Criteria for advancing:** Demonstrate target practice independently for 5+ sessions at Current Level. Facilitator present for Level 3+ partner practices.

**Fallback alternatives:** Learners with trauma history use grounding only at Level 1; progress to body scan only with facilitator present. No prescribed breath-count ratios at any level (per SAFE-03). Reduce duration before intensity; stop if dissociative response occurs.

---

### 3.2 Mind

| Level | Name | Criteria to Enter | Example Practices | Developmental Line Focus |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Awareness Starter | No prior metacognitive training needed | Single-prompt thought log, one cognitive bias named | Cognitive awareness |
| 2 | Pattern Recognizer | Completed 3+ Level 1 reflections | Weekly bias journal, simple systems map sketch | Cognitive + Metacognitive |
| 3 | Critical Analyst | Demonstrated consistent journaling with evidence | Structured argument analysis, multi-perspective mapping | Cognitive + Moral reasoning |
| 4 | Integrative Thinker | Applies multiple frameworks spontaneously | Extended belief examination, cross-domain reasoning task | Cognitive + Interpersonal |

**Criteria for advancing:** Submit at least one reflection artifact showing the new skill before progressing. Facilitate peer review at Levels 3–4.

**Fallback alternatives:** For learners with cognitive fatigue, allow audio-prompt responses instead of written reflection. Reduce prompt complexity rather than skipping the practice.

---

### 3.3 Spirit/Meaning

| Level | Name | Criteria to Enter | Example Practices | Developmental Line Focus |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Meaning Noticer | No prior contemplative experience needed | 3-min gratitude noting, values word selection | Spiritual/Meaning |
| 2 | Contemplative Beginner | Completed 3+ Level 1 practices | 10-min guided breath sit, loving-kindness reflection | Spiritual + Emotional |
| 3 | Regular Practitioner | Consistent 10-min sits for 2+ weeks | 20-min unguided sit, values-in-action mapping | Spiritual + Cognitive |
| 4 | Inquiry Practitioner | Self-sustained sitting practice | Extended open-awareness sit, self-inquiry, nature immersion | Spiritual + Interpersonal presence |

**Criteria for advancing:** Reflection journal entry demonstrating quality of attention before progressing. For Level 4, facilitator check-in recommended.

**Fallback alternatives:** For learners with anxiety or high cognitive arousal, use walking or movement-based contemplative forms (eyes open, gentle pace) instead of seated silence. Eyes-open sitting (soft gaze, upright posture, no closed-eye instruction) is also an appropriate lower-intensity alternative at any level. Loving-kindness reflection (silently directing goodwill phrases toward self and others) is lower intensity than open-awareness sits and suitable for most Level 2 learners.

### 3.4 Shadow

> **[HUMAN-REQUIRED]** Safety boundaries for this ladder must be reviewed and approved by a qualified SME before use in live cohorts. See [Shadowwork Safety Standard](/internal/safety/shadowwork-safety-standard).

| Level | Name | Criteria to Enter | Example Practices | Developmental Line Focus |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Aware Observer | Completed baseline regulation practice; explicit consent given | Reactive emotion log (name + situation only), trigger frequency noting | Emotional + Cognitive |
| 2 | Pattern Investigator | Consistent Level 1 practice for 1+ week; self-regulation skill demonstrated | Projection check journal (who irritates me and what it might reflect), assumption mapping | Emotional + Interpersonal |
| 3 | Parts Explorer | Demonstrated stable self-observation at Level 2; distress check passed | Structured parts dialogue (written), role-reversal thought experiment | Emotional + Moral |
| 4 | Integration Practitioner | Facilitator approval; sustained stability across Level 3 practices | Active inner-critic negotiation, interpersonal repair using shadow insight | Emotional + Interpersonal + Moral |

**Criteria for advancing:** Distress check-in required before each level transition. Level 3–4 transitions require facilitator review. Stop rules apply at all levels.

**Fallback alternatives:**
- If Level 2 produces destabilization: return to Level 1 trigger noting only.
- If Level 3 is inaccessible: substitute with cognitive reframing (Mind family) until regulation is stable.
- Always offer sensory grounding as an exit from any shadow practice.

**Stop rules:** Stop immediately if escalating panic, dissociation, inability to orient, or risk expression to self or others is present. Refer to [Shadowwork Safety Standard](/internal/safety/shadowwork-safety-standard).

### 3.5 Ethics

| Level | Name | Criteria to Enter | Example Practices | Developmental Line Focus |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Values Identifier | No prerequisites | Personal values list, single-value check-in | Moral awareness |
| 2 | Dilemma Analyst | Completed 2+ Level 1 practices | Structured ethical dilemma analysis, stakeholder identification | Moral + Cognitive |
| 3 | Commitment Practitioner | Demonstrated dilemma analysis with documented reasoning | Commitment contract with accountability partner, values-action gap audit | Moral + Interpersonal |
| 4 | Ethical Leader | Active accountability partnership for 4+ weeks | Community dilemma facilitation, ethical policy analysis | Moral + Interpersonal + Systems |

**Criteria for advancing:** Completed written artifact demonstrating the skill before progressing. Level 3–4 requires peer or facilitator acknowledgment.

**Fallback alternatives:** Learners uncomfortable with partner formats use solo written commitment at Level 3. Scale community scope down at Level 4 for those with limited community access.

### 3.6 Relationships

| Level | Name | Criteria to Enter | Example Practices | Developmental Line Focus |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Appreciation Practitioner | No prerequisites | Written appreciation or gratitude to another person, acknowledgment note | Interpersonal + Emotional |
| 2 | Active Listener | Completed 2+ Level 1 practices | Structured listening exercise, reflective response practice | Interpersonal + Cognitive |
| 3 | Dialogue Partner | Demonstrated listening skills across 2+ sessions | Full structured dialogue protocol with a peer, perspective-taking exercise | Interpersonal + Moral |
| 4 | Repair and Renewal | Stable Level 3 skill; willing partner present | Difficult conversation protocol, interpersonal repair sequence | Interpersonal + Moral + Emotional |

**Criteria for advancing:** Partner confirmation of experience quality required at Levels 3–4. Facilitator framing available but not required at Level 3; recommended at Level 4.

**Fallback alternatives:** For learners without an available partner, use written perspective-taking exercises or recorded dialogue review at Levels 2–3. Reduce scope of repair at Level 4 rather than skipping entirely.

### 3.7 Service

| Level | Name | Criteria to Enter | Example Practices | Developmental Line Focus |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Contribution Planner | No prerequisites | Identify one micro-contribution; write what, where, when | Moral + Systems awareness |
| 2 | Active Contributor | Completed at least one Level 1 contribution | Complete a defined service task; reflect on impact | Moral + Interpersonal |
| 3 | Consistent Servant | Sustained contributions for 3+ weeks | Mentorship exchange, ongoing community role | Moral + Interpersonal + Cognitive |
| 4 | Systems Change Agent | Demonstrated sustained service commitment | Community project leadership, public advocacy action | Moral + Systems + Interpersonal |

**Criteria for advancing:** Reflection artifact documenting the contribution and learning before progressing.

**Fallback alternatives:** For learners with time or capacity constraints, reduce contribution scope at all levels rather than skipping. Solo reflection on contribution opportunities is a valid Level 1 starting point.

### 3.8 Emotional

| Level | Name | Criteria to Enter | Example Practices | Developmental Line Focus |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Emotion-Aware | No prior emotional literacy training needed | Emotion wheel journalling (daily, 5–10 min): name 2–3 emotions from wheel, note context | Emotional awareness |
| 2 | Emotionally Literate | Completed Level 1 journalling for 5+ sessions | ABC chain analysis (10–15 min): Activating event → Belief → Consequence; Reappraisal micro-practice (3–5 min): real-time cognitive reframe | Emotional + Cognitive |
| 3 | Self-Regulating | Consistent Level 2 practice for 3+ weeks; can identify regulation state | Somatic emotion tracking (10–15 min, Tier 2 — facilitator recommended); Affect labelling in conversation | Emotional + Somatic + Interpersonal |
| 4 | Emotionally Facilitative | Spontaneous emotional regulation across contexts; demonstrated ability to hold emotional complexity | Designing emotion-informed practices for self or groups; co-regulation facilitation; teaching emotion vocabulary to peers | Emotional + Facilitation + Systemic design |

**Criteria for advancing:** Demonstrate target practice independently for 5+ sessions at current level. Level 3 requires facilitator presence for somatic tracking. Level 4 requires documented facilitation experience or peer feedback on emotional leadership.

**Fallback alternatives:** For learners with trauma history, use emotion wheel journalling only at Level 1; progress to body-based tracking (Level 3) only with facilitator present. If somatic tracking produces destabilisation, substitute with cognitive reappraisal (Level 2) until regulation is stable. Always offer grounding as an exit from body-based emotional practices.

### 3.9 Interpersonal

| Level | Name | Criteria to Enter | Example Practices | Developmental Line Focus |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Relational Observer | No prerequisites | Perspective accuracy verification: check one assumption about another's experience (3–5 min) | Interpersonal + Cognitive |
| 2 | Skill Builder | Completed 5+ perspective checks; demonstrated basic self-observation | Four Horsemen self-audit (10–15 min): review recent conflict for criticism, contempt, defensiveness, stonewalling | Interpersonal + Emotional + Cognitive |
| 3 | Dialogue Practitioner | Consistent Level 2 practice for 3+ weeks; willing partner available (or facilitator-facilitated) | PACE attunement listening (10–15 min, Tier 2); NVC repair attempt drafting (15–20 min); Cross-difference dialogue (20–30 min, Tier 2) | Interpersonal + Moral + Emotional |
| 4 | Relational Facilitator | Sustained Level 3 practice across multiple partners; facilitator approval | Facilitating cross-difference dialogue; designing relational practice sequences for groups; teaching repair protocols | Interpersonal + Facilitation + Systems |

**Criteria for advancing:** Partner confirmation of experience quality required at Level 3 (for dialogue practices) and Level 4 (for facilitation). Facilitator framing recommended at Level 3; required at Level 4. Solo written alternatives accepted when no partner is available at Levels 2–3.

**Fallback alternatives:** For learners without an available partner, use solo written perspective-taking exercises and recorded dialogue review at Levels 2–3. For cross-difference dialogue without a facilitator, substitute with written reflection on a past cross-difference interaction until facilitator access is available. Reduce scope of repair drafting (Level 3) rather than skipping: write the NVC draft without delivery if relational safety is uncertain.

## 4. Safety Boundaries for Shadow and Contemplative Practices

> **[HUMAN-REQUIRED]** This section must be reviewed and approved by a qualified SME (mental health or trauma-informed practice professional) before publication or cohort use. Copilot output is a draft for human validation only.

### 4.1 Shadow Practice Safety Boundaries

| Boundary | Specification |
|---|---|
| Consent | Explicit informed consent required before any shadow practice begins. Consent must be renewed when advancing levels. |
| Tier classification | Level 1 = Tier 1 (self-guided allowed). Level 2 = Tier 1 with safety prompts. Level 3 = Tier 2 (facilitator-supervised recommended). Level 4 = Tier 3 (facilitator-led only). |
| Entry check | Distress check-in required at session start. Learner must report current emotional state before proceeding. |
| Stop rules | Escalating panic, dissociation, inability to orient, or any expression of risk to self or others requires immediate stop. Shift to grounding protocol. |
| Grounding menu | Breath anchor (4-count inhale, 6-count exhale), sensory orientation (5 senses named), physical movement reset (shake or walk). |
| Escalation path | Level 1 incidents: document and monitor. Level 2–3 incidents: notify facilitator and require follow-up check. Any crisis expression: immediate handoff to qualified human support. |
| Contraindications | Active trauma response or crisis state; unmanaged PTSD; no active support system; history of repeated destabilization during reflective practice without support plan. |

### 4.2 Contemplative (Spirit/Meaning) Practice Safety Boundaries

| Boundary | Specification |
|---|---|
| Anxiety and panic | Learners with history of panic attacks begin with movement-based or eyes-open contemplative forms only. Closed-eye sitting at Level 3+ only with facilitator awareness. |
| Dissociation | Learners with dissociative tendencies require facilitator guidance before attempting High-intensity contemplative formats. Open-awareness sits are contraindicated without qualified oversight. |
| Grief and loss | Offer grief-adapted alternatives (e.g., self-compassion reflection instead of extended silence) when learner discloses recent significant loss. |
| Spiritual emergence | If a learner reports unusual or destabilizing perceptual experiences during contemplative practice, pause immediately and refer to facilitator or qualified support. Do not encourage continuation without assessment. |
| Duration limits | No self-guided session at Level 1–2 should exceed 15 minutes. Extended practices at Level 3–4 require explicit readiness check. |
| Stop rules | Inability to return to ordinary awareness within 3 minutes of practice end; panic response; overwhelming emotional flooding. |

## 6. Practice Card Template

Each practice implementation must define the following fields:

| Field | Description |
|---|---|
| Practice ID | Unique identifier (e.g., ILP-BODY-001) |
| Family | One of: Body, Somatic, Mind, Spirit/Meaning, Shadow, Ethics, Relationships, Service, Emotional, Interpersonal |
| AQAL tags | Quadrant(s), Level, Line(s), State, Type notes |
| Duration | Short / Medium / Long / Extended |
| Intensity | Low / Moderate / High |
| Tier | 1 / 2 / 3 (safety tier; applies to Shadow and Spirit/Meaning practices per [Shadowwork Safety Standard](/internal/safety/shadowwork-safety-standard); mark N/A for Body, Mind, Ethics, Relationships, and Service practices unless emotionally elevated) |
| Instructions | Step-by-step learner-facing directions |
| Reflection prompt | Open-ended prompt for post-practice journaling |
| Evidence capture | Method for documenting completion |
| Safety notes | Contraindications and stop rules specific to this practice |
| Fallback practice | Lower-intensity or lower-duration alternative |
| Escalation trigger | Condition requiring facilitator or support contact |

#### ILP-EMOT-001: Emotion Wheel Journalling

| Field | Description |
|---|---|
| Practice ID | ILP-EMOT-001 |
| Family | Emotional |
| AQAL tags | Quadrant: I (Upper-Left); Level: L1–L2; Lines: Emotional (primary), Cognitive (secondary); States: Reflective waking |
| Duration | Short (5–10 min) |
| Intensity | Low |
| Tier | 1 (solo-safe) |
| Instructions | 1. Open an emotion wheel (or list). 2. Scan recent experience (last 24 h or current moment). 3. Name 2–3 emotions from the wheel that match your experience. 4. For each emotion, note: what situation or thought preceded it? 5. Write without analysing or judging. 6. Repeat daily for at least 5 sessions. |

| Fallback practice | Use a simplified emotion list (glad, sad, afraid, angry) instead of a full emotion wheel. Reduce to 1–2 emotions. |
| Escalation trigger | Escalating distress that does not subside with grounding — pause practice and notify facilitator if in a supported program. |

#### ILP-EMOT-002: ABC Chain Analysis

| Field | Description |
|---|---|
| Practice ID | ILP-EMOT-002 |
| Family | Emotional |
| AQAL tags | Quadrant: I (Upper-Left); Level: L2–L3; Lines: Emotional (primary), Cognitive (secondary); States: Reflective waking |
| Duration | Medium (10–15 min) |
| Intensity | Low–Moderate |
| Tier | 1 (solo-safe) |
| Instructions | 1. Recall a recent emotional activation (not a crisis-level event). 2. **A — Activating event:** Write what happened — facts only, no interpretation. 3. **B — Belief:** Write the thought or belief that linked the event to the feeling. 4. **C — Consequence:** Write the emotional response (name the emotion) and what you did or wanted to do. 5. Review the chain: Is the belief accurate? Is there another way to interpret the event? 6. Note any pattern across multiple chains. |
| Reflection prompt | Which belief in the chain surprised you? If you could revise one belief, what would it be — and how would that change the consequence? |
| Evidence capture | Written ABC chain with date. Self-report. |
| Safety notes | Avoid selecting activating events involving recent trauma, abuse, or current crisis. This is a cognitive-reflective practice, not an exposure exercise. |
| Fallback practice | Write only A and C (skip belief analysis) if cognitive load is high. Use a neutral or mildly positive event for first attempts. |
| Escalation trigger | If reviewing the activating event produces re-experiencing or inability to return to present within 5 min — stop, ground, and notify facilitator. |
| Evidence reference | Emotional Appraisal & Meaning-Making (EI-05): Gross (2015) process model of emotion regulation; cognitive reappraisal efficacy (Webb, Miles & Sheeran, 2012). |

#### ILP-EMOT-003: Reappraisal Micro-Practice

| Field | Description |
|---|---|
| Practice ID | ILP-EMOT-003 |
| Family | Emotional |
| AQAL tags | Quadrant: I (Upper-Left); Level: L2–L3; Lines: Emotional (primary), Cognitive (secondary); States: Reflective waking / in-vivo |
| Duration | Short (3–5 min) |
| Intensity | Low |
| Tier | 1 (solo-safe) |
| Instructions | 1. Notice an emotional shift during your day (frustration, disappointment, anxiety). 2. Pause for 3–5 s. 3. Ask silently: "Is there another way to see this?" 4. Generate one alternate interpretation — not "positive thinking" but a plausible different framing. 5. Check: did the emotional intensity change? 6. | Fallback practice | Write reappraisals after the fact rather than in real time. Use only mildly activating situations. |
| Escalation trigger | If reappraisal produces dissociation or emotional numbing rather than regulation — stop, shift to grounding, and discuss with facilitator. |

#### ILP-EMOT-004: Somatic Emotion Tracking

| Field | Description |
|---|---|
| Practice ID | ILP-EMOT-004 |
| Family | Emotional |
| AQAL tags | Quadrant: I (Upper-Left) + It (Upper-Right); Level: L2–L3; Lines: Emotional (primary), Somatic (adjacent); States: Reflective waking with body awareness |
| Duration | Medium (10–15 min) |
| Intensity | Moderate |
| Tier | 2 (facilitator-recommended) |
| Instructions | 1. Sit comfortably in a quiet space. 2. Bring to mind a recent mild emotional experience — not a traumatic or intensely charged memory. 3. Scan your body slowly from head to feet. 4. Where does the emotion live? (chest tightness, throat constriction, stomach fluttering, shoulder tension). 5. Name the quality of the sensation: pressure, warmth, tingling, heaviness, expansion. 6. Stay with the sensation without trying to change it. 7. Note: does the sensation shift, dissolve, or stay stable? 8. End with grounding: feet on floor, three slow breaths. |
| Reflection prompt | What did you learn from your body that your thinking mind had not already told you? Was there a gap between the emotion you named and the sensation you found? |
| Evidence capture | Journal entry with emotion named, body location, sensation quality, and any shift observed. Self-report; facilitator observation for Tier 2 validation. |
| Safety notes | **Tier 2 — Facilitator recommended.** Body-focused attention can activate unprocessed trauma. Contraindicated for: active trauma history without practitioner support; dissociation or depersonalisation patterns; recent somatic practice-related destabilisation. If at any point the body sensation becomes overwhelming, STOP immediately and use grounding protocol. Do not push through intensifying sensations. |
| Fallback practice | Use emotion wheel journalling (ILP-EMOT-001) instead. If body scanning produces mild tension without distress, reduce scan to hands and feet only. |
| Escalation trigger | Rapid breathing or breath-holding; muscle rigidity; dissociative gaze; flashback or intrusive imagery; inability to re-orient to present within 5–10 min — stop, ground, notify facilitator. Per Emotional & Interpersonal Safety Addendum, refer to licensed trauma-informed clinician if pattern persists across sessions. |
| Evidence reference | Emotional Intelligence & the Somatic Line (EI-06): Porges (2011) polyvagal theory; interoceptive awareness and emotional regulation (Füstös et al., 2013); Emotional & Interpersonal Safety Addendum, somatic body scan trigger. |

#### ILP-EMOT-005: Affect Labelling in Conversation

| Field | Description |
|---|---|
| Practice ID | ILP-EMOT-005 |

#### ILP-INT-001: Perspective Accuracy Verification

| Field | Description |
|---|---|
| Practice ID | ILP-INT-001 |
| Family | Interpersonal |
| AQAL tags | Quadrant: We (Lower-Left) + I (Upper-Left); Level: L2; Lines: Interpersonal (primary), Cognitive (secondary); States: Reflective waking / in-vivo |
| Duration | Live practice (3–5 min check) |

| Tier | 1 (solo-safe) |
| Instructions | 1. After a conversation with someone you know reasonably well, identify one assumption you hold about their experience. 2. #### ILP-INT-002: PACE Attunement Listening

| Field | Description |
|---|---|
| Practice ID | ILP-INT-002 |
| Family | Interpersonal |
| AQAL tags | Quadrant: We (Lower-Left); Level: L3; Lines: Interpersonal (primary), Emotional (adjacent); States: Reflective waking, partner dialogue |
| Duration | Medium (10–15 min) |
| Intensity | Moderate |
| Tier | 2 (facilitator-recommended) |
| Instructions | 1. Work with a partner. One person speaks about a moderate-concern topic for 5 min. 2. #### ILP-INT-003: Four Horsemen Self-Audit

| Field | Description |
|---|---|
| Practice ID | ILP-INT-003 |
| Family | Interpersonal |
| AQAL tags | Quadrant: We (Lower-Left) + I (Upper-Left); Level: L2–L3; Lines: Interpersonal (primary), Emotional (secondary); States: Reflective waking |
| Duration | Medium (10–15 min) |
| Intensity | Moderate |
| Tier | 1 (solo-safe) |
| Instructions | 1. Recall a recent conflict or difficult interaction. 2. Review your own behaviour against the **Four Horsemen** (Gottman): **Criticism** — did you attack the person's character rather than addressing a specific behaviour? **Contempt** — did you use sarcasm, mockery, eye-rolling, or hostile humour? **Defensiveness** — did you deflect responsibility, counter-attack, or play the victim? **Stonewalling** — did you withdraw, shut down, or give the silent treatment? 3. Rate each Horseman: Not present / Mild / Significant. 4. For any significant rating, write: what function was that behaviour serving? What was I protecting? 5. Write one alternative behaviour you could try next time. |
| Reflection prompt | Which Horseman showed up most — and what does that tell you about your conflict pattern? If you were your own coach, what would you recommend? |
| Evidence capture | Written self-audit with Horsemen ratings and alternative behaviour. Self-report. |
| Safety notes | This is a self-audit, not a partner confrontation. Do not share your audit with the conflict partner unless in a structured repair process with facilitator support. Do not use this practice to self-flagellate; the goal is mapping, not punishment. |
| Fallback practice | Audit a minor disagreement rather than a significant conflict. If all four Horsemen are significantly present, work with a facilitator before attempting live repair. |
| Escalation trigger | If audit triggers shame spiral, self-loathing, or urges to confront the other person impulsively — stop, ground, and notify facilitator. |
| Evidence reference | Relational Repair & Conflict Navigation (EI-10): Gottman (1999, 2015) Four Horsemen of the Apocalypse; marital conflict and repair research. |

#### ILP-INT-004: NVC Repair Attempt Drafting

| Field | Description |
|---|---|
| Practice ID | ILP-INT-004 |
| Family | Interpersonal |
| AQAL tags | Quadrant: We (Lower-Left) + I (Upper-Left); Level: L3; Lines: Interpersonal (primary), Emotional (secondary); States: Reflective waking |
| Duration | Medium (15–20 min) |
| Intensity | Moderate |
| Tier | 1 (solo-safe for drafting; Tier 2 if delivering live with facilitator) |
| Instructions | 1. Identify a specific interpersonal rupture or conflict you would like to repair. 2. Draft a repair attempt using Nonviolent Communication (NVC) format: **Observation** — what happened, stated without judgment ("When you arrived 20 min after we agreed..."). **Feeling** — your emotional response ("I felt hurt and unimportant."). **Need** — the underlying value or need that was unmet ("Because I need reliability and mutual respect in our friendship."). **Request** — a concrete, present-focused ask ("Would you be willing to text me if you're running late next time?"). 3. Read aloud to yourself. Does it land as a request, not a demand? 4. Revise until the draft feels clean — no blame, no hidden ultimatum. |
| Reflection prompt | Which NVC component was hardest to write without blame? What did you discover about your actual needs beneath the complaint? |
| Evidence capture | Written NVC draft. Self-report. |
| Safety notes | Drafting is Tier 1 solo-safe. If you choose to deliver the draft live to the other person, facilitator support is recommended when: the conflict involves a significant power imbalance, the relationship has a history of repair failure, or recent harm was significant. Delivery is not required — the drafting itself is the practice. |
| Fallback practice | Draft the NVC for a minor disagreement first. Write the draft purely for yourself without intention to deliver. |
| Escalation trigger | If drafting reactivates distress about the relationship to a degree that impairs functioning — pause and discuss with facilitator before deciding whether to deliver. |
| Evidence reference | Relational Repair & Conflict Navigation (EI-10): Rosenberg (2003, 2015) Nonviolent Communication framework; repair process in close relationships. |

#### ILP-INT-005: Cross-Difference Dialogue

| Field | Description |
|---|---|
| Practice ID | ILP-INT-005 |
| Family | Interpersonal |
| AQAL tags | Quadrant: We (Lower-Left); Level: L3–L4; Lines: Interpersonal (primary), Emotional (secondary), Moral (adjacent); States: Reflective waking, structured dialogue |
| Duration | Long (20–30 min) |
| Intensity | Moderate–High |
| Tier | 2 (facilitator-required) |
| Instructions | 1. Work in a small group (2–4 people) with a trained facilitator. 2. The facilitator frames a structured dialogue: "We are here to understand, not to agree. You do not need to change your view. You do need to stay curious about how the other person's view makes sense from within their life." 3. Each person shares their perspective on a pre-agreed topic involving identity or values difference. 4. Before responding, each listener reflects back what they heard: "What I hear you saying is..." 5. Check for accuracy: "Is that right? What did I miss?" 6. The facilitator monitors for power dynamics, emotional safety, and participation balance. 7. Debrief: What did you learn about how the other person's context shapes their view? What was hardest to hear without reacting? |
| Reflection prompt | Which perspective was hardest to stay curious about — and what inside you was activated? What, if anything, shifted in your understanding of the other person? |
| Evidence capture | Journal entry describing the experience, what was learned, and what was activated. Facilitator observation required for Tier 2 validation. |
| Safety notes | **Tier 2 — Facilitator required.** Cross-difference dialogue can activate identity threat and minority stress. Per Emotional & Interpersonal Safety Addendum: monitor for guardedness, disengagement, intellectualised detachment, or reports of feeling 'unsafe' or 'attacked.' Facilitator must have completed cultural competency or diversity-informed facilitation training. Do not require participants to 'work through' identity pain in a group setting. If a participant reports harm → document without defensiveness. Contraindicated: identity-related experiences that are too raw or recent; history of harm in cross-difference conversations without adequate support. |
| Fallback practice | Written reflection on a past cross-difference interaction (solo). Structured curiosity exercise: research another person's perspective and write a summary from their point of view without argument. |
| Escalation trigger | If a participant becomes guarded, disengages, or reports feeling harmed by the exercise → stop the structured dialogue. Offer individual debrief, break and return to simpler exercise, or end participation. If identity-related distress persists → refer to clinician with demonstrated cultural competence. |
| Evidence reference | Cross-Difference Dialogue & Trust-Building (EI-11): Sue et al. (2007) microaggressions; cultural competence and intergroup dialogue frameworks; Emotional & Interpersonal Safety Addendum, cross-difference dialogue trigger.
