## 🗺️ Interpersonal Line Stage Map (Amber through Integral)

## Overview

The **Interpersonal developmental line** tracks how human beings relate: how the self is organized in relationship, what generates trust, how conflict is interpreted, and how many perspectives can be held without losing coherence. Like all developmental lines in AQAL, the Interpersonal line develops semi-independently. A learner may show strong cognitive complexity while still using an earlier-stage trust model or conflict pattern under pressure.

This map follows the platform's adult-learning stage axis — **Amber/Mythic, Rational/Orange, Pluralistic/Green, and Integral/Teal** — and uses Kegan's subject–object framework to clarify the structural shift in the relational self at each altitude. These rows describe **modal patterns**, not fixed types. Real people often show mixed profiles across contexts, relationships, and stress conditions.

**How to use this map:**
- Locate the trust and conflict patterns that feel most familiar without treating any row as a total identity.
- Read the Kegan mapping in each row as a clue to *what the self can and cannot yet take as object* in relationship.
- Use the shadow table to identify the predictable relational distortion that tends to accompany each stage's genuine strengths.
- Return to this map after completing [EI-08 · Interpersonal Line Overview & Orientation](../modules/interpersonal-line-overview-orientation) and the stage-orientation modules listed below.

This is a **reference document**, not a diagnostic instrument. It is best used to support inquiry, module design, and facilitated reflection rather than to classify people.

## Stage × Interpersonal Line Matrix

The table below describes how the Interpersonal line characteristically manifests at the four principal developmental altitudes addressed by this platform. Each row includes the corresponding Kegan structural mapping so module authors can connect relational behaviour to subject–object development rather than to surface personality alone.

| Stage | Relational Self-Sense | Trust Model | Conflict Style | Perspective-Taking Capacity | Characteristic Shadow | Growth Edge |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Amber / Mythic**  \n*Kegan: Interpersonal / Institutional boundary* | The self is organized through role, loyalty, duty, and belonging. Relationship is experienced primarily through legitimate authority, family role, and in-group membership. To remain connected is to remain aligned with the shared moral order. | Trust is built through reliability inside established roles: keeping promises, honouring duty, respecting legitimate authority, and showing visible loyalty to the group. Shared belief and behavioural conformity are interpreted as safety. | Conflict is experienced as a threat to order and belonging. The default moves are avoidance, appeal to authority, moralisation, or pressure toward restored harmony without deep differentiation of perspectives. | Strong in-group empathy and role-based social reading, but limited capacity to hold conflicting viewpoints inside a shared frame. Perspective-taking usually tracks loyalty categories more than reciprocal subjectivity. | Loyalty can harden into conformity, suspicion of out-groups, and indirect aggression toward anyone who threatens the group's relational script. Harmony may be preserved externally while resentment, triangulation, or scapegoating grows underneath. | Differentiate personal experience from role expectation. Learn that trust can survive honest disagreement, and that another person's different perspective does not automatically signal betrayal or disorder. |
| **Rational / Orange**  \n*Kegan: Institutional* | The self experiences itself as an autonomous agent who *has* relationships rather than being constituted by them. Relationship becomes a domain for negotiated exchange, mutual benefit, and coordinated goals. | Trust is built through competence, consistency, honesty, and follow-through. People are trusted when they are capable, dependable, and clear about expectations and incentives. | Conflict is approached as a problem to solve, negotiate, or contain. Directness increases, but emotional and relational complexity may be flattened into efficiency, argument quality, or outcome management. | Stable third-person perspective-taking and the capacity to see one's own position as one perspective among others. Strong at strategic empathy and viewpoint coordination, weaker at surrendering control to mutual transformation. | Instrumental relating, emotional distance, and subtle commodification of relationship: people become collaborators, obstacles, or resources. The autonomous self may protect its system so strongly that vulnerability and mutual dependence are treated as inefficiencies. | Recognise that trust is not built by competence alone. Develop genuine receptivity to another's interiority, including needs and values that cannot be reduced to performance logic or negotiated exchange. |
| **Pluralistic / Green**  \n*Kegan: Institutional / Interindividual boundary* | The relational self becomes more porous, dialogical, and identity-sensitive. Mutual recognition, authenticity, and shared meaning matter as much as competence or role performance. | Trust is built through authenticity, emotional openness, listening, inclusion, and visible care. People feel trustworthy when they seem sincere, self-revealing, and willing to be changed by relationship. | Conflict is interpreted through rupture, power, identity, and impact. There is more willingness to stay with feeling and multiple truths, but also a risk of over-processing, consensus pressure, or equating emotional intensity with moral authority. | Strong multi-perspectival empathy and sensitivity to context, identity, and power. Can often hold many voices at once, but may struggle to preserve self-other differentiation when perspectives are highly charged. | Empathic flooding, over-accommodation, conflict aversion masked as care, and emotional merging. The wish to honour every perspective can erode boundaries, clarity, and the capacity to say a clean no. | Build stable differentiation inside closeness. Learn to hold care and boundary together, to stay relational without collapsing into consensus, and to let conflict clarify rather than dissolve the self. |
| **Integral / Teal**  \n*Kegan: Interindividual* | The self is relationally differentiated and can participate in mutual transformation without losing coherence. Relationship is understood as a living system in which self, other, culture, and developmental context are all active. | Trust is built through contextual integrity: competence, care, honesty, developmental humility, repair capacity, and accurate response to difference. Trust languages are read stage-relatively rather than assumed to be universal. | Conflict is approached as a complex field requiring perspective coordination, power awareness, emotional regulation, and repair. The practitioner can stay in contact across significant difference without collapsing into domination, rescue, or fusion. | Can hold first-, second-, and third-person perspectives in coordinated form, and can remain curious about how multiple meaning systems are shaping the interaction. Perspective-taking becomes genuinely mutual and self-transforming. | Developmental condescension, subtle management of others through frameworks, and using meta-perspective to stay one step removed from real vulnerability. The shadow is warm control: relating *about* the relationship more easily than simply being in it. | Surrender the need to manage the interaction from above it. Let mutuality become more important than correct mapping, and allow relationship to change the self as much as the self can skillfully hold the relationship. |

## Interpersonal Line Shadow at Each Stage

The following table isolates the characteristic shadow of the **Interpersonal line** at each stage and names its typical behavioural expression. Read it alongside the [Shadow & Developmental Lines Integration Map](./shadow-developmental-lines-map), where the Interpersonal row provides the cross-line companion frame.

| Stage | Core Interpersonal Shadow | Typical Behavioural Expression | Relationship Cost | Practice Entry Point |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Amber / Mythic** | Conformist loyalty shadow: belonging is protected through suppression of difference, projection onto outsiders, and reliance on authority to settle relational tension. | Triangulation, indirect criticism, moralised blame, outward harmony with hidden resentment, and rapid distrust of people who do not share the group's code. | Surface cohesion hides brittle trust. Conflict goes underground, out-group suspicion rises, and honest voice becomes unsafe. | Gentle perspective-taking work, structured dialogue with clear norms, and reflection on what is personally felt before the group script takes over. |
| **Rational / Orange** | Instrumental autonomy shadow: relationship is over-organized around goals, contracts, and self-sufficiency. | Transactional relating, premature problem-solving, low tolerance for dependency, conflict framed as a negotiation problem rather than a relational event. | Others may feel managed, unseen, or valued mainly for utility. Trust becomes narrow and brittle under stress. | Practise curiosity without immediate fixing, name vulnerability in concrete language, and treat emotional/relational data as relevant rather than distracting. |
| **Pluralistic / Green** | Merging-and-consensus shadow: care for connection overrides boundary, discernment, and asymmetry awareness. | Over-accommodation, blurred boundaries, guilt when saying no, prolonged processing loops, and treating emotional disclosure as sufficient repair. | Real differences remain unresolved, burnout increases, and groups confuse inclusion with the inability to make clean distinctions. | Boundary work paired with attunement, repair practices that include accountability, and self-other differentiation under emotional activation. |
| **Integral / Teal** | Meta-relational shadow: frameworks, stage-awareness, and systems language are used to subtly regulate the relationship from above it. | Soft condescension, over-contextualising others' reactions, translating live vulnerability into developmental analysis, and remaining the calm container instead of a mutual participant. | Others may feel understood but not fully met. The relationship becomes skillful yet asymmetrical, with intimacy limited by hidden self-protection. | Suspend interpretation during live contact, invite feedback on subtle power, and practise mutuality that is not organized around being the most developed person in the room. |
