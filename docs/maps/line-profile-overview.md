# Line Profile Overview

Most people assume development is like a rising tide — everything goes up together. It doesn't. Development happens unevenly across different capacities, like a landscape with peaks and valleys rather than a flat plain gaining altitude.

This page synthesizes all seven developmental lines in one place: what each line tracks, why they develop at different rates, and what uneven development actually looks like in a real person's daily life.

---

## Stages vs. Lines

Stage development tracks the **overall center of gravity** — the altitude at which your meaning-making tends to organize experience across domains. Line development tracks **specific capacities** that can advance or lag relative to each other and to your overall stage.

The AQAL Overview explains this distinction in detail. See the [Skills (Lines)](./aqal-overview#skills-lines) section for the framework, and the [Depth (Levels)](./aqal-overview#depth-levels) section for stage descriptions.

The key insight: you can be at one stage cognitively and quite another emotionally. You can think with postformal complexity while still defaulting to earlier patterns of emotional regulation under stress. This is not a flaw — it is the normal architecture of human development.

## The Seven Developmental Lines

| Line | What it tracks | Start here |
|------|---------------|------------|
| **Cognitive** | How we know and reason — the structural evolution of thinking from concrete operations through metasystematic cognition | [Cognitive Line Overview & Orientation](../modules/cognitive-line-overview-orientation) |
| **Emotional** | How we feel and regulate — the capacity to differentiate, name, and work with affective experience with increasing precision | [Emotional Line Overview & Orientation](../modules/emotional-line-overview-orientation) |
| **Interpersonal** | How we relate to others — the developmental arc of relational skill from transactional relating to genuine I–Thou contact | [Interpersonal Line Overview & Orientation](../modules/interpersonal-line-overview-orientation) |
| **Moral** | How we judge right and wrong — parallel development on justice reasoning and care-based ethical responsibility | [Moral Line Overview & Dual-Track Orientation](../modules/moral-line-overview-dual-track) |
| **Self (Ego)** | How we construct identity and meaning — the meaning-making structure through which all experience is organized, following Cook-Greuter's full nine-level arc | [Self Line Overview & Psychograph](../modules/self-line-overview-psychograph) |
| **Spiritual** | How we relate to ultimate meaning — the evolving structural relationship to the sacred, ultimate reality, and what matters most | [Spiritual Line Overview & Orientation](../modules/spiritual-line-overview-orientation) |
| **Somatic** | How we inhabit our bodies — interoceptive awareness, nervous system regulation, and embodied presence | [Somatic Line Overview & Orientation](../modules/somatic-line-overview) |

> **Note:** The [AQAL Overview](./aqal-overview) lists six common lines. The **Self (Ego) line** is the seventh — it serves as the architecturally central thread because all other lines are integrated through the meaning-making structure the Self line provides. Cook-Greuter describes the ego as the "master navigator" for this reason: it doesn't replace the other lines, but it coordinates them into a coherent whole.

## Uneven Development Is Normal

The psychograph — a visual profile of development across lines — is the most empirically accurate picture of how human development actually works. Few people develop evenly across all lines. Most people show a characteristic profile: some lines substantially ahead of their overall center of gravity, others behind it, and several clustered near the center.

This unevenness is not a failure of effort or character. It is a product of life experience: which capacities were demanded, supported, modeled, or penalized in your particular developmental environment. The cognitive line may develop rapidly in educational settings while the interpersonal line lags. The emotional line may stall in environments that don't provide vocabulary and regulation modeling. The somatic line may advance quickly in an athlete or performer while other lines remain average.

The point of the psychograph is not judgment — it is orientation. Knowing your profile lets you invest attention where it will make the most difference, rather than continuing to develop your strongest lines while neglecting capacities that keep you from the life you want.

## A Worked Example

The following is a fictional composite profile. It does not represent any real person. It exists to make uneven development concrete enough to recognize — in yourself and in others.

### Maria, 34

Maria is a data analyst at a mid-sized nonprofit. She is well-educated, professionally competent, and generally considered reliable and smart by colleagues. Her developmental profile, if it were assessed, might look something like this:

| Line | Approximate level | What it looks like in daily life |
|------|------------------|----------------------------------|
| **Cognitive** | Orange/Rational | Thinks clearly, evaluates evidence systematically, trusts her own analysis. Can handle complexity, make sound decisions, and explain her reasoning to others. |
| **Emotional** | Amber/Conventional | Can name basic emotions but loses access to vocabulary under stress. Relies on external rules ("I should feel grateful") rather than internal sensing of what she actually feels. Conflict produces a tight chest and withdrawal — she knows this happens but can't shift it in the moment. |
| **Interpersonal** | Amber/Conventional | Values harmony and shared norms. Finds disagreement destabilizing — she'll agree outwardly to keep the peace even when she disagrees inside. Struggles to set boundaries without feeling guilty. Genuinely cares about people but relates through roles and expectations more than open contact. |
| **Moral** | Orange/Rational | Has strong internal principles grounded in fairness and evidence. Can articulate ethical positions clearly. Occasionally confused when values conflict — leans toward rule-application rather than context-sensitive judgment. |
| **Somatic** | Developing/Orange | Notices body signals — tight shoulders, shallow breathing — but treats them instrumentally. Pushes through rather than listening. Exercise is for fitness, not self-regulation. Body is a vehicle for tasks, not a source of information. |

### What This Actually Means

Maria's cognitive line is her strongest — the one that got the most investment (school, work, analytical tasks) and the most consistent reinforcement. When a problem can be solved by thinking about it, she's effective.

Her emotional and interpersonal lines are substantially less developed than her cognitive line. Under everyday conditions — when things are going smoothly — this asynchrony is manageable. The gap shows up under pressure:

- **In meetings** where someone challenges her analysis: she can defend her reasoning cogently (*cognitive strength*), but the challenge produces a physiological stress response she can't regulate in real time (*emotional gap*). She leaves meetings tense and exhausted, replaying what she "should have said."

- **In relationships** when there's conflict: she can analyze what's happening with real clarity (*cognitive strength*), but she can't always feel her way through it (*emotional gap*), and she struggles to stay present and direct rather than accommodating (*interpersonal gap*). She finds herself saying "I know this shouldn't bother me" while being bothered — the cognitive understanding doesn't directly transfer to emotional regulation or relational skill.

- **In her body**: she sits at a desk for hours, feels tension build, ignores it, then crashes. She doesn't yet have the somatic literacy to use body signals as regulation data — so the body just accumulates stress until it demands attention through exhaustion or illness (*somatic gap*).

None of this means Maria is failing, broken, or developmentally stunted. It means she has a psychograph profile — peaks and valleys — like every human being does. The developmental work is not to judge the valleys but to invest in them with the same quality of attention the peaks received.

## No Formal Cross-Line Assessment Yet

This platform does not yet offer a formal cross-line assessment tool. The individual line assessments that exist (Cognitive, Moral, Emotional/Interpersonal progress self-assessments) each measure one line — they cannot be combined into a single validated cross-line profile.

**What you can do right now:**
- Explore any line overview below that interests you
- Use the [Find Your Path](/start) assessment for a starting-point recommendation
- Read the worked example on this page as a way to think about your own profile informally

A validated cross-line assessment is on the development roadmap. For now, this synthesis page is an orientation tool — a way to see the whole landscape — not a diagnostic instrument.
