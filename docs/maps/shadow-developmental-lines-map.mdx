## 🗺️ Shadow & Developmental Lines Integration Map

## Overview

In the AQAL framework, **developmental lines** — also called "streams" or "multiple intelligences" — are relatively independent capacities that each follow their own developmental trajectory. A person does not develop as a unified whole; rather, their cognitive, emotional, moral, interpersonal, somatic, values, and spiritual lines can advance at very different rates, producing a characteristic profile of strengths and gaps. Ken Wilber's "all-level, all-line" principle holds that a complete map of any individual's development must account for where they stand on *each* line, not simply where their "center of gravity" appears to be across lines combined. This multi-line architecture is one of the most clinically and pedagogically consequential insights in the AQAL system — and one of the most frequently overlooked.

**Shadow is not a single-line phenomenon.** Each developmental line generates its own distinct category of shadow material, arising from precisely the capacities, values, and meaning-making structures specific to that line. The cognitive line can produce an intellectualizing shadow that converts genuine emotional material into abstract analysis. The moral line can produce self-righteous blind spots that enforce accountability on others while shielding the self. The somatic line can accumulate body-held armor, chronic tension, and freeze responses that persist long after the corresponding psychological insight has been achieved. The values line can produce an enacted-vs.-espoused gap, in which a person articulates values at one developmental level while enacting behaviors from a previous level under pressure. Each form of shadow requires practices specifically attuned to the line in which it is held.

The practical consequence is that **single-line shadow work produces single-line results**. A learner who engages exclusively with cognitive shadow work — journaling, analysis, 3-2-1 processes — may develop a sophisticated map of their projections while the somatic residue of those same projections remains fully intact in the body. The "integration" achieved is real but partial. Genuinely durable shadow work addresses the full developmental profile: the insight achieved on the cognitive line must eventually be metabolized by the emotional, somatic, and interpersonal lines as well. This map provides a reference matrix for identifying the characteristic shadow of each line, the most common blind spot associated with it, the category of practice most appropriate for working that line's shadow, and early warning signs that shadow has become entrenched on that line.

This map is a **reference resource**, not a self-paced module. It is designed to be used alongside the Shadow Work pathway modules, particularly after completing [Shadow Work Foundation](../modules/shadow-work-foundation) and [The 3-2-1 Shadow Process](../modules/shadow-321-process). Facilitators and developmental coaches will find it particularly useful as a structured assessment frame when working with clients across multiple sessions. Learners can use it as a diagnostic compass — not to pathologize their profile, but to identify which lines are carrying unexamined shadow and which categories of practice are most likely to move that material.

## Integral / Teal Stage Shadow Across Developmental Lines

At the **Integral / Teal stage**, shadow takes on a distinctive and particularly elusive form. The cognitive sophistication of 2nd Tier enables practitioners to construct elaborate, internally consistent rationales for avoiding precisely the shadow encounter that the stage ostensibly endorses. Teal shadow frequently hides behind the language of integration itself: the same frameworks used to map and work with shadow are deployed as a defense against genuinely encountering it. This makes Teal-stage shadow exceptionally difficult to surface in standard reflective practice, because the practitioner's defenses are built from the same conceptual material as their practice.

The table below describes Integral / Teal-specific shadow expression for each of the seven developmental lines. All line descriptions in the [Shadow × Developmental Lines Matrix](#shadow--developmental-lines-matrix) above apply across stages; this section describes the *distinctive Teal-altitude form* of each line's shadow.

| Developmental Line | Teal-Stage Shadow Expression | Teal-Specific Blind Spot | Recommended Practice | Teal Red Flags |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Cognitive** | Framework proliferation functions as sophisticated avoidance: the practitioner generates new theoretical maps faster than any map can be inhabited and tested against lived experience. Map-territory confusion is the characteristic cognitive failure — the map is experienced as equivalent to the territory it describes, so producing a more precise map feels equivalent to engaging the actual terrain. Intellectual complexity is deployed to bypass direct experience: a genuine moment of emotional activation is immediately converted into a case study in lines asynchrony, dissolving the experience before it can be metabolized. | The cognitive line's Teal shadow is nearly invisible to the practitioner enacting it because the frameworks produced are genuinely sophisticated and pass every intellectual test they know how to apply. The blind spot is that cognitive output cannot be evaluated using cognitive tools alone; whether cognition is functioning as avoidance can only be detected in the emotional, somatic, and behavioral lines. | [Integral Shadow: The Teal Trap](../modules/integral-shadow-teal-trap) for examining framework-as-avoidance; [Critical Thinking Foundations](../modules/critical-thinking-foundations) applied to one's own theoretical productions rather than to external content; embodied presence practices that deliberately suspend framework generation | Generates new integrative frameworks faster than existing frameworks are embodied; describes emotional or somatic experience in exclusively theoretical language; resolves confusion by adding theoretical complexity rather than contacting direct experience; consistently analyzes the shadow work of others without equivalent self-application. |
| **Emotional** | "Post-emotional" bypass is the characteristic Teal emotional shadow: genuine emotional responses are dismissed or reframed as "Green-stage reactivity" using 2nd Tier developmental language. This is not simple intellectualization — it is a specific deployment of the integral framework to pathologize ordinary emotional experience as a developmental failure, insulating the practitioner from the information the emotion carries. Beneath the surface of apparent equanimity, subtle contempt for emotional processing — in oneself and in others — gradually erodes the affective grounding that authentic integration requires. | The Teal emotional blind spot is that developmental language provides a seemingly rigorous justification for emotional suppression: the practitioner is not "avoiding feelings" but has a theoretical framework that classifies the feeling as sub-Teal and therefore already transcended. This makes the bypass particularly resistant to standard emotional intelligence interventions, because the integral framework is applied to reframe those interventions as further evidence of Green-stage thinking. | [Emotional Granularity](../modules/emotional-granularity) with an explicit focus on welcoming rather than evaluating emotional responses; [Shadow Spiritual Bypassing](../modules/shadow-spiritual-bypassing) for the bypass pattern in its developmental-language form; relational accountability practices that require noticing and naming emotional responses without reframing | Reports equanimity in situations that reliably produce strong feeling in peers at comparable altitude; uses stage language when describing emotional responses in self or others; emotional flatness that correlates with increased theoretical productivity; describes emotional activations as data points rather than as lived experience. |
| **Moral** | At Teal, moral shadow takes the form of ethical flexibility rationalized as developmental sophistication: context-sensitivity and perspectival ethics — genuine developmental achievements — are deployed to avoid clear moral accountability. "Skillful means" becomes a mechanism for post-hoc justification of convenient choices rather than genuinely responsive ethical engagement. The practitioner can construct a defensible ethical argument for nearly any position, and this fluency erodes the moral backbone that makes accountability possible; holding every perspective from a meta-position provides a ready escape from the specificity of moral responsibility. | The Teal moral blind spot is perspectival paralysis: because the practitioner can genuinely hold multiple ethical frameworks simultaneously, principled moral stances can be experienced as "merely conventional," creating resistance to the concrete accountability actions those stances require. The sophistication is real; the bypass it enables is equally real. The test is not the quality of the ethical reasoning but the specificity of the behavioral accountability that follows it. | [Integral Ethics: Beyond Relativism](../modules/integral-ethics-beyond-relativism) with explicit attention to the difference between meta-ethical sophistication and behavioral accountability; [Shadow Immunity to Change](../modules/shadow-immunity-to-change) applied to the competing commitments that maintain ethical flexibility as avoidance; concrete repair and accountability practices with specific, named people | Uses "it depends on the level" formulations to deflect moral accountability; produces sophisticated ethical reasoning without concrete behavioral resolution; invokes skillful means or perspectivalism when asked to make a specific commitment or repair; describes others' moral failures through stage-level analysis rather than direct relational response. |
| **Interpersonal** | Teal interpersonal shadow takes the form of relational withdrawal into frameworks: genuine peer relationships — horizontal, mutual, non-hierarchical — are progressively replaced by relationships organized around developmental hierarchy. The practitioner relates most comfortably as a teacher, guide, or meta-perspective holder, and experiences genuinely mutual relationships as an implicit demand to surrender the developmental vantage point that structures their identity. The result is a subtle but pervasive relational loneliness masked by a full calendar of developmental conversations. | The interpersonal blind spot at Teal is that the developmental hierarchy organizing relationships feels entirely justified — it is, after all, based on genuine differences in altitude. The practitioner is not wrong that perspectives differ; the shadow is the use of those differences to avoid the vulnerability of mutual, non-hierarchical encounter. The pattern is most invisible to the person whose relational world is organized entirely around people who are learning from them. | [Shadow in Relationships](../modules/shadow-in-relationships) focused on horizontal relational patterns; [Authentic Dialogue and Collaborative Meaning](../modules/authentic-dialogue-collaborative-meaning) with explicit attention to mutual rather than hierarchical relating; sustained practice in communities of peers where no developmental advantage is available as a relational position | Relational network organized primarily around teacher-student or guide-seeker configurations; discomfort in genuinely peer-level relationships where developmental status is undifferentiated; describes close relationships primarily through the frame of "supporting their development"; interpersonal conflicts consistently explained through the other party's developmental stage rather than the practitioner's own contribution. |
| **Somatic / Kinesthetic** | Teal somatic shadow takes a distinctive form: chronic disconnection from body sensation is rationalized as "transcendence of the gross state." The practitioner has a theoretical framework — gross/subtle/causal state hierarchy — that re-describes somatic disconnection as spiritual advancement. Rather than first-tier suppression of bodily awareness, the Teal practitioner has an integral map that reclassifies the dissociation as development: chronic tension, shallow breathing, and postural collapse are reframed as the natural condition of a practitioner whose center of gravity is above the gross-body level. | The somatic blind spot at Teal is that the theoretical reclassification is performed in the cognitive line — the line that at Teal is most developed and most trusted — while the somatic line itself remains unaddressed. The practitioner's confidence in their own cognitive frameworks is precisely what makes this shadow inaccessible through cognitive means; the body is the only instrument that can detect somatic shadow, and accessing it requires temporarily suspending the frameworks that explain it away. | [Gross State Awareness](../modules/gross-state-awareness) with an explicit frame that gross-state grounding is a prerequisite for genuine state development rather than a lower rung to be left behind; [Emotional Intelligence and the Somatic Line](../modules/emotional-intelligence-somatic-line) with attention to the Teal-specific rationalization pattern; trauma-informed somatic approaches (Tier 2, qualified practitioner required) for body-held material | Describes somatic disconnection or chronic tension in terms of state hierarchy ("I'm less identified with the gross body"); cannot sustain attention on bodily sensation without immediately reframing in theoretical terms; somatic practices consistently redescribed as "working at the gross level" as if this diminishes their relevance; reports state experiences that carry no somatic anchor. |
| **Values** | At Teal, the values line shadow takes a specific form: values become increasingly abstract and systemic — articulated at the level of integral or planetary care — while the practitioner loses felt, embodied connection to the concrete value commitments that ground ethical action. Articulating a 2nd Tier values framework satisfies the values line enough that no concrete behavioral audit is triggered. The gap between the altitude at which values are articulated and the altitude at which they are enacted becomes the defining characteristic of Teal values shadow. | The Teal values blind spot is that the integral values framework is genuinely admirable — it represents real developmental achievement — and this makes the gap between articulation and enactment nearly invisible. A practitioner can sincerely believe they are enacting integral-altitude values while behavioral choices are driven by Orange-altitude status motives or Amber-altitude loyalty structures, because the values line has outpaced the emotional and somatic lines needed to translate values into consistent enacted behavior. | [Shadow Immunity to Change](../modules/shadow-immunity-to-change) applied specifically to the gap between 2nd Tier values articulation and 1st Tier enacted behavior; [Integral Shadow: The Teal Trap](../modules/integral-shadow-teal-trap) for the values dimension of Teal shadow; behavioral audit practices that require specific, concrete, recent examples for each stated value | Values consistently articulated at the systemic or integral-evolution level without behavioral anchors; unable to provide concrete recent examples of acting from stated values under pressure; surrounding community affirms the values articulation without examining enacted behavior; gap between stated priorities and actual time, energy, or resource allocation is visible to others but not to the practitioner. |
| **Spiritual / Meaning** | Teal spiritual shadow takes the form of using integral frameworks to intellectualize contemplative experience: genuine state experiences are immediately narrated through the AQAL lens — gross/subtle/causal/nondual, state × stage, waking-up vs. cleaning-up — before they have been inhabited and metabolized. The narration is not false, but it is premature; the framework converts the experience into an object of understanding before it has been allowed to complete its work as experience. State experiences are described, mapped, and categorized rather than lived through; the sophisticated map replaces the territory it was meant to illuminate. | The spiritual blind spot at Teal is that the integral framework for understanding state development is genuinely sophisticated and useful, which makes the intellectualization of experience feel like practice rather than avoidance. The practitioner who can describe the nondual state in four paragraphs of precise theoretical language may be further from genuine nondual presence than a beginner who sits with ten minutes of confused attention; the eloquence is real and the bypass is equally real. | [Causal / Witness State](../modules/causal-witness-state) and [Nondual Awareness Orientation](../modules/nondual-awareness-orientation) with explicit attention to the distinction between mapping a state and inhabiting it; [Shadow Spiritual Bypassing](../modules/shadow-spiritual-bypassing) for the Teal-specific form of spiritual bypass; contemplative accountability practices that track direct experience rather than theoretical understanding of experience | State experiences consistently described in framework language immediately upon completion rather than first rested in; descriptions of contemplative depth are theoretically sophisticated but do not correlate with relational or somatic change; uses state × stage map to evaluate the experiences of other practitioners; practice investment correlates with increased theoretical output rather than increased somatic or relational presence. |

## Magic / Purple and Red Stage Shadow Across Developmental Lines

Among all the shadow material adults carry, **Magic/Purple and Red stage material is the most reliably activated under stress, threat, and regression**. This reflects a structural feature of development: Magic/Purple and Red are the earliest stable psychosocial stages — their cognitive, emotional, moral, and somatic organizations are the first to be laid down in the nervous system, are held longest, and are therefore the most accessible when regulatory capacity built at later stages is eroded by fatigue, threat, grief, or acute interpersonal conflict. When a Rational or Pluralistic adult regresses under acute stress, regression frequently reaches through Amber directly to these earlier structures, bypassing the stages in between. This is the mechanism behind familiar patterns: a person who ordinarily operates with Green-altitude empathy and nuance suddenly exhibiting tribal contempt and black-and-white thinking in a conflict; a person with well-developed Teal cognitive sophistication regressing to egocentric self-assertion when their identity is threatened.

This dynamic intersects directly with the **pre/trans fallacy** (Wilber, 1980). Because Magic/Purple and Red are both pre-rational, their expressions in adults can superficially resemble the non-rational, holistic modes associated with post-rational development — genuine transpersonal knowing, embodied wisdom, and integral non-linear awareness. Red's emotional intensity and directness can be mistaken for authentic spiritual aliveness and somatic intelligence. Purple's animistic sensing and tribal bonding can be mistaken for non-dual awareness or integral community. The structural differentiator — applied consistently in the Magic/Purple and Red stage orientation modules — is whether the non-rational mode *includes and integrates* prior rational achievements or *excludes and bypasses* them. Regression to pre-rational structure typically excludes; genuine transpersonal development includes. Practitioners and coaches should apply this distinction carefully before interpreting non-rational presentation as either developmental regression or spiritual advancement.

The table below describes how unintegrated Magic/Purple and Red material characteristically surfaces in adults whose center of gravity has moved on. These are not descriptions of adults whose primary center of gravity is at these stages. They describe the **regressive shadow patterns** that adults at Amber, Orange, Green, and Teal altitudes carry from these earliest developmental layers — patterns that become most visible precisely when the person's more developed capacities are under stress.

| Developmental Line | Magic/Purple & Red Shadow Expression in Adults | Typical Blind Spot | Recommended Practice | Red Flags |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Cognitive** | **Magic/Purple:** Magical causality resurfaces — coincidences are read as personally significant signs, pattern-matching generates supernatural attributions, and superstitious logic ("if I feel it must be connected, it is") displaces rational evaluation under stress. This is not post-rational synchronicity or integral intuition; it is pre-causal, egocentric, and does not survive rational scrutiny once the stressor has passed. **Red:** Egocentric binary thinking returns — people and situations sort instantly into ally/enemy, strong/weak, with me/against me. Nuanced reasoning collapses; the cognitive field narrows to tactical threat assessment. > **Pre/trans note for practitioners:** Magic/Purple and Red shadow material in adults presents the most acute pre/trans diagnostic challenge: the presentation is non-rational, and non-rational expression can reflect either pre-rational regression or trans-rational development. The structural differentiator — apply it consistently before interpreting any non-rational presentation — is whether the non-rational mode includes and integrates prior rational achievements or excludes and bypasses them. Regressive Magic/Red activation tends to resolve when the stressor does; genuine trans-rational development tends to persist, deepen, and increase relational care and humility rather than self-focus.

> **Somatic-first intervention note:** Because Magic/Purple and Red material is held in the body's earliest regulatory patterns, somatic interventions — grounding, slow diaphragmatic breathing, physical orientation to present surroundings — are typically the first-line response for regressive activation at these levels. Cognitive and reflective approaches are most effective *after* the autonomic system has sufficiently deactivated. Attempting to work Magic/Red shadow cognitively during full activation is rarely effective and may intensify the episode. See the [Shadowwork Safety Standard](/internal/safety/shadowwork-safety-standard) for the Tier 2 threshold that applies when body-held early developmental material is the active target.

## Lines Asynchrony: Why Development Is Never Uniform

The principle of **lines asynchrony** holds that developmental lines are relatively independent: advancement on one line does not guarantee, and does not produce, equivalent advancement on other lines. A person's center of gravity — the stage level from which they most consistently operate — is a statistical summary across lines, not a uniform altitude. Any honest developmental profile will show significant variation: some lines will be several stages ahead of others, producing characteristic gaps and characteristic shadows.

This is not a deficiency or a failure of development. It is the normal architecture of human growth. What matters clinically and pedagogically is that the asynchrony is recognized and worked with deliberately, rather than concealed behind the most visible (typically cognitive) line.

### Concrete Example 1: Teal Cognitive / Amber Moral

A learner has engaged extensively with integral theory, can map AQAL dimensions with genuine fluency, holds a multi-perspectival worldview on political and scientific questions, and has completed significant cognitive shadow work. Yet on closer examination, their moral line operates from Amber-altitude structures: a strong in-group / out-group distinction drives their loyalty, exceptions to stated universal principles are consistently made for people in their tribal circle, and moral accountability is primarily sought from institutional or authority sources rather than self-generated. The cognitive line's Teal altitude provides sophisticated language for explaining these patterns ("loyalty is a virtue," "context matters") that makes the Amber moral structure nearly invisible. The gap between the two lines produces a specific shadow: the ability to construct an AQAL-fluent narrative for behavior that a simpler moral lens would immediately recognize as inconsistent.

**Practice implication:** This learner needs moral line development — [Contextual Ethics and Moral Complexity](../modules/contextual-ethics-moral-complexity), interpersonal accountability practices, and honest relational feedback — more urgently than further cognitive or framework development. Adding cognitive content to this profile risks deepening the shadow rather than addressing it.

### Concrete Example 2: Green Emotional / Red-Amber Somatic

A learner has done significant emotional line work — they can name subtle emotional states, maintain empathic attunement in dialogue, and have processed a substantial amount of emotional history through therapy or reflective journaling. Their emotional vocabulary and interpersonal sensitivity operate at a Green altitude. Yet their somatic line has received almost no attention: they carry significant chronic tension in the shoulder and throat, their breathing is consistently shallow and chest-held, and when activation arises in interpersonal conflict, their autonomic nervous system enters freeze responses that are entirely discordant with the emotional intelligence visible in calmer states. The body has not metabolized what the emotional line has processed cognitively and verbally. The freeze response is not a Green-altitude response — it reflects an earlier, pre-verbal, somatic structure that emotional line work has not touched.

**Practice implication:** This learner needs body-centered, trauma-informed somatic work — specifically practices that address autonomic nervous system regulation and body-held early material — before or alongside continued emotional development. Somatic Experiencing, Sensorimotor Psychotherapy, or trauma-sensitive yoga address the somatic line at the level where it is actually functioning. This is Tier 2 work requiring a qualified practitioner.

### Concrete Example 3: Teal Values / Orange Interpersonal

A learner articulates their values with impressive developmental sophistication: they speak fluently about integral ethics, second-tier care, and the limitations of Orange-altitude achievement orientation. When asked about their life priorities, they consistently name depth, meaning, and relational richness over status or material success. Yet their observable relational patterns are substantially Orange: they unconsciously organize their social choices around people who can advance their professional standing, they experience interpersonal interactions through a subtle competitive frame, and their closest relationships are structured around admiration for the learner rather than genuine mutuality. The values line has outpaced the interpersonal line; the person articulates Teal-altitude values while enacting Orange-altitude relational strategies.

**Practice implication:** This learner needs interpersonal line development: [Shadow in Relationships](../modules/shadow-in-relationships) for projection work with specific, real people; honest feedback from people who do not have a stake in maintaining the learner's self-image; and sustained practice in relationships where their status is not at stake. The values-interpersonal gap here is not primarily a values problem — it is an interpersonal shadow problem that is currently invisible because the values line is more visible and more discussed.

<Admonition type="note" title="🧑‍🏫 Practitioner Note: Using This Map in 1:1 Developmental Coaching Assessment">

**Purpose in coaching practice:** This matrix is a structured inquiry frame, not a diagnostic instrument. It is not designed to produce a numerical developmental profile or to label a client at a particular stage on each line. Its purpose is to help both coach and client identify *which lines are carrying shadow that is currently unaddressed*, and to match the client's practice investment to the lines most in need of attention.

**Assessment approach:** In an initial or mid-point developmental coaching session, you can use the seven lines as a structured inquiry protocol. For each line, ask the client to:
1. Describe a recent situation in which this line was activated — a moment of cognitive challenge, emotional intensity, moral discomfort, interpersonal friction, somatic activation, values conflict, or meaning-making pressure.
2. Notice what they did with the activation: did they engage it, avoid it, rationalize it, suppress it, or flood?
3. Identify what they do *not* know about this line in themselves — where are the genuine gaps in their self-knowledge on this developmental stream?

**Reading the profile:** The lines where clients speak most fluently and confidently — where they can easily generate rich self-narrative — are typically the lines that have received the most attention and are therefore the lines least likely to be carrying active shadow. The lines where clients become vague, where their language becomes abstract, or where they can describe the terrain of others (colleagues, partners, clients) without describing themselves — these are the lines most likely to be carrying unexamined shadow.

**Matching practice to line:** A client whose cognitive line is highly developed but whose somatic line has been neglected does not need more journaling, more AQAL frameworks, or more reflective exercises. They need body-centered practices. A client whose emotional line is rich but whose values line has been unexamined does not need more emotion-processing — they need an honest behavioral audit of their enacted vs. espoused values. Match the practice category to the line in need, not to the line that is most comfortable.

**Contraindication:** If assessment reveals significant unresolved developmental line material — particularly at the somatic line, where body-held trauma may be present, or at the interpersonal line, where attachment-level material may be active — this is a referral signal for appropriately qualified clinical or therapeutic support. Coaching works with developmental growth on functional lines; it does not treat clinical-level trauma or attachment disruption. See the [Shadowwork Safety Standard](/internal/safety/shadowwork-safety-standard) for the full Tier model and referral criteria.

</Admonition>

## Safety Notes

This reference map involves analytical and reflective engagement with shadow material across multiple developmental lines. It is **Tier 1 (low intensity — analytical and reflective, no active emotional induction)** as a standalone document.

**Consent:** Before using this map in a coaching or facilitation context with another person, obtain explicit informed consent. Explain that the process involves exploring developmental gaps and shadow patterns, that participation is voluntary, and that the learner may pause or stop at any point.

**Contraindications:** This map is not suitable for individuals who are currently in acute psychological crisis, who are experiencing active dissociation, or who have not established a stable reflective baseline. If contraindications are present, defer to the [Shadowwork Safety Standard](/internal/safety/shadowwork-safety-standard) for appropriate referral pathways.

**Stop rules:** Stop using this map and seek qualified support if a learner experiences significant dissociation, emotional flooding that cannot be self-regulated, or physical activation (trembling, nausea, dissociative numbness) that persists after the session. These stop rules apply to both self-directed and facilitated use.

**Grounding:** Before engaging in any line-specific shadow inquiry — particularly somatic line work — establish a grounding practice. A simple body-anchor (both feet on the floor, three slow breaths, awareness of physical contact with the chair) provides sufficient grounding for Tier 1 reflective work. If somatic activation is high, use a full grounding sequence before proceeding.

**Escalation path:** If this map surfaces material that exceeds self-directed or coaching-level work — particularly body-held trauma material on the somatic line, attachment-disruption-level interpersonal patterns, or moral injury that is causing current functional impairment — the appropriate escalation is to a licensed mental health professional with trauma-informed training. Developmental coaching is not a substitute for clinical care.

- When this map is used as an assessment frame in developmental coaching, the safety tier of the resulting practice depends on the depth of the line-specific shadow work initiated, not on the map itself.
- Somatic line work that approaches body-held early trauma material is **Tier 2 minimum** and requires a qualified practitioner. Do not initiate somatic shadow work in coaching or self-directed contexts without the safety provisions described in the [Shadowwork Safety Standard](/internal/safety/shadowwork-safety-standard).
- Do not use this map to generate stage-level labels for learners or clients. Lines asynchrony is a normal developmental pattern, not a diagnostic category.
- For the full Tier model and referral criteria, see the [Shadowwork Safety Standard](/internal/safety/shadowwork-safety-standard).
