# 🗺️ State × Stage Integration Reference Map

## Overview

One of Ken Wilber's most theoretically sophisticated contributions in *Integral Spirituality* (2006) and *The Religion of Tomorrow* (2017) is the demonstration that **the same state can be accessed by practitioners at any developmental stage, yet will be interpreted through radically different meaning-making lenses**. A practitioner who glimpses nondual awareness at an Amber stage centre of gravity may describe it as dissolving into God's love; the same glimpse encountered from an Orange centre of gravity may be decoded as a neurological anomaly or a peak flow state; from Green it may be received as an experience of deep ecological interdependence; and from Teal it may be integrated into a multi-perspectival, intersubjective map of consciousness itself.

This asymmetry has profound practical consequences. Two practitioners reporting "the same experience" may be using identical words to point at entirely different meaning structures. Conversely, two practitioners at the same developmental altitude may dismiss each other's reports as naïve or confused simply because their stage lenses render the shared state unrecognisable. Understanding this cross-matrix relationship protects against spiritual inflation (believing a state experience equals developmental advancement), spiritual bypassing (using state access to avoid stage-level shadow work), and inter-practitioner misunderstanding in peer learning and facilitation contexts.

The **state axis** of the matrix draws on the five-state spectrum used throughout this platform: gross waking, subtle (dream-like luminosity and energy), causal/formless (deep stillness), the witnessing position itself, and nondual. The **stage axis** uses four developmental altitudes — Amber (Mythic-Conventional), Orange (Rational-Achievement), Green (Pluralistic-Egalitarian), and Teal (Integral-Systemic) — corresponding to the developmental bands most active in adult learning populations engaging with this curriculum.

It is important to emphasise that the matrix cells describe **interpretive frameworks**, not states themselves. The gross state is not "better" at Teal than at Amber; it is simply interpreted through a richer conceptual apparatus. Similarly, a high-altitude stage does not guarantee superior state access. Growing Up (stage development) and Waking Up (state development) are relatively independent developmental streams that enrich one another when integrated, but neither reduces to the other.

This reference map is designed for learners who have completed foundational modules in both state access and stage awareness, as well as for facilitators and coaches who need a practical tool for holding multiple interpretive frameworks simultaneously in a learning or coaching conversation.

## The State × Stage Matrix

The table below describes how each state is typically **interpreted and integrated** at each developmental stage. Each cell is a 2–4 sentence description of the meaning-making pattern, not a prescription for how the state "should" be experienced. Real experience is always richer, messier, and more individual than any map cell can capture.

| State | **Amber** (Mythic-Conventional) | **Orange** (Rational-Achievement) | **Green** (Pluralistic-Egalitarian) | **Teal** (Integral-Systemic) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Gross (waking)** | Ordinary reality is sacred creation governed by divine order or natural law handed down through tradition. The body is a gift from God or a vessel of duty; sensory experience confirms the rightness of established roles and structures. Disruptions to the familiar are interpreted as threats to order or signs of moral failure. | Ordinary waking reality is the primary arena of rational agency, material achievement, and measurable progress. The body is a performance system; sensorimotor experience is data for optimisation. States that do not produce measurable results tend to be dismissed as unproductive. | Waking experience is a shared, socially constructed reality in which multiple valid perspectives coexist. The body carries cultural conditioning and intersectional identity; ordinary awareness is the site of empathy, presence, and relational attunement. Ordinary distress is often processed through dialogue and community. | Gross waking reality is understood as one band in a spectrum of consciousness, embedded within systemic and evolutionary processes. The body is an integrated bio-psycho-social system; ordinary awareness is both the platform for action and a lens that can be refined through practice, shadow work, and feedback loops. |
| **Subtle (luminous/absorbed)** | Profound states of inner light, absorption, or visionary experience are interpreted as divine visitation, saintly grace, angelic communication, or answered prayer. They confirm the reality of the supernatural order and the favour of God or higher powers. Significance is received through mythic-religious interpretation and often mediated by a spiritual authority or sacred text. | Subtle states are decoded through a naturalistic lens: they are attributed to neurological events, flow states, right-hemisphere activity, or altered dopamine and serotonin dynamics. They may be valued as productivity tools (peak performance, creative insight) or as fascinating anomalies worth studying. Metaphysical interpretations are typically rejected or held with ironic distance. | Subtle experiences are celebrated as evidence of human depth, spiritual possibility, and the mystery beyond rational reductionism. They are often shared in community as stories of healing, connection, or personal transformation. Multiple interpretive frameworks — from somatic to transpersonal — are held simultaneously, sometimes without a coherent integration principle. | Subtle states are placed in their AQAL coordinate: an interior-individual experience accessible at multiple stages, with both phenomenological richness and neurological correlates, meaningful within multiple interpretive frameworks simultaneously. Stabilisation, integration into daily life, and discernment between peak experience and genuine stage-shift are all tracked. |
| **Causal / formless** | The formless or empty ground of deep stillness is rare, highly valued, and may be interpreted as the presence of the divine beyond all images — the dark night, the cloud of unknowing, the via negativa of mystical traditions. It may also provoke anxiety if it is experienced as loss of God's presence rather than pure presence itself. | Formless stillness is often unfamiliar territory and may initially be experienced as disorienting or "blank." It may be pathologised as a dissociative episode or dismissed as merely sleepiness. If explored, it tends to be approached empirically — as a state to be documented, reproduced, and explained in terms of default mode network quieting or trance. | Causal stillness is often welcomed as a felt sense of openness and non-grasping that supports non-judgement and compassionate presence. It may be interpreted through Buddhist frameworks of sunyata (emptiness), indigenous concepts of receptive spaciousness, or psychodynamic ideas of ego softening. Integration is frequently relational — what does this openness call me toward in community? | The causal state is recognised as the formless ground from which witnessing awareness arises — a distinct and mappable experience on the state spectrum. It is studied with both phenomenological precision and developmental awareness. Practitioners at Teal can track causal access across sits, notice how stage-level concerns modulate even apparently contentless awareness, and use the state to support shadow integration rather than bypass it. |
| **Witness (turiya / pure awareness)** | The witnessing position — pure observing awareness prior to content — is rarely distinguished from the content it witnesses at this stage. If encountered, it may be interpreted as God watching through human eyes, or as the soul's native perspective. Mystical traditions at this altitude tend to describe the witness in personal, devotional terms rather than as an impersonal aware space. | The witness is approached analytically: it may be equated with meta-cognition, executive function, or the observer effect. Practitioners with strong mindfulness practice may intellectually map the witness but have difficulty sustaining identification with awareness rather than with thought. The instinct to explain or narrate the witnessing experience in third-person terms is strong. | The witnessing position is often experienced as a deeply welcome relief from the relentless social and interpersonal demands of the pluralistic stage. It is valued for its non-judgemental, compassionate quality. However, there is sometimes a tendency to merge the witness with the Green-stage ideal of unconditional acceptance, subtly importing a social value into what AQAL treats as a relatively content-free awareness position. | At Teal, the witness is precisely located on the state spectrum — a stabilised, impersonal awareness position that precedes both subject and object. Practitioners can distinguish witness from meta-cognition, sustain it across ordinary activities, and recognise when witnessing becomes a subtle subject resisting release into nondual. The witness is valued as both an intrinsic capacity and a developmental platform. |
| **Nondual** | Nondual glimpses, when they occur, are typically interpreted through the available mythic-religious grammar: union with God, dissolution into the divine, bridal mysticism, or loss of the individual in the ocean of the Absolute. The interpretation is richly meaningful and often transformative. However, because the stage lens does not yet include a concept of awareness as impersonal ground, the nondual event tends to reinforce the devotional relationship rather than dissolve the subject-object structure in a stable way. | If nondual glimpses occur at an Orange centre of gravity, they are frequently bracketed as anomalous, described in hedged language ("a kind of oneness feeling"), or explained away as a neurological event. The stage's strong investment in rational self-authorship makes it uncomfortable to report the dissolution of the separate self. Nondual experiences are therefore either pathologised, privatised, or translated into motivational/flow-state language. | At Green, nondual experience is embraced and celebrated within a framework of interconnection, ecology, deep listening, and mystical communion. It is often the most emotionally open stage for receiving nondual glimpses and sharing them in community. The risk is shallow integration: nondual language proliferates as a shared Green ideal without the discriminating awareness needed to distinguish glimpse from realisation, or to integrate the experience with developmental shadow work. | Teal holds the nondual in its full AQAL coordinate: it is recognised as the most inclusive state (since it contains all others), it is distinguished from stage-level realisations, and it is held within an explicit developmental framework that includes the impermanence of glimpses, the necessity of integration across all quadrants, and the risk of spiritual bypassing. Practice is designed to deepen access while maintaining discernment and stage-level accountability. |

<Admonition type="note" title="🧑‍🏫 Facilitator Note: Using This Map in Coaching and Group Facilitation">

**Purpose of the map in practice:** This matrix is not a diagnostic tool. It does not tell you which stage a learner is "at" based on their state language. It is a *widening frame* — a way of holding the possibility that two practitioners describing the same state may be meaning very different things, and that both accounts are coherent within their respective stage lenses.

**In individual coaching:** When a client reports a profound state experience (a meditation insight, a peak moment, a felt sense of dissolution), use the matrix rows and columns as a gentle inquiry framework. Ask how they are making meaning of the experience: What does it mean to them? What does it confirm or challenge? What tradition or worldview informs their interpretation? These questions — not the matrix itself — surface the stage lens without labelling the person.

**In group facilitation:** When learners compare notes after a shared practice (e.g., a group sit or a structured reflection), use the matrix to normalise interpretive differences. Two people who sat in the same room, with the same instructions, may report radically different experiences. Naming the stage-lens effect — "we are all working from different interpretive frameworks, and all of them are valid at their level" — can significantly reduce the subtle competition and comparison that emerges in contemplative learning communities.

**Developmental ethics:** Never use this map to rank or dismiss a learner's experience. A profound devotional interpretation of a nondual glimpse (Amber) is not a lesser experience than a Teal meta-perspective on the same event. The map describes interpretation, not experience quality. Treat every stage lens with genuine respect.

**Contraindication:** This map is not appropriate for use in crisis situations, for learners experiencing state-related distress, or as a substitute for stage-level developmental coaching grounded in the [Instructional Design Protocol](../quality/instructional-design-protocol). If a learner's state experiences are causing significant confusion or distress, refer to the [State Development Safety Standard](../safety/state-development-safety-standard).

</Admonition>

## Safety Notes

This reference map involves conceptual engagement with altered states of consciousness and developmental frameworks. It is **Tier 1 (low intensity — intellectual and reflective, no active state induction)**.

- This map does not induce states and does not require active contemplative practice to use.
- If using this map alongside active state practices, follow the safety guidance in the relevant state modules (see State Development Modules above).
- If a learner is experiencing confusion, distress, or functional impairment related to state or stage work, refer to the [State Development Safety Standard](../safety/state-development-safety-standard) and, if appropriate, to a qualified teacher or mental health professional.
- Do not use stage labels (Amber, Orange, Green, Teal) in ways that demean, pathologise, or dismiss a learner's experience. All stage lenses reflect genuine coherence at their level of complexity.
- See the [Shadowwork Safety Standard](../safety/shadowwork-safety-standard) for additional guidance if state practice reveals shadow material.
