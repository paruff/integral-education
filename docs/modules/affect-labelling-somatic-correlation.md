## Affect Labelling & Somatic Correlation

This module develops the competency at the junction of the Emotional and Somatic lines (AQAL Competency Map L2), training the capacity to pair a distinct emotional concept with its concrete bodily sensation.

## Learn

### 1. The Interoception–Emotion–Cognition Circuit
Emotions are not purely mental events. They begin as sub-cortical processing patterns derived from interoception—the nervous system's ongoing mapping of internal physiological states (heart rate, respiration, metabolic demands, and muscular tension). The cognitive brain interprets these raw bodily signals through the lens of prior experience and situational context, constructing what we consciously experience as a distinct emotion.

### 2. Damasio’s Somatic Markers
According to Antonio Damasio’s somatic marker hypothesis, body-based feeling states are computationally prior to and constitutive of conscious emotional experience. Before your conscious mind rationalizes a decision or names a feeling, changes in your somatic state (e.g., vascular constriction, gut motility shifts, muscular bracing) act as pre-conscious signals that bias attention and inform decision-making in real-time.

### 3. Lieberman’s Affect Labelling
Affect labelling refers to the explicit process of putting feelings into words. Functional MRI (fMRI) evidence demonstrates that verbalizing an emotional state (e.g., stating "I feel a tightening anxiety") actively down-regulates emotional reactivity. The act of linguistic naming decreases activation in the amygdala (the brain's threat-detection hub) while simultaneously increasing engagement in the prefrontal cortex—effectively "naming to tame."

### 4. The Somatic Topography of Emotions
Research into somatic mapping (Nummenmaa et al., 2014) demonstrates that basic and complex emotions correlate with highly consistent topographical maps of bodily sensation across diverse cultures. For example, anger consistently manifests as increased activation in the chest and upper limbs, while depression correlates with a profound systemic deactivation or numbness throughout the extremities. 

*Caveat: While broad activation clusters replicate cross-culturally, individual nuance varies based on linguistic habits and personal interoceptive baseline conditioning.*

### 5. Definitional Framework: Signal Graduation
To build a precise somatic practice, you must clearly distinguish between four distinct layers of internal experience:

- **Sensation:** The raw, uninterpreted physiological signal (e.g., localized heat, muscular tightness, accelerated heart rate).
- **Feeling:** The conscious, valenced awareness of that signal as either positive, negative, or neutral.
- **Emotion:** A conceptually categorized feeling state mapped to a specific linguistic label (e.g., "indignation" or "grief").
- **Mood:** A prolonged, diffuse background state that lowers the threshold for triggering specific emotional ranges without being bound to a single objective cause.

## Safety Screening & Trauma Note (Tier 2 Content)

> ### ⚠️ Mandatory Pre-Practice Screening & Safety Protocols
> Somatic awareness exercises require directing focused attention inward. For individuals processing body-based trauma or acute post-traumatic stress, turning attention to internal physical sensations can trigger hyper-arousal or involuntary flashbacks.
>
> **Self-Screening Checklist:**
> Before running the body scan practice, ask yourself:
> 1. Do I currently feel connected enough to my physical environment to maintain awareness of my surroundings?
> 2. Am I free from acute somatic flashbacks or severe body dissociation right now?
> 
> **OPT-OUT & STOP RULE:** If at any point during an internal somatic practice you feel intense discomfort, panic, or a sense of flooding, halt the practice immediately. 
> 
> **GROUNDING ALTERNATIVE:** Open your eyes, change your physical posture, look around your room, and slowly name five distinct physical objects you can see. Press your feet firmly into the floor and touch a tangible object nearby to re-anchor your awareness externally. This educational framework is not a substitute for clinical therapeutic support.

## Practice

### Exercise 1: Somatic Scan & Label Pairing (20 Minutes)
1. Sit or recline comfortably. Take 3 slow, natural breaths.
2. Direct your attention systematically through the body, starting at the crown of your head and moving downward to your feet.
3. At each major physical region (jaw, throat, chest, solar plexus, gut), pause and identify any active physical sensations.
4. If a sensation is present, pair it with a precise emotional category using the table format below:

| Body Region | Observed Sensation (Raw Signal) | Paired Emotion Label (Concept) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| *Example: Throat* | *Constriction, localized tightness* | *Unexpressed grief / Hesitation* |
| *Example: Chest* | *Expansion, warmth, light pressure* | *Appreciation / Connection* |

### Exercise 2: Emotion-Sensation Flashcard Drill (10 Minutes)
Utilize an emotion wheel alongside a blank body outline diagram to build cognitive-somatic agility:
- **Forward Path:** Select an abstract emotion label from the wheel, then immediately locate and map where its corresponding somatic footprint typically manifests in your physical body.
- **Reverse Path:** Focus on an active body sensation, trace its boundaries, and quickly map it to 2–3 precise candidate labels on your emotion wheel.

### Exercise 3: Real-Time Somatic Tracking
During an active conversation or high-focus work session today, maintain a dual-track awareness. Log 3 distinct instances of real-time somatic shifts using this 3-column format:

| Triggering Situation / Context | Observed Somatic Sensation | Associated Emotion Label |

- **24-Hour Interval:** Re-run the Core Scan and Label Pairing exercise using a completely fresh somatic state.
