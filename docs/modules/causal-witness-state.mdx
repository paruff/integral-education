## 🧠 Learn

## Three States to Distinguish

The three states addressed in this module are frequently confused with one another. Clarity here is practically essential — what you are looking for determines whether you find it.

| State | Everyday Analogue | Experiential Signature | Practice Access |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Causal** | Deep dreamless sleep | Complete absence of objects, imagery, or content; undifferentiated formlessness; sometimes experienced as pure rest or void | Formless meditation, deep samadhi, open awareness |
| **Witnessing** (*Turiya*) | The aware presence that persists across all other states | Pure, objectless awareness noticing that waking, dreaming, and deep sleep occur *within* it; sense of being the unchanging witness | Self-inquiry ("Who is aware?"), noting practice, open awareness |
| **Nondual** | No everyday analogue | Subject–object distinction dissolves; awareness and its contents are experienced as one seamless whole; not a state one enters but a recognition of what already is | Points to recognition, not a technique — cannot be manufactured |

**Why the distinction matters for practice:** If you seek the causal state by trying to create blankness or void, you are likely manufacturing a subtle-state approximation (suppression) rather than relaxing into formless awareness. If you seek the witness by constructing a mental observer-self, you have created another object rather than found awareness itself. The practices below are designed to *point toward* rather than construct.

## The Causal State in AQAL

Ken Wilber's AQAL framework maps the **causal state** as the third natural state, corresponding to deep dreamless sleep in the ordinary sleep cycle (Wilber, *Integral Spirituality*, 2006). Unlike the subtle state, which is characterised by rich interior content (imagery, emotion, luminosity), the causal state is characterised by the *absence* of content — it is formless, undifferentiated awareness.

In the Vedantic tradition, this state is called *prajna* or the deep sleep of the causal body (*karana sharira*). In the Tibetan Buddhist tradition, formless absorption is approached through the higher jhanas and certain dzogchen pointing instructions. In both traditions, the practitioner's challenge is to remain *aware within* the formlessness rather than simply passing through it unconsciously — as happens in ordinary deep sleep.

> The causal realm is the domain of formless absorption — not blankness but luminous emptiness; not the absence of awareness but awareness in the absence of objects.
> — Wilber, *Integral Spirituality* (2006), paraphrase

## The Witness / Turiya

*Turiya* (Sanskrit: "the fourth") is a term from the Mandukya Upanishad denoting the awareness that witnesses the other three states — waking, dreaming, and deep sleep — without itself being any of those states. It is the **witnessing consciousness**: not a state among other states, but the unchanging presence within which all states arise and pass.

Wilber's integral framing places witness access as the key developmental pivot on the Waking Up path (Wilber, *Integral Spirituality*, 2006). The witness is "always already" present — it cannot be attained or constructed, only recognised. What obscures it is the habitual identification with the contents of awareness (thoughts, feelings, images, sensations) rather than with awareness itself.

In Adyashanti's framing (adapted from Advaita and Zen), awakening to the witness is the first moment of genuine recognition: *I am not my thoughts. I am not my feelings. I am that which is aware of thoughts and feelings* (Adyashanti, *The End of Your World*, 2008).

In Ramana Maharshi's non-dual Advaita teaching, self-inquiry (*atma-vichara*) — "Who am I?" or "Who is aware?" — is the primary direct method for recognising the witness (Maharshi, *Who Am I?*, 1902/2010).

## Why This Is Pedagogically Delicate

The witness cannot be created through instruction — yet practice can create conditions in which recognition is more likely. This paradox requires the practitioner to:

1. **Use the pointer without grasping it.** Descriptions and concepts point toward the witness; they are not the witness itself.
2. **Resist the urge to fabricate a "watcher."** A mental image of a watcher watching thoughts is still a subtle-state object. Awareness itself has no image.
3. **Be patient with ambiguity.** Genuine witness access may first register as a faint background quality — a sense of spaciousness or stillness that does not come and go with thoughts. It is often subtle before it becomes unmistakeable.

## Spiritual Bypassing: The Central Safety Concern

**Spiritual bypassing** (Welwood, *Journal of Transpersonal Psychology*, 1984; Masters, *Spiritual Bypassing*, 2010) describes the use of spiritual practice or belief to avoid, rather than integrate, psychological material. In the context of causal/witness work, bypassing risks include:

- **Dissociation misidentified as the witness.** Dissociation creates a sense of detachment or unreality that can superficially resemble witness access. Key distinction: dissociation is characterised by numbness, emotional flatness, loss of agency, and an inability to re-engage at will. Genuine witness access is characterised by clarity, equanimity, and full capacity for warm engagement.
- **Premature emptiness claims.** Declaring oneself beyond emotions, preferences, or personal psychology on the basis of brief causal-state glimpses, bypassing ongoing shadow, emotional, and relational developmental work.
- **Formlessness as escape.** Using formless meditation to avoid rather than face difficult emotional or interpersonal material present in the subtle state.

The Assess section provides rubric criteria to help distinguish genuine witness access from bypassing and dissociation.

## Practice 1 — Open Awareness Sitting (20 minutes)

**Purpose:** Release the subtle-state habit of following or managing content, and rest in the formless openness that precedes and surrounds all arising.

**Duration:** 20 minutes

**Preparation:**
- Sit comfortably upright — in a chair or cross-legged — with the spine gently lengthened. Eyes may be lightly closed or at a downward soft gaze.
- Set a gentle alarm for 22 minutes (20 minutes of practice plus a 2-minute orientation buffer for the return phase).
- Eliminate distractions. You do not need silence, but relative quiet is helpful for initial sessions.

---

*The following is a facilitator-readable script. Read slowly, pausing at ellipses for 4–6 seconds. Allow long silences. The pauses do more work than the words.*

---

**Settling (3 minutes)**

Take three slow, deliberate breaths … letting each exhale be a release … a setting down of what you are carrying … Allow your body to become still … not rigid … simply at rest … like a stone that has found its place on the bottom of a stream.

Close your eyes, or soften your gaze toward the floor. Allow your hands to rest … your jaw to soften … your shoulders to drop.

For the first minute, simply be aware that you are breathing. You do not need to control the breath. Simply notice … the breath is happening … you are here … aware.

---

**Releasing Content (5 minutes)**

Notice that thoughts are arising … You do not need to follow them, suppress them, or analyse them … Simply notice: a thought has arisen … and like a cloud in a vast sky, it will pass …

*Notice the thought …* *(pause)* *… and let it go …*

Notice any sound in your environment … You do not need to identify its source or judge its pleasantness … simply: *a sound is arising in awareness …* … and passing …

Notice any sensation in the body … without narrating it or managing it … *a sensation is here* … and shifting …

You are not trying to create blankness … you are simply not chasing anything that arises … The sky does not need to clear the clouds … it simply is sky … vast … open … prior to whatever passes through it …

---

**Resting as Awareness (10 minutes)**

Now, gently shift your attention from what you are *noticing* … to *the noticing itself* …

What is it that is aware of these thoughts? … What is it that knows the sensation is present? … You do not need to answer in words … Simply turn the light of attention back toward its own source …

You may notice that wherever you look for this awareness … it is already there … prior to the looking … You cannot see it as an object because it is the seeing itself …

Rest as this … Not as a concept … not as an image … but as the open, presencing quality that is already here before any thought begins …

*If a thought arises, simply notice: there is thinking happening …* *(pause)* *…and return to the open …*

*If a feeling arises, notice: there is feeling happening …* *(pause)* *… and return to the open …*

You are not behind the eyes looking out … You are not localised in the head … Allow awareness to be as large as it is … extending in all directions … without edges … without a centre that it protects …

*(Long pause — 3 to 4 minutes of silence here.)*

There is just this … awareness, present … not doing anything … simply being what it is …

---

**Return (2 minutes)**

Gently allow your attention to gather again around the breath … Feel the weight of the body … Feel the hands … the feet … Take a few deliberate breaths.

Before opening your eyes, take 30 seconds to allow any quality of what you touched — however faint — to be present without grasping it. You cannot hold it. You do not need to.

Open your eyes slowly. Take one minute before speaking or reaching for a device.

## Practice 2 — "Who Is Aware?" Self-Inquiry (15 minutes)

**Purpose:** Use the direct inquiry method adapted from Ramana Maharshi (via Wilber and Adyashanti) to de-identify from content and point directly toward the witnessing awareness.

**Duration:** 15 minutes

**Preparation:**
- Sit comfortably upright. Eyes lightly closed.
- Set a gentle alarm for 17 minutes (15 minutes of practice plus a 2-minute orientation buffer for the closing).
- This practice involves asking a question internally — not to generate an intellectual answer, but to turn attention back toward its own source. If an intellectual answer arises, it is not the answer. Let it go and return to the question.

---

*Script:*

---

**Settling (2 minutes)**

Allow the body to become still. Take a few natural breaths … not controlling … simply arriving … You are going to use a question as a pointer — not a puzzle to solve, but a direction in which to look.

---

**The Inquiry (10 minutes)**

When a thought arises — any thought: a plan, a memory, a feeling, a sensation — notice it … and then, gently, ask internally:

*Who is aware of this thought?*

*(long pause — 20 seconds)*

Do not answer with another thought. Simply turn attention toward whatever is *aware of the thought* … not the content of the thought … but the awareness in which the thought is appearing …

*Where is this awareness?* … Is it located anywhere? … Does it have a centre? … Does it come and go? …

*(pause — 20 seconds)*

Notice: the thought appeared … the thought passed … but something remained … that which noticed the thought … Is that something present before the thought arose? … Is it present now? …

*Who is aware of this awareness?* …

*(long pause — 30 seconds)*

You may notice that every attempt to find the witness as an object simply reveals another layer of witnessing … The witness is not found by looking — it is recognised as that which is already looking …

Let the question rest … in whatever openness it opens … You are not seeking a conclusion … you are inviting recognition …

If the mind insists on an answer: notice — that insistence is another thought … *Who is aware of that insistence?* …

*(silence — 4 minutes)*

---

**Closing (3 minutes)**

Allow the question to dissolve … Rest in whatever quality is present — whether that is clarity, confusion, stillness, or ordinary thinking … All of these are arising in awareness … and awareness remains …

Take three slow breaths. Gently return attention to the body and the room. Take 60 seconds before speaking or moving quickly.

## Practice 3 — Noting Practice for De-centering from Thought (10 minutes)

**Purpose:** Use a structured noting practice — adapted from Vipassana and contemporary mindfulness — to break the automatic identification with thought, and reveal the witnessing space that thought arises within.

**Duration:** 10 minutes

**Preparation:**
- Sit comfortably upright, eyes lightly closed.
- Set a gentle alarm for 12 minutes (10 minutes of practice plus a 2-minute orientation buffer for the closing).
- Noting practice involves silently labelling what arises in awareness with a brief, gentle word. Labels are approximate and are not meant to be analytically precise.

---

*Script:*

---

**Opening (1 minute)**

Settle into the body … Allow the breath to be natural … You are going to watch what arises in awareness and quietly name it — not to analyse it, but to create a moment of separation between awareness and its contents.

---

**Noting (8 minutes)**

When a thought arises, note silently: *"thinking …"*

When a feeling or emotion arises, note: *"feeling …"*

When a sensation in the body arises, note: *"sensing …"*

When a sound is heard, note: *"hearing …"*

When a planning or future-oriented thought arises, note: *"planning …"*

When a memory arises, note: *"remembering …"*

Allow each note to be gentle, brief, without judgment … The note is simply a flag planted: *something arose here …*

*(pause — 10 seconds)*

After noting, notice: *did the content fully occupy awareness, or was awareness still present as the space in which the content arose?*

*(pause — 10 seconds)*

Continue noting whatever arises …

*(pause — 30 seconds)*

*Thinking … feeling … sensing … hearing … planning … remembering …*

*(pause — 30 seconds)*

Notice: between the notes, in the small gap before the next arising, there is awareness … unmarked … not labelled … simply present …

*(silence — 3 minutes)*

---

**Closing (1 minute)**

Allow the noting to become less frequent … Let the awareness rest without needing to label anything … Simply be present …

Take three deliberate breaths. Open your eyes slowly. Take 30 seconds of silence before speaking.

## 🔍 Reflect

Record responses in your journal as soon as possible after completing any of the three practices, while the experience is still fresh.

1. During the practice, did you notice a quality of awareness that seemed to *persist* across the arising and passing of different thoughts, feelings, or sensations? How would you describe this quality? Use phenomenological language — what did it feel like, rather than what you believe it to be?

2. Reflect on the difference between *experiencing* the witness and *thinking about* the witness. Did moments arise where you were clearly thinking *about* witnessing rather than simply being aware? How did those moments feel different from moments where awareness was more direct?

3. Where in your daily life do you already touch witnessing awareness without calling it that — moments of natural presence, openness, or being fully here without the commentary mind? What conditions seem to support this?

4. Consider the caution about spiritual bypassing. Are there areas of your inner life — emotional patterns, relational difficulties, shadow material — that you might be tempted to declare "transcended" or irrelevant on the basis of the open, spacious quality you touched in practice? Be honest.

5. If you encountered confusion, blankness, restlessness, or a sense that "nothing happened" during the practices, reflect on what that experience itself reveals. What was present? What was the quality of awareness in that apparent nothing?

## 📊 Assess

Use this rubric after completing each practice at least twice. Review again after one week of regular practice. The rubric also addresses spiritual bypassing and dissociation markers to support honest self-assessment.

| Criterion | Not yet | Developing | Stable |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Witnessing presence** — You can notice a quality of awareness that persists as thoughts, feelings, and sensations come and go | Cannot locate any quality distinct from the content of experience; identification with content is total | A faint sense of background spaciousness or stillness is occasionally present; difficult to sustain | A clear and stable quality of witnessing awareness is accessible within 2–3 minutes of sitting |
| **De-centring from thought** — You can observe thoughts without being pulled into their content or narrative | Thoughts pull attention fully; no gap between arising and identification | Can note thoughts briefly but quickly re-identified; noting feels effortful | Thoughts arise, are noted, and pass without full absorption; a sustained gap is present |
| **Returning at will** — You can disengage from thought or emotion and return to open awareness using a simple pointer (the question, a breath, a note) | Cannot return voluntarily; requires distraction or external interruption | Can return but requires significant effort; takes 1–2 minutes | Can return within a few breaths using a brief pointer |
| **Distinguishing witness from dissociation** — You can identify whether a quality of detachment is warm/clear (witness) or numb/flat (dissociation) | Cannot identify the difference or consistently reports both as identical | Recognises the distinction conceptually but uncertain in practice | Can reliably identify in real time; reports warmth, clarity, and full relational capacity alongside equanimity |
| **Not fabricating a watcher** — You can recognise when you have constructed a mental observer-self rather than resting as awareness | Consistently reports a "watcher" that feels like a separate, watching entity | Recognises the distinction and catches the construction, but construction recurs frequently | Rests as awareness without consistently constructing a separate observer |
| **Bypassing awareness** — You maintain honest recognition that causal/witness access does not dissolve developmental needs in other lines (emotional, relational, shadow) | Declares emotional or relational development "transcended" on the basis of state access | Understands the distinction intellectually; some conflation in practice | Clearly distinguishes state access from stage development; continues shadow and relational work alongside state practice |
| **Integration** — You can briefly articulate what was touched in practice and identify at least one point of contact with daily life | Cannot recall or articulate; practice feels entirely other-worldly | Can recall but cannot connect to daily life concerns | Can articulate and identify at least one waking-life integration point |

**Passing threshold:** Five or more criteria at *Developing* or *Stable* after two weeks of consistent practice.

**Red-flag indicators — pause practice and consult a qualified facilitator if present:**
- Persistent difficulty returning to ordinary engaged waking life after practice; loss of interest in relationships, responsibilities, or embodied experience.
- Sense of being "beyond" emotional or relational needs on the basis of state experiences alone, without corresponding shift in actual relational skill or shadow awareness.
- Experiences of depersonalisation, derealization, emotional numbness, or a sense that the self or others "are not real" that persists beyond the practice session.
- Inability to distinguish the practice state from ordinary dissociation; reporting that dissociative episodes in daily life feel like "witnessing."
- Using causal or witness framing to avoid ongoing clinical, therapeutic, or shadow work that has been identified as appropriate.

- **Level 3:** "During meditation, I experienced about 30 seconds where I disappeared — there was just awareness with no separate self watching."
- **Level 4:** "I recognise the Witness during dishwashing: hands moving, water running, and a clear sense that I was the space in which this was occurring."


## 🔗 Integrate

Carry the witness perspective gently into waking life — not as a performance of spiritual detachment, but as a quietly available ground of presence:

- **Morning noting:** In the first three minutes after waking, before reaching for a device, silently note whatever arises in awareness for 60 seconds: *"thinking … feeling … sensing …"* Notice the awareness that contains each arising.
- **Pause and witness:** Once daily, at a natural break in activity, take 60 seconds to ask: *"What is aware of this moment?"* Do not answer verbally — simply attend to the question as a pointer. Return to activity.
- **Embodied presence check:** After any formless or witness-state sitting, deliberately re-engage the body — feel both feet on the floor, press the hands together, take a slow breath, notice three sensory details in the room. This prevents chronic subtle dissociation from building up through practice.
- **Shadow and witness integration:** If shadow or emotional material arose during practice — strong feeling, a memory, an interpersonal image — bring it to your journal using the Reflect prompts from the [Subtle State Access](./subtle-state-access) module or the shadow integration framework in your broader practice. The witness sees clearly; it does not transcend what needs integration.
- **Waking recognition log:** Keep a brief log (one or two sentences per entry) of moments in daily life when witnessing awareness was naturally present — conversations, creative work, moments in nature. Over two weeks, note whether these moments are becoming more frequent or more recognisable.

:::caution Safety Note

This module is rated **Tier 2** for individuals with a personal history of dissociation, depersonalisation, derealisation, trauma, or psychosis-spectrum experience. For all others it operates as a **Tier 1 (self-guided)** module with heightened self-monitoring requirements given the formless and de-centering nature of the practices.

**Consent:** Proceed when you are willing to release habitual identification with thoughts, feelings, and the sense of a fixed self-location, and to observe what remains. This is a significant invitation. You may pause or stop at any time. The grounding anchor — pressing both feet firmly into the floor, opening your eyes, and naming five objects you can see — is available at any moment.

**Contraindications:** If you are currently experiencing dissociation, depersonalisation, psychosis, mania, or acute trauma response, do not undertake this module without guidance from a qualified mental health professional or trained integral facilitator. This platform does not provide clinical care.

**Spiritual bypassing caution:** Causal and witness-state practices can produce a genuine sense of spaciousness and relief from the weight of personal narrative. This is a valid and valuable experience. It does not mean that ongoing developmental work in emotional, relational, shadow, and somatic lines is complete. Continue that work in parallel. Do not use state access to avoid integration.

**Dissociation caution:** A sense of detachment from thoughts, emotions, or the body is a normal feature of witness practice *during* seated sessions. If this quality persists into daily life — reducing your capacity for engaged, warm contact with others or with your own emotional experience — pause the practice and consult a qualified facilitator.

**Stop rules:** If at any point you feel unable to return to ordinary waking engagement, feel acute panic or terror, experience prolonged derealization (sense that the world or self is not real), or feel as though you are losing your mind, stop immediately. Open your eyes. Press both feet firmly into the floor. Name five objects you can see. Breathe slowly. If disorientation persists beyond five minutes, contact a qualified support person.

**Escalation:** If any distress or disorientation persists after pausing practice, step away from all state-development practices and contact a trusted support person or qualified practitioner.

For the full safety framework, see the [Shadowwork Safety Standard](/internal/safety/shadowwork-safety-standard). Use the [Pre/Trans Discernment Guidelines](/internal/safety/shadowwork-safety-standard#pretrans-discernment-guidelines) if what arises feels like dissolution or regression rather than clear witnessing.

:::
