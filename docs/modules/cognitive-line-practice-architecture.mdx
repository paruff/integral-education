## 🧭 Overview

This module operationalizes cognitive development as deliberate practice over 10 weeks. The aim is not content accumulation. The aim is **operational restructuring**: developing new ways of thinking by repeatedly working at the edge of current capacity, meeting contradictions that current operations cannot resolve, and making thinking itself visible for analysis.

## 🔁 Modified Mastery Loop

This module uses a modified loop: **Orient → Practice (Embody) → Reflect → Assess → Integrate**. The **Embody** section is intentionally the most substantive because cognitive growth requires repeated restructuring practice, not one-time conceptual understanding.

## 🧭 Orient

Vygotsky's zone of proximal development and Commons' hierarchical complexity research converge on a practical point: cognitive operations develop when tasks exceed current operations but remain workable with scaffolding (Vygotsky, 1978; Commons & Richards, 1984). Mezirow adds that critical reflection on assumptions is a catalyst for transformation, not just better argument (Mezirow, 1991). In practice, this means you develop cognitively by restructuring how you think, not by reading more facts.

## Three accelerants with concrete examples

1. **Edge-of-capacity engagement**  
   Work one problem each week that your current operations cannot solve comfortably.  
   *Example:* A product manager who usually optimizes single-team outcomes maps second-order effects across policy, user behavior, and market dynamics before proposing a solution.

2. **Contradiction encounter**  
   Identify genuine paradoxes that resist your current frame and stay with unresolved tension long enough for reorganization.  
   *Example:* "We need standardized processes for reliability" and "innovation requires local autonomy" are both true; neither can be discarded without loss.

3. **Metacognitive reflection**  
   Make your own thinking explicit and inspectable so assumptions can be revised.  
   *Example:* After a failed decision, you reconstruct the reasoning path, identify hidden assumptions, and test what a more complex operation would have noticed.

## 🧘 Practice

## 🌱 Embody

This is the core of the module: a 10-week architecture with three streams. Keep one written weekly entry for each active stream.

### Stream 1 (Weeks 1–10): Edge Problems

Each week, choose one problem from your own professional or intellectual domain that is explicitly beyond your current cognitive comfort zone.

**Weekly protocol:**
- Define the problem and why it exceeds your current default operation.
- Attempt a first-pass solution and document where reasoning breaks down.
- Add one scaffold (mentor prompt, alternative framework, systems map, or model).
- Write one revised operation you used that you did not reliably use before.

### Stream 2 (Weeks 1–10): Contradiction Journaling

Each week, identify one genuine contradiction in your worldview that current thinking cannot resolve.

**Weekly protocol:**
- State both poles as claims you endorse.
- Name what real value each pole protects.
- Record your impulse toward premature closure (choosing one pole, averaging, avoidance).
- Practice holding the contradiction unresolved for at least 48 hours before synthesis attempts.

### Stream 3 (Weeks 3–10): Metacognitive Mapping

Starting Week 3, run a weekly Schön-style reflection-in-action / reflection-on-action review.

**Weekly prompts (required):**
- What was I thinking?
- What assumptions was I making?
- What would a more complex cognitive operation reveal here?

Add one concrete behavioral experiment for next week based on your answers.

## 🔍 Reflect

Use these prompts at the end of each week:

1. Where did I hit genuine cognitive edge rather than perform familiar analysis?
2. Which contradiction did I try to close too quickly, and why?
3. What assumption became newly visible this week?
4. What changed in my operations, not just in my opinions?

## 📊 Assess

Rate at Week 1 (baseline), Week 5 (midpoint), and Week 10 (close):

| Criterion | Not yet (1) | Emerging (2) | Developing (3) | Stable (4) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| I regularly choose edge problems beyond comfort-zone analysis | Rarely | Inconsistent | Weekly with evidence | Weekly and generalized across domains |
| I can hold genuine contradiction without premature closure | Usually avoid/close | Sometimes hold | Usually hold with quality notes | Reliably hold and derive higher-order synthesis |
| I make assumptions visible and revise them deliberately | Minimal visibility | Some visibility | Clear weekly maps | Real-time metacognitive adaptation |
| I can identify a shift in cognitive operations over the cycle | No clear shift | Early hints | Observable in multiple entries | Consolidated and behaviorally transferable |

**Passing threshold:** 12/16 with no criterion below 2.

:::note Safety Note
This module is rated **Tier 1 (self-guided, low intensity)**.

- **Consent:** Proceed only if you are willing to engage contradiction and cognitive discomfort at a manageable pace.
- **Right to stop:** You may pause or stop any activity at any time.
- **Contraindications:** If you are currently in acute distress, active trauma response, severe depression, suicidality, dissociation, or psychosis-spectrum symptoms, do not do this module self-guided.
- **Stop rules:** Stop immediately if you feel overwhelmed, dysregulated, dissociated, or unable to orient.
- **Grounding alternative:** If activated, switch to a grounding-only week: breathe slowly for two to three minutes at whatever pace feels comfortable, name five things you can see, and place both feet firmly on the floor.
- **Escalation path:** If distress persists after grounding, stop the practice and contact a qualified clinician, facilitator, or local emergency support as appropriate.
- This module is educational and is **not therapy, diagnosis, or clinical care**.
:::
