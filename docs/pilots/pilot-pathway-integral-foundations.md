# Pilot Pathway A: Integral Foundations (4 Weeks)

Audience: adult learners building baseline integral literacy and practical transfer skills.

## Overview

This pathway introduces learners to the Integral/AQAL framework and builds the foundational competencies required for applied integral practice. By the end of week 4, learners will be able to analyze real-world challenges using all five AQAL dimensions and propose evidence-grounded interventions.

Safety tier: **Tier 1** (self-paced, no facilitator required).

Related backlog issue: E5-1.

## AQAL Mapping

### Quadrants

| Quadrant | Target Competency | Primary Learning Activity | Evidence of Mastery |
|---|---|---|---|
| I (Interior-Individual) | Recognize personal perspective bias; apply self-reflection practices | Reflection prompts, journaling, concept mapping | Written reflection citing at least two AQAL dimensions |
| We (Interior-Collective) | Identify multiple valid perspectives on shared challenges | Scenario analysis, dialogue prompts | Scenario analysis distinguishing at least two quadrant perspectives |
| It (Exterior-Individual) | Map observable behaviors and skills to AQAL levels | Behavioral examples, skills audit | Skills audit table mapped to competency levels |
| Its (Exterior-Collective) | Recognize systemic and cultural factors using AQAL | Systems mapping exercise | Systems map with at least one Its-quadrant intervention |

### Levels

Levels use the L1–L4 bands defined in the [AQAL Competency Map](../maps/aqal-competency-map): L1 (Foundational) → L2 (Differentiated) → L3 (Integrative) → L4 (Transformative).

- Entry level assumption: L1–L2 (learners can name basic concepts and apply them in familiar contexts).
- Progression target: L2–L3 (learners apply concepts independently across moderately novel contexts by week 4).
- Mastery threshold: consistent L2 performance across all four quadrants, with evidence of emerging L3 integration in the capstone task.

### Lines

- **Cognitive:** distinguishing quadrants, levels, and lines as distinct analytic categories.
- **Emotional:** recognizing emotional states as data; applying basic state regulation before reflective tasks.
- **Moral:** identifying ethical dimensions within AQAL analysis scenarios.
- **Interpersonal:** taking and articulating perspectives beyond one's own default quadrant.

### States

- Target state: alert, grounded, and reflective (low-arousal engaged state).
- State preparation: 2-minute breath anchor or body scan before each session (provided as optional micro-reset).
- Debrief: brief written check-out at end of each weekly block.

### Types

- Introverts: all activities support solo written reflection before any peer-facing task.
- Extroverts: optional peer dialogue and discussion board prompts are provided for each week.
- Analytic learners: data tables, rubric grids, and evidence-citation tasks are scaffolded throughout.
- Narrative learners: case studies and story-based scenario prompts accompany every analytic exercise.

## Design Constraints

- Microlearning units are 3-5 minutes, one concept each.
- Every week includes retrieval at 24h, 72h, and 7d.
- Interleaving progression: weeks 1-2 use AABBCC blocked practice; weeks 3-4 use ABCABC interleaved practice.

## Weekly Arc

| Week | Focus | AQAL Emphasis | Retrieval Pattern | Assessment |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Orientation and quadrant basics | Quadrants (I, We, It, Its) | AABBCC — cluster A | Concept map + formative check |
| 2 | Levels and developmental lines | Levels + Lines | AABBCC — cluster B | Applied scenario analysis |
| 3 | States, types, and integrative synthesis | States + Types + 4Q synthesis | ABCABC — interleaved | Performance memo |
| 4 | Transfer to real context | Full AQAL stack | ABCABC — interleaved | Capstone mini-case and reflection |

## Week 1: Orientation and Quadrant Basics

### Learning Objectives

1. Name and define each of the four quadrants (I, We, It, Its) in plain language.
2. Explain why single-perspective analysis produces predictable blind spots.
3. Give at least one example of each quadrant drawn from a familiar work or personal context.
4. Complete a quadrant-tagging exercise on a provided scenario with ≥ 75% accuracy.
5. Identify which quadrant you most often default to and state one consequence of that default.

### Micro-Lesson Sequence

| # | Title | Format | Duration | Concept |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1.1 | Why Partial Perspectives Fail | Text + diagram | 4 min | The cost of single-quadrant thinking; real-world example of a failed initiative |
| 1.2 | The Four Quadrants: A Quick Map | Text + visual | 4 min | I, We, It, Its definitions; interior/exterior and individual/collective axes |
| 1.3 | Quadrant Tagging Drill | Practice | 5 min | Classify 10 statements by quadrant; immediate answer feedback |
| 1.4 | Levels as Complexity, Not Labels | Text | 4 min | Introducing L1–L4 bands; framing levels as expanding capacity, not ranking worth |
| 1.5 | Which Quadrant Do You Default To? | Reflection | 5 min | Self-inventory prompt; journaling entry on personal default quadrant |
| 1.6 | Concept Map Activity | Practice | 5 min | Draw or annotate a simple AQAL grid for a familiar challenge |

**Formats key:** Text = written micro-lesson; Practice = interactive exercise or drill; Reflection = journaling or structured prompt.

### Retrieval Schedule — Week 1 (AABBCC: cluster A = Quadrants)

| Day | Retrieval Type | Items | Spacing |
|---|---|---|---|
| Day 2 (24 h) | Cluster A recall quiz | 5 multiple-choice: quadrant definitions and examples | First retrieval after initial learning |
| Day 4 (72 h) | Cluster A application drill | Scenario-to-quadrant matching (5 new scenarios) | Spaced reinforcement |
| Day 8 (7 d) | Cluster A reflection | Short written response: apply quadrant map to one new situation from this week | Consolidation retrieval |

### Week 1 Performance Task

**Task:** Create an annotated AQAL concept map for a real or hypothetical workplace challenge. Label at least one factor in each of the four quadrants and explain why it belongs there.

**Rubric:**

| Dimension | Developing (1) | Meeting (2) | Exceeding (3) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Quadrant coverage | 1-2 quadrants populated | 3 quadrants populated with accurate labels | All 4 quadrants populated with accurate, specific labels |
| Explanation quality | Labels only, no reasoning | Brief reasoning provided for each label | Clear, specific reasoning linked to source material |
| Self-awareness | No reflection on default quadrant | Reflection present but generic | Specific insight about personal default with one stated consequence |

Passing threshold: ≥ 5 / 9 (sum across all dimensions).

## Week 2: Levels and Developmental Lines

### Learning Objectives

1. Describe the four developmental levels (L1–L4) and their characteristic capacities.
2. Identify at least three developmental lines (cognitive, emotional, moral, interpersonal) and explain how they differ.
3. Recognize that a learner may operate at different levels across different lines simultaneously.
4. Apply the levels-and-lines framework to a provided scenario, identifying which line is most relevant.
5. Complete a self-assessment of your own developmental profile across at least three lines.

### Micro-Lesson Sequence

| # | Title | Format | Duration | Concept |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2.1 | Levels Revisited: What Expands at Each Stage | Text | 4 min | Deepening L1–L4 with behavioral markers; common misreading of levels as fixed identity |
| 2.2 | Developmental Lines: More Than One Kind of Smart | Text + examples | 4 min | Cognitive, emotional, moral, interpersonal, somatic lines; multiple intelligences parallel |
| 2.3 | Lines Are Relatively Independent | Text + case study | 4 min | Case study: high cognitive, lower emotional line; implications for learning design |
| 2.4 | Lines Mapping Exercise | Practice | 6 min | Learner maps one real person (self or composite) across 4 lines using provided scaffold |
| 2.5 | Levels + Lines in a Real Scenario | Practice | 5 min | Apply levels-and-lines lens to a provided organizational scenario; identify leverage point |
| 2.6 | Self-Assessment: Your Developmental Profile | Reflection | 6 min | Structured self-rating across 4 lines; written reflection on one growth edge |
| 2.7 | Applied Scenario Analysis | Practice | 5 min | Full scenario: identify quadrant, level, and line for each element of the scenario |

### Retrieval Schedule — Week 2 (AABBCC: cluster B = Levels + Lines)

| Day | Retrieval Type | Items | Spacing |
|---|---|---|---|
| Day 2 (24 h) | Cluster B recall quiz | 5 items: level definitions, line names, key distinctions | First retrieval after initial learning |
| Day 4 (72 h) | Cluster B application drill | Scenario: identify level and most activated line for three characters | Spaced reinforcement |
| Day 8 (7 d) | Cluster B mixed reflection | Written: describe one moment this week where you noticed a line-level mismatch in yourself or another | Consolidation retrieval |

### Week 2 Performance Task

**Task:** Write a 250–350 word applied scenario analysis. Choose a real or hypothetical situation involving at least two people. Identify the quadrant, developmental level, and primary developmental line at play for each person. Propose one concrete action that addresses the most limiting factor.

**Rubric:**

| Dimension | Developing (1) | Meeting (2) | Exceeding (3) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Quadrant identification | 0-1 quadrant accurately named | 2 quadrants accurately named and justified | All relevant quadrants named and justified |
| Levels accuracy | Level applied but not justified | Level applied with one behavioral marker | Level applied with two or more specific behavioral markers |
| Lines differentiation | Lines not distinguished from levels | At least one line named and distinguished from level | Two or more lines named, distinguished, and applied to the scenario |
| Action quality | Action not linked to AQAL analysis | Action linked to one AQAL dimension | Action clearly targets the most limiting dimension with rationale |

Passing threshold: ≥ 8 / 12 (sum across all dimensions).

## Week 3: States, Types, and Integrative Synthesis

### Learning Objectives

1. Distinguish between states (temporary) and stages/levels (enduring) using at least one concrete example.
2. Describe how type differences (style, personality, cultural background) modulate AQAL expression.
3. Construct a full AQAL analysis of a complex scenario using all five dimensions.
4. Identify an appropriate intervention for each quadrant in a given scenario.
5. Communicate an AQAL analysis clearly and concisely in writing (performance memo format).

### Micro-Lesson Sequence

| # | Title | Format | Duration | Concept |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 3.1 | States vs. Stages: What Changes and What Persists | Text | 4 min | Defining states; examples of altered, peak, and ordinary states; contrast with levels |
| 3.2 | States as Learning Variables | Text + practice | 4 min | How learner state affects uptake; state preparation before learning; brief body scan option |
| 3.3 | Types and Styles: Working with Difference | Text | 4 min | Introvert/extravert processing; cultural type variations; types do not determine levels |
| 3.4 | State Check-In Protocol | Practice | 3 min | Guided 2-minute body scan + emotion label; structured check-in form |
| 3.5 | Full AQAL Stack: From Analysis to Synthesis | Text + diagram | 5 min | Layering all five AQAL dimensions onto one scenario; decision sequence |
| 3.6 | 4Q Problem Mapping Drill | Practice | 6 min | Apply all four quadrants plus levels and lines to a new scenario; identify one intervention per quadrant |
| 3.7 | Performance Memo: Worked Example | Video/text | 5 min | Demonstrated AQAL memo with commentary on reasoning choices |
| 3.8 | Performance Memo Draft | Practice | 10 min | Learner drafts their own performance memo for a provided complex scenario |

### Retrieval Schedule — Week 3 (ABCABC interleaved: A = Quadrants, B = Levels + Lines, C = States + Types + Synthesis)

| Day | Retrieval Type | Items | Spacing |
|---|---|---|---|
| Day 2 (24 h) | Interleaved mixed quiz | 6 items: 2 from A (quadrants), 2 from B (levels/lines), 2 from C (states/types) | First interleaved retrieval |
| Day 4 (72 h) | Interleaved scenario drill | Three-part scenario: identify quadrant (A), level/line (B), and state/type factor (C) for each element | Spaced interleaved reinforcement |
| Day 8 (7 d) | Synthesis reflection | Written: describe one decision this week where you used more than two AQAL dimensions simultaneously | Consolidation + transfer |

### Week 3 Performance Task

**Task:** Write a 300–400 word AQAL performance memo analyzing a workplace or community challenge you select. The memo must address all five AQAL dimensions and propose one evidence-grounded intervention per quadrant. Use the performance memo format from micro-lesson 3.7.

**Rubric:**

| Dimension | Developing (1) | Meeting (2) | Exceeding (3) |
|---|---|---|---|
| AQAL completeness | 1-2 dimensions addressed | 3-4 dimensions addressed | All 5 dimensions addressed with clear linkage |
| Intervention design | Actions not linked to quadrant | One action per quadrant, loosely linked | One specific action per quadrant, explicitly linked to AQAL analysis |
| Evidence quality | No reasoning or citation | Reasoning present; at least one source or example cited | Reasoning well-developed; at least two sources or specific examples cited |
| Clarity | Structure unclear; ideas difficult to follow | Mostly clear structure with minor gaps | Clear, logical structure; non-specialist could follow the argument |

Passing threshold: ≥ 9 / 12 (sum across all dimensions).

## Week 4: Transfer to Real Context

### Learning Objectives

1. Apply the full AQAL stack independently to a personally chosen real-world challenge.
2. Identify one ILP practice per quadrant that fits your current context and readiness.
3. Construct a personal 4-week integral learning plan with specific next-step actions.
4. Evaluate the quality of an AQAL analysis (your own or a peer's) using the pathway rubric.
5. Articulate one insight from this pathway that you will carry forward into practice.

### Micro-Lesson Sequence

| # | Title | Format | Duration | Concept |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 4.1 | Capstone Orientation: Integrating Your Learning | Text | 4 min | Purpose of transfer tasks; overview of capstone deliverables |
| 4.2 | Selecting and Framing Your Challenge | Reflection | 6 min | Guided prompt: choose a real challenge; frame it using the four quadrants before analysis |
| 4.3 | ILP Practices for Each Quadrant | Text | 4 min | Menu of low-intensity practices mapped to each quadrant; selection criteria |
| 4.4 | Drafting Your AQAL Case Study | Practice | 10 min | Learner produces a full AQAL analysis of their chosen challenge with all 5 dimensions |
| 4.5 | Evaluating AQAL Analyses: What Good Looks Like | Text + examples | 4 min | Annotated examples at developing, meeting, and exceeding levels; self-check rubric |
| 4.6 | Self-Assessment Against Competency Markers | Practice | 5 min | Learner rates own capstone draft against the rubric before submission |
| 4.7 | Personal ILP Plan: Next Steps | Reflection | 6 min | Structured template: one practice per quadrant, frequency, starting date, first check-in |

### Retrieval Schedule — Week 4 (ABCABC continued interleaving)

| Day | Retrieval Type | Items | Spacing |
|---|---|---|---|
| Day 2 (24 h) | Interleaved all-pathway quiz | 6 items: 2 from A (quadrants), 2 from B (levels/lines), 2 from C (states/types/synthesis) | Cumulative interleaved retrieval |
| Day 4 (72 h) | Applied capstone scenario | New scenario: full AQAL analysis in 15 minutes; compare your response to a model answer | Spaced transfer practice |
| Day 8 (7 d) | Integration reflection | Written: one thing you will do differently in the next 30 days as a result of this pathway; which AQAL dimension prompted the change | Consolidation + forward planning |

### Week 4 Performance Task (Capstone)

**Task:** Produce a 400–500 word AQAL mini-case for a real challenge you face. Include: (1) full AQAL analysis across all five dimensions; (2) one intervention per quadrant with rationale; (3) a personal ILP plan for the next 4 weeks; (4) one evidence citation per non-trivial claim.

**Rubric:**

| Dimension | Developing (1) | Meeting (2) | Exceeding (3) |
|---|---|---|---|
| AQAL completeness | 1-3 dimensions addressed | 4 dimensions addressed | All 5 dimensions addressed with explicit linkage across dimensions |
| Intervention quality | Actions vague or not linked to analysis | Specific actions linked to AQAL analysis | Specific, prioritized actions with stated rationale and feasibility note |
| ILP plan | Plan missing or generic | Plan includes at least one practice per quadrant | Plan includes one practice per quadrant, frequency, start date, and first review point |
| Evidence and citation | No citations | At least one claim supported by a source or example | All non-trivial claims supported; sources are verifiable |
| Reflection depth | No personal insight | One personal insight stated | Insight is specific, linked to a named AQAL dimension, and connected to a concrete next action |

Passing threshold: ≥ 11 / 15 (sum across all dimensions).

## Full Retrieval Schedule Summary (AABBCC → ABCABC Progression)

**Topic cluster definitions:**

- **A — Quadrant literacy:** definitions, examples, tagging, concept mapping.
- **B — Levels and lines:** L1–L4 bands, developmental line distinctions, scenario application.
- **C — States, types, and synthesis:** states vs. stages, type variation, full 5-dimension analysis.

| Week | Day | Cluster | Retrieval Task | Pattern |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Day 2 | A | 5-item quadrant definitions quiz | AABBCC — blocked on A |
| 1 | Day 4 | A | Scenario-to-quadrant matching (5 scenarios) | AABBCC — blocked on A |
| 1 | Day 8 | A | Written reflection: apply quadrant map to new situation | AABBCC — blocked on A |
| 2 | Day 2 | B | 5-item levels + lines definitions quiz | AABBCC — blocked on B |
| 2 | Day 4 | B | Scenario: identify level and line for three characters | AABBCC — blocked on B |
| 2 | Day 8 | B | Written: level-line mismatch observation | AABBCC — blocked on B |
| 3 | Day 2 | A+B+C | 6-item interleaved quiz (2A + 2B + 2C) | ABCABC — interleaved |
| 3 | Day 4 | A+B+C | Three-part scenario: quadrant / level+line / state+type | ABCABC — interleaved |
| 3 | Day 8 | A+B+C | Written: decision using 2+ AQAL dimensions | ABCABC — interleaved |
| 4 | Day 2 | A+B+C | 6-item cumulative interleaved quiz | ABCABC — interleaved |
| 4 | Day 4 | A+B+C | Full AQAL analysis of new scenario (15 min timed) | ABCABC — interleaved |
| 4 | Day 8 | A+B+C | Written: 30-day forward intention + AQAL dimension link | ABCABC — interleaved |

**Design rationale:** Weeks 1-2 use blocked practice so that novices build fluency in each concept cluster before combining them. Weeks 3-4 shift to interleaving, which is more cognitively demanding but produces stronger long-term retention and transfer (Kornell & Bjork, 2008; Rohrer & Taylor, 2007).

## Facilitation Notes

- Keep examples workplace-relevant and grounded in learner contexts.
- Encourage peer discussion but prevent abstract theoretical drift; anchor back to observable behaviors.
- Require citation or a specific example for every non-trivial claim in reflections and memos.
- For learners who default strongly to one quadrant, offer an optional "counter-quadrant" challenge: frame the same scenario from the least-preferred quadrant first.
- Facilitator is not required for Tier 1 delivery; a course moderator checking in at the end of each week is recommended for cohort settings.
- Performance task passing thresholds are intentionally scaffolded: week 1 sets a low bar (≥ 5/9, ~56%) to build confidence and reveal gaps; thresholds rise to ≥ 8/12 (67%) in week 2, ≥ 9/12 (75%) in week 3, and ≥ 11/15 (73%) in week 4. Faculty may adjust thresholds based on cohort readiness data from the pilot.

## Evidence and Citations

Evidence quality tiers follow the definitions in the [Evidence Vetting Checklist](../quality/evidence-vetting-checklist): Tier 1 = RCT or meta-analysis; Tier 2 = strong observational or systematic review; Tier 3 = theoretical or expert consensus.

| Claim | Source | Evidence Quality Tier | Caveat |
|---|---|---|---|
| Retrieval practice improves long-term retention compared to re-reading | Roediger & Karpicke (2006), *Psychological Science* | Tier 1 (RCT) | Effect sizes vary by material complexity; quizzes must be low-stakes to avoid test anxiety |
| Interleaved practice produces stronger transfer than blocked practice | Kornell & Bjork (2008), *Journal of Experimental Psychology* | Tier 1 (RCT) | Interleaving initially feels harder; learner briefing on this effect is recommended |
| Spacing practice across 24h, 72h, and 7d optimizes retention curves | Cepeda et al. (2006), *Psychological Bulletin* | Tier 1 (meta-analysis) | Optimal spacing ratios depend on retention interval; 7-day gap is a conservative estimate |
| Interleaving outperforms blocked practice on transfer and post-delay retention | Rohrer & Taylor (2007), *Instructional Science* | Tier 1 (experimental) | Effect observed primarily with procedural tasks; concept-based tasks show similar patterns |
| Multiple intelligences (Gardner) supports distinct developmental lines concept | Gardner (1983), *Frames of Mind* | Tier 3 (theoretical) | Empirical support for strict modularity is contested; use as a heuristic, not a biological fact |
| AQAL framework as an integral meta-theory | Wilber (2000), *A Theory of Everything* | Tier 3 (theoretical) | Empirical validation of the full AQAL system is limited; treat as an organizational heuristic |

## Safety Notes

Safety tier: **Tier 1**. This pathway does not include emotionally intensive shadow work or high-arousal practices. The optional body-scan micro-reset in week 3 is low-intensity.

If a learner encounters distress during any reflective activity:
1. Stop the activity and use the grounding protocol: 3 slow breaths, name 5 things visible in the room, make brief notes.
2. Skip the activity and return to it another day.
3. If distress persists, contact a qualified counselor or mental health professional. This pathway does not provide therapy or clinical support.
