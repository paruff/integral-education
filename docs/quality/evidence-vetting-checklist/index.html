Evidence-Vetting Checklist

Use this checklist before publishing any learning module, claim, rubric descriptor, or controversial case study.

Gate 1: Claim Definition​

  • Claim is specific and testable.
  • Claim scope (population, context, conditions) is explicitly stated.
  • Claim type identified: descriptive, correlational, causal, normative.

Gate 2: Source Quality​

  • Primary source located and archived.
  • Source type documented (RCT, meta-analysis, cohort, qualitative, theory paper).
  • Publication and author credentials checked.
  • Funding/conflict-of-interest disclosure reviewed.
  • At least one independent corroborating source identified for non-trivial claims.

Gate 3: Method Validity​

  • Methods are appropriate to the claim.
  • Sample and context are relevant to adult online learning use case.
  • Limitations section extracted and summarized.
  • Effect sizes or practical significance documented where available.

Gate 4: Causality Guardrails​

  • Observational findings are not written as causal claims.
  • Animal/cell findings are not generalized to human outcomes without clear caveat.
  • Correlations are presented with uncertainty language.

Gate 5: Language Integrity​

  • Remove or qualify hype terms: breakthrough, cure, revolutionary, guaranteed.
  • Replace absolute statements with evidence-quality qualifiers where needed.
  • Distinguish recommendation from established evidence.

Gate 6: Citation Integrity​

  • In-text citations and references are consistent.
  • Citation points to exact claim location.
  • No citation laundering (secondary source cited as if primary).
  • AI-generated summaries are manually validated against sources.

Gate 7: Bias and Balance​

  • Counter-evidence was actively searched and documented.
  • Competing interpretations represented fairly.
  • Reviewer confirms no ideological loading language.

Gate 8: Publication Readiness​

  • Evidence quality tier assigned per claim:
    • Tier A strong convergent evidence
    • Tier B moderate evidence with known limitations
    • Tier C provisional guidance
  • Reviewer sign-off completed.
  • Escalation route used for unresolved disputes.

Red Flags (Auto-Escalation)​

  • Strong claim with weak/unclear evidence.
  • Sensitive recommendation lacking contraindications.
  • Major discrepancy between source conclusion and module claim.
  • Uncited AI output used as final authority.

Required Artifacts​

  • Evidence log (claim -> source -> quality tier -> caveat)
  • Reviewer notes and decision log
  • Final claim language approved for publication
