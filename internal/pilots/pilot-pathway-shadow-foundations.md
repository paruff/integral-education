## Pilot Pathway B: Shadow Integration Foundations (4 Weeks)

Audience: adult learners ready for low-intensity shadow literacy and projection practices with explicit safety scaffolding.

> **[HUMAN-RECOMMENDED]** This pathway is a Copilot-generated draft. Review and approval by the safety board is required before pilot deployment. See backlog issue E5-2, Task 3.

## Overview

This pathway introduces learners to shadow literacy — the ability to recognize, name, and begin to integrate disowned aspects of self — using exclusively Tier 1 (low-intensity, self-guided) practices. By the end of week 4, learners will be able to identify projection patterns, map triggers to underlying needs, and construct a personal integration protocol with clear stop rules and grounding practices.

Safety tier: **Tier 1** (self-paced, no facilitator required; Tier 2 facilitator available for escalation only).

Related backlog issue: E5-2. Related safety standard: [Shadowwork Safety Standard](../safety/shadowwork-safety-standard).

## Mandatory Safety Conditions

- Explicit written consent required before beginning week 1 and renewed at the start of each subsequent week.
- Tier 1 default throughout. Week 3 and week 4 contain optional perspective-taking activities at the Tier 1–2 boundary; these require a facilitator check-in before proceeding.
- Tier 3 practices are excluded entirely from this pilot.
- All activities are self-guided with asynchronous facilitator availability (response within 24 hours).
- Learner distress score must be ≤ 3 / 10 at each session start.

## AQAL Mapping

### Quadrants

| Quadrant | Target Competency | Primary Learning Activity | Evidence of Mastery |
|---|---|---|---|
| I (Interior-Individual) | Recognize projection patterns and reactive emotional triggers; apply shadow-literacy vocabulary to own experience | Trigger journaling, projection spotting prompts, emotion-labeling exercises | Written reflection accurately naming one projection with its trigger and felt sense |
| We (Interior-Collective) | Understand how shadow projections affect shared meaning and interpersonal dynamics | Perspective-taking scenarios, values-clarification dialogues (written) | Scenario analysis identifying one We-quadrant impact of a projection pattern |
| It (Exterior-Individual) | Observe behavioral patterns linked to projection without deep narrative exploration | Trigger frequency log, behavioral pattern notes | Pattern log with ≥ 3 entries naming trigger, behavior, and felt sense |
| Its (Exterior-Collective) | Recognize how cultural norms and shared assumptions create collective shadow patterns | Reflection prompt on social norms and disowned cultural qualities | Written note identifying one cultural assumption that reinforces a personal pattern |

### Levels

- Entry level assumption: learners have completed at least the Mindfulness Basics module and can self-regulate at a basic level.
- Progression target: movement from unreflective projection (L1) toward conscious pattern recognition (L2) and initial integration (L2–L3).
- Mastery threshold for this pilot: consistent L2 performance — learner can name and describe a projection pattern without collapsing into the experience.

### Lines

- **Emotional:** naming and tolerating emotional triggers without over-identification.
- **Cognitive:** distinguishing projection from accurate perception; understanding shadow mechanics.
- **Interpersonal:** recognizing how disowned qualities affect relational dynamics.
- **Moral:** exploring values conflicts as a site of shadow formation.

### States

- Target state: alert, grounded, and reflective (low-arousal engaged state; distress ≤ 3 / 10).
- State preparation: 2-minute grounding breath before each session (mandatory for this pathway, not optional).
- Debrief: written check-out (1–3 sentences on how you are feeling now) required at the end of every session.

### Types

- Introverts: all activities support solo written reflection; peer dialogue is never required.
- Extroverts: optional peer dialogue prompts are provided with a netiquette protocol to maintain low-disclosure norms.
- Analytic learners: pattern-mapping tables and trigger logs provide structured scaffolding.
- Narrative learners: case vignettes accompany each analytical exercise.

## Design Constraints

- Microlearning units: 3–5 minutes each, one concept per unit.
- Practice activities: 5–10 minutes each; stop at any point.
- Retrieval loops at 24 h, 72 h, and 7 d on safety cues and core concepts.
- Weeks 1–2 use blocked retrieval (cluster A = shadow literacy; cluster B = trigger and needs mapping).
- Weeks 3–4 shift to interleaved retrieval (A + B + C where C = perspective repair and integration).
- Every session ends with a grounding protocol and written check-out.

| Week | Focus | Safety Tier | Assessment |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Shadow literacy and projection basics | Tier 1 | Reflection quality check |
| 2 | Trigger patterns and reactivity mapping | Tier 1 | Trigger-to-need mapping task |

## Week 1: Shadow Literacy and Projection Basics

### Learning Objectives

1. Define shadow in plain language and explain why shadow formation is a normal developmental process.
2. Describe the mechanism of projection using at least one concrete everyday example.
3. Identify at least two personal triggers using the reactive emotion log format.
4. Demonstrate the grounding protocol (breath anchor + sensory orienting) before and after a reflective activity.
5. Acknowledge the right to stop at any time and describe what to do if distress increases.

### Micro-Lesson Sequence

| # | Title | Format | Duration | Concept |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1.1 | What Is the Shadow? | Text | 4 min | Shadow as disowned self; normal development, not pathology; brief example |
| 1.2 | How Projection Works | Text + example | 4 min | Mechanism of projection; triggers as projection signals; illustrative vignette |
| 1.3 | Grounding Before We Begin | Practice | 3 min | Mandatory breath anchor (4-count inhale, 4-count exhale × 3); sensory orienting (name 5 visible things) |
| 1.4 | The Reactive Emotion Log | Practice | 6 min | Guided trigger log: situation, emotion label, body sensation, intensity (1–10); first 2 entries |
| 1.5 | Shadow as Gift: Reframing the Concept | Text | 4 min | Shadow as a source of energy and creativity when integrated; normalizing the inquiry |
| 1.6 | Reflection: Your First Shadow Observation | Reflection | 5 min | Prompt: *What quality in others most reliably triggers a strong reaction in you? Describe the trigger and the felt sense — stop there.* |

**Formats key:** Text = written micro-lesson; Practice = interactive exercise or structured log; Reflection = structured journaling prompt.

### Safety Check-In — Week 1

Complete the following before beginning any session activity:

| Check | Prompt | Action if Concern |
|---|---|---|
| Distress level | Rate your current distress 0–10. | If ≥ 4, stop and use the grounding protocol. Return another day. |
| Consent reminder | You may stop at any point and return later. No activity requires disclosure to others. | Acknowledge: write "I understand and consent" in your log. |
| Contraindications | Are you currently experiencing acute distress, a recent loss, or a crisis state? | If yes, postpone this pathway. Contact a qualified support person. |

### Retrieval Schedule — Week 1 (AABBCC: cluster A = shadow literacy)

| Day | Retrieval Type | Items | Spacing |
|---|---|---|---|
| Day 2 (24 h) | Cluster A recall | 5 short-answer: define shadow, projection, trigger, integration, felt sense | First retrieval after initial learning |
| Day 4 (72 h) | Cluster A recognition drill | Match 5 scenarios to the correct shadow mechanism (projection, trigger, or integration attempt) | Spaced reinforcement |
| Day 8 (7 d) | Cluster A reflection | Written: describe one moment this week when you noticed a possible projection. What was the trigger? What was the felt sense? Stop before interpreting. | Consolidation retrieval |

### Week 1 Performance Task

**Task:** Complete a reactive emotion log with a minimum of 3 entries across the week. Each entry must include: (1) a brief situation description; (2) the emotion label; (3) the body sensation; (4) an intensity rating (1–10); (5) one sentence noting the quality you reacted to in the other person — stop there. Do not analyze or interpret.

**Rubric:**

| Dimension | Developing (1) | Meeting (2) | Exceeding (3) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Log completeness | Fewer than 3 entries or missing fields | 3 entries with all 5 fields completed | 3+ entries, all fields complete, entries across different situations |
| Emotion labeling | Vague labels (e.g., "bad," "upset") | Specific emotion labels (e.g., "contempt," "shame," "irritation") | Specific labels with body sensation linked to the emotion |
| Tone and containment | Entry slips into interpretation or storytelling | Entry stays descriptive; quality named without deep analysis | Entry is concise, descriptive, and demonstrates containment |

Passing threshold: ≥ 5 / 9.

## Week 2: Trigger Patterns and Reactivity Mapping

### Learning Objectives

1. Distinguish between a trigger (the event) and the underlying unmet need or disowned quality it reveals.
2. Map at least two trigger entries from week 1 to a possible underlying need or disowned quality.
3. Use the assumption-mapping format to chart one recurring interpersonal assumption.
4. Apply the cognitive reframing prompt to one automatic negative interpretation.
5. Describe the difference between emotional regulation (managing the state) and integration (owning the pattern).

### Micro-Lesson Sequence

| # | Title | Format | Duration | Concept |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2.1 | Triggers and Needs: The Hidden Link | Text | 4 min | Trigger as signal of unmet need or disowned quality; moving from reaction to inquiry |
| 2.2 | Projection Spotting: A Structured Method | Text + practice | 5 min | Step-by-step projection check: name the quality → ask "where in me?" → rate certainty (not claim it as fact) |
| 2.3 | Assumption Mapping | Practice | 6 min | Scaffold: recurring situation → my assumption → impact on my behavior → alternative reading |
| 2.4 | Cognitive Reframing for Shadow Patterns | Text + practice | 5 min | Guided reframe: identify the automatic interpretation, question it, generate one alternative |
| 2.5 | Regulation vs. Integration | Text | 4 min | Regulation = managing the activated state; integration = owning the disowned quality; both matter, different purposes |
| 2.6 | Reflection: Trigger-to-Need Mapping | Reflection | 6 min | Prompt: *Take one trigger from your week 1 log. What need was not being met? What quality were you reacting to in the other person that you have not owned in yourself? Write without judgment.* |
| 2.7 | Grounding Practice: Body Scan + Emotion Label | Practice | 4 min | Guided 2-min body scan; label any sensations with emotion words; write 2–3 sentences |

### Safety Check-In — Week 2

| Check | Prompt | Action if Concern |
|---|---|---|
| Distress level | Rate your current distress 0–10. | If ≥ 4, stop. Use grounding protocol. Do not proceed to week 2 content today. |
| Consent renewal | Review: this work involves looking at your own reactions — not analyzing others. You are the subject of inquiry. | Acknowledge: write "I consent and understand the scope" in your log. |
| Readiness check | Were you able to complete at least 2 log entries in week 1 without significant distress? | If no, repeat week 1 before continuing. |

### Retrieval Schedule — Week 2 (AABBCC: cluster B = trigger and needs mapping)

| Day | Retrieval Type | Items | Spacing |
|---|---|---|---|
| Day 2 (24 h) | Cluster B recall | 5 short-answer: define trigger, need, assumption mapping, cognitive reframing, regulation vs. integration | First retrieval |
| Day 4 (72 h) | Cluster B application drill | Given 3 trigger scenarios, map each to a possible underlying need; no right answer — justify your reasoning | Spaced reinforcement |
| Day 8 (7 d) | Cluster B reflection | Written: identify one recurring assumption you hold in a particular relationship. What would change if the assumption were only 50% true? | Consolidation retrieval |

### Week 2 Performance Task

**Task:** Complete a trigger-to-need mapping for two entries from your week 1 reactive emotion log. For each entry: (1) restate the trigger situation in one sentence; (2) name the quality you reacted to in the other person; (3) ask "where in me does this quality exist, even slightly?" and write one sentence answering the question — no self-criticism required; (4) name the underlying need that was not met.

**Rubric:**

| Dimension | Developing (1) | Meeting (2) | Exceeding (3) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Trigger clarity | Trigger vague or describes the other person at length | Trigger stated as a brief situation; focus stays on own reaction | Trigger concisely stated; emotion and body sensation both included |
| Projection inquiry | Skips the "where in me" question or denies the quality entirely | Attempts the inquiry; identifies one possible connection | Identifies connection with specificity; notes degree ("slightly" or "sometimes") without over-claiming |
| Needs identification | Need not identified | One specific need named | Need named and linked to the disowned quality with brief reasoning |

Passing threshold: ≥ 5 / 9 per entry (complete at least 2 entries).

## Week 3: Role Reversal and Perspective Repair

### Learning Objectives

1. Explain what perspective-taking in shadow work involves and what it does not involve (it is not the same as relationship counselling or requiring contact with another person).
2. Complete a written role-reversal thought experiment for one trigger scenario.
3. Identify at least one way the other person's perspective might reveal a quality you are not currently owning.
4. Practice a values-clarification exercise to surface one core value that is activated in a trigger pattern.
5. Apply the structured dialogue (written parts dialogue) format at the Tier 1 level.

> **Tier boundary note:** The written parts dialogue activity (lesson 3.5) sits at the Tier 1–2 boundary. Before beginning lesson 3.5, complete the facilitator check-in (see safety checkpoint below). If no facilitator is available, use the Tier 1 fallback alternative (written letter to the quality, not sent).

### Micro-Lesson Sequence

| # | Title | Format | Duration | Concept |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 3.1 | What Role Reversal Is (and Is Not) | Text | 4 min | Written perspective-taking as a cognitive exercise; no contact required; not about excusing harm |
| 3.2 | The Role Reversal Thought Experiment | Practice | 6 min | Step-by-step: imagine the situation from the other person's position; write what they might be protecting or needing; stay brief |
| 3.3 | Values Clarification: What Is at Stake? | Practice | 5 min | Prompt: name the value you felt was violated in the trigger situation; where did this value come from? |
| 3.4 | Projection and Values: The Hidden Overlap | Text | 4 min | Disowned qualities are often also disowned values; the trigger often reveals a value you want to live more fully |
| 3.5 | Written Parts Dialogue (Tier 1 version) | Practice | 8 min | Structured written dialogue: "the part of me that reacts" vs. "the part of me that observes"; two-voice format; stop after one exchange |
| 3.6 | Fallback Alternative: Letter to the Quality | Practice | 6 min | Write a short letter to the disowned quality; thank it for its protective function; do not send |
| 3.7 | Reflection: What Did the Other Perspective Reveal? | Reflection | 5 min | Prompt: *After the role reversal or parts dialogue, what did you learn about the quality you are not yet owning? Write one sentence — no more.* |

### Safety Checkpoint — Week 3 (Tier Boundary)

Before attempting lesson 3.5 (Written Parts Dialogue):

| Check | Prompt | Action |
|---|---|---|
| Facilitator check-in | Has a Tier 2-available facilitator been notified that you are beginning lesson 3.5? | Yes → proceed. No → use the letter-to-quality fallback (lesson 3.6) instead. |
| Distress level | Rate distress 0–10 right now. | If ≥ 3, use fallback (lesson 3.6). |
| Readiness | Were you able to complete the week 2 task without significant distress? | If no, repeat week 2 content or consult facilitator before continuing. |

Stop-rule for week 3: if the role reversal or parts dialogue produces a distress spike to ≥ 5 / 10, stop immediately, use the grounding protocol, write the check-out statement, and notify the facilitator channel.

The artifact must include: (1) a one-sentence context (what triggered this practice); (2) the practice output (dialogue or letter); (3) a one-sentence check-out statement confirming your current distress level is ≤ 3 / 10.

## Week 4: Integration Plan and Boundary Setting

### Learning Objectives

1. Define integration in the context of shadow work: making the unconscious pattern conscious and choosing a new relationship to it.
2. Construct a personal trigger-to-integration protocol for one recurring interpersonal pattern.
3. Articulate at least one personal boundary that the integration practice is supporting.
4. Complete a self-assessment of shadow literacy competency across the four-week pathway.
5. Identify the stop rules and grounding protocol you will use if distress arises in ongoing personal practice.

> **Tier boundary note:** The integration protocol task (lesson 4.4) is at the Tier 1–2 boundary if the learner chooses to work with a more charged pattern. Facilitate a check-in before beginning this task if distress has been elevated during the pathway.

**Task:** Produce a personal trigger-to-integration protocol for one recurring interpersonal pattern. The protocol must include: (1) the trigger situation described in 1–2 sentences; (2) the emotion and body sensation; (3) the disowned quality being projected; (4) the underlying unmet need; (5) the integration intention in one sentence; (6) one personal boundary that supports this intention; (7) the stop rules and grounding steps you will use; (8) a check-out statement confirming current distress ≤ 3 / 10.

**Rubric:**

| Dimension | Developing (1) | Meeting (2) | Exceeding (3) |

## Safety Checkpoints and Escalation Path

| Step | Action | Tool |
|---|---|---|

### Weekly Safety Self-Report

At the end of each week, complete the following self-report:

| Item | Prompt |
|---|---|
| Session completion | How many sessions did you complete this week (out of planned)? |
| Peak distress | What was the highest distress rating you experienced this week (0–10)? |
| Stop-rule use | Did you use a stop rule this week? If yes, describe briefly. |
| Grounding use | Did you use the grounding protocol? Was it effective? |
| Concerns | Any concerns to flag to the facilitator? |

### Facilitator Check-In Prompts (Weekly)

Facilitators reviewing weekly self-reports should ask:

- "Your peak distress was [X]. How are you feeling now, compared to the start of the week?"
- "Did anything surprise you in the practice? What felt manageable? What felt like a stretch?"
- "Is there anything you want to hold off on in the coming week?"

### Stop Rules

Stop the practice immediately if any of the following occur:

| Condition | Action |
|---|---|
| Distress rises to ≥ 5 / 10 during a session | Exit activity immediately; use grounding protocol; write check-out; notify facilitator channel |
| Escalating panic, dissociation, or loss of present-moment orientation | Stop all activities; use grounding protocol; contact facilitator or crisis support if not resolved within 10 minutes |
| Expression of risk to self or others | Stop immediately; contact designated safety escalation contact (see below) |
| Repeated inability to regulate with grounding protocol over two sessions | Pause pathway; consult facilitator before continuing |
| Any contraindicated condition emerges during the pathway | Pause pathway; consult facilitator; do not continue without clearance |

### Grounding and Stabilization Protocol

Use this sequence whenever distress increases during a session, or whenever a stop rule is triggered:

1. **Breath anchor:** 4-count inhale through the nose, hold for 2, 4-count exhale through the mouth. Repeat three times.
2. **Sensory orienting:** Name aloud or in writing — 5 things you can see, 4 you can touch, 3 you can hear, 2 you can smell, 1 you can taste.
3. **Self-location:** Say or write: "I am [name]. I am in [location]. Today is [date]. I am safe right now."
4. **Gentle movement (optional):** Slowly roll shoulders; place feet flat on the floor; feel the weight of your body in the chair.
5. **Write a brief check-out:** In 1–3 sentences, note how you are feeling now. Rate distress 0–10.

If distress does not drop below 4 / 10 after this protocol, stop the session and contact the facilitator.

### Escalation Path

| Level | Condition | Action | Contact |
|---|---|---|---|
| Tier 1 self-management | Distress ≥ 4 / 10 at session start | Use grounding protocol; skip session; return another day | No escalation required |
| Tier 1 → facilitator check-in | Distress ≥ 5 / 10 during session; stop-rule triggered | Exit activity; use grounding; notify facilitator asynchronously within the session | Facilitator channel (response within 24 h) |
| Tier 1 → Tier 2 threshold | Peak distress ≥ 6 / 10 across two consecutive sessions; or any stop-rule event involving dissociation | Pause pathway; facilitator conducts a synchronous check-in before learner continues | Designated Tier 2 facilitator — `[HUMAN-REQUIRED: name and contact to be populated]` |
| Immediate escalation | Any expression of risk to self or others; acute crisis; unable to orient after full grounding protocol | Stop all activity; contact crisis support immediately | Crisis support — `[HUMAN-REQUIRED: crisis line and local contact to be populated]` |

## Session Flow

Every session in this pathway follows this sequence:

1. Consent check and readiness scan (distress rating + consent acknowledgment).
2. Grounding breath (mandatory 3 cycles).
3. Practice briefing with contraindications noted.
4. Timed activity (5–10 min; stop at any point).
5. Grounding protocol (repeat if needed).
6. Reflection capture (written check-out: 1–3 sentences; distress rating).
7. Optional peer dialogue (with netiquette protocol; low-disclosure; never required).

## Full Retrieval Schedule Summary (AABBCC → ABCABC Progression)

**Topic cluster definitions:**

- **A — Shadow literacy:** shadow definition, projection mechanism, trigger recognition, reactive emotion log format.
- **B — Trigger and needs mapping:** trigger-to-need, assumption mapping, cognitive reframing, regulation vs. integration.
- **C — Perspective repair and integration:** role reversal, parts dialogue, values clarification, integration protocol format.

| Week | Day | Cluster | Retrieval Task | Pattern |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Day 2 | A | 5-item recall: define shadow, projection, trigger, integration, felt sense | AABBCC — blocked on A |
| 1 | Day 4 | A | Match 5 scenarios to shadow mechanism | AABBCC — blocked on A |
| 1 | Day 8 | A | Written reflection: one projection observation this week | AABBCC — blocked on A |
| 2 | Day 2 | B | 5-item recall: trigger, need, assumption mapping, reframing, regulation vs. integration | AABBCC — blocked on B |
| 2 | Day 4 | B | Map 3 trigger scenarios to underlying needs | AABBCC — blocked on B |
| 2 | Day 8 | B | Written: one recurring assumption and an alternative reading | AABBCC — blocked on B |
| 3 | Day 2 | A+B+C | 6-item interleaved quiz (2A + 2B + 2C) | ABCABC — interleaved |
| 3 | Day 4 | A+B+C | Three-part scenario: projection / trigger-to-need / value at stake | ABCABC — interleaved |
| 3 | Day 8 | A+B+C | Written: one projection you caught this week | ABCABC — interleaved |
| 4 | Day 2 | A+B+C | 6-item cumulative interleaved quiz | ABCABC — interleaved |
| 4 | Day 4 | A+B+C | New scenario: full projection-to-integration analysis | ABCABC — interleaved |
| 4 | Day 8 | A+B+C | Written: 30-day forward practice intention | ABCABC — interleaved |

**Design rationale:** Weeks 1–2 use blocked retrieval so that learners build fluency with each concept cluster before combining them. Weeks 3–4 shift to interleaving, which produces stronger transfer and long-term retention (Kornell & Bjork, 2008; Rohrer & Taylor, 2007). Retrieval items for shadow content are kept at low emotional intensity (recognition and description, not deep excavation) to remain within Tier 1 constraints.

## Facilitation Notes

- All activities are designed for low emotional intensity. Do not push learners to go deeper than the prompt invites.
- Peer dialogue is always optional and governed by a low-disclosure netiquette protocol (no sharing of raw trigger content without explicit consent).
- Facilitator is not required for Tier 1 delivery. A course moderator checking weekly self-reports is strongly recommended for any cohort setting.
- Tier 2 facilitator must be designated and available for escalation before pilot deployment.
- For learners who engage very analytically (intellectualizing about shadow rather than noticing felt sense), gently redirect to body sensation and emotion label: "What do you notice in your body right now?"
- For learners who over-disclose or over-engage emotionally, the containment rubric dimensions are your tool: acknowledge the experience briefly and redirect to the structured format.
- Performance task passing thresholds are scaffolded: week 1 and week 3 set a 56% bar (≥ 5/9); week 2 sets a similar 56% bar for two entries; week 4 capstone rises to 73% (≥ 11/15). Faculty may adjust thresholds based on pilot data.

## Safety Notes

Safety tier: **Tier 1**. This pathway uses exclusively low-intensity shadow-literacy practices: trigger journaling, projection spotting, cognitive reframing, written perspective-taking, and integration planning. No trauma-adjacent narratives, deep parts work, or high-arousal processing is included.

This platform provides educational and developmental practices only. It does not provide therapy, clinical treatment, or crisis intervention. If you are experiencing acute psychological distress, please contact a qualified mental health professional.

If distress increases during any activity:
1. Stop the activity immediately.
2. Use the grounding protocol (see Safety Checkpoints section).
3. Write your check-out statement and rate distress.
4. If distress does not drop below 4 / 10 after grounding, stop the session and contact the facilitator.
5. If you are in crisis, contact emergency services or a crisis support line immediately.

Learners who have recently experienced significant trauma, loss, assault, or a crisis event within the past 30 days should postpone this pathway until they have ongoing professional support in place.
