## Retention Test Items (Draft)

**Status:** AI draft, **NOT VALIDATED** (P5-02, #531). A human must validate
every item before use `[HUMAN-REQUIRED]`. Source: the seven modules of
`docs/quickstarts/traditional-to-rational.mdx`, per the protocol in
`retention-study-protocol.md`.

Each set has 14 items, two per module, so a learner who skipped nothing is
tested evenly. Set A and set B cover the same constructs with different
wording. Randomly assign which set is the pre-test and which is the delayed
test. Score each item 0 or 1 against the key points; an item is correct when
the learner gives **all** the required points. Do not show the keys to
learners.

Items test ideas as the modules state them. They do not test whether the
stage models are true (FR-14), and they never ask a learner to place
themselves at a stage.

## Validation checklist (for the human reviewer)

For each item, confirm and tick:

- [ ] The key matches the module text (cite the section if not)
- [ ] One correct answer; no second defensible reading
- [ ] Sets A and B are of equal difficulty for the same module
- [ ] No wording that implies a stage ranking or a diagnosis
- [ ] Understandable without having seen the module's own cards verbatim
- [ ] Reading level suitable for adult general learners

Record the reviewer, the date and any changes at the end of this file.

## Set A

**M1 · Amber/Mythic Orientation**

- **A1.** Name three strengths of the Amber/Mythic stage. *Key:* any three of moral conviction, belonging/community identity, willingness to sacrifice and serve, narrative coherence, role clarity/reliability.
- **A2.** In Cook-Greuter's terms, what is the Conformist stage, and why can deviating from group expectations feel threatening there? *Key:* identity is fused with role, group and norms (1); so deviation feels like a threat to the self (2).

**M2 · Rational/Orange Orientation**

- **A3.** How does Orange differ from Amber on where authority comes from? *Key:* Amber places authority outside the self (tradition, institution); Orange builds internal standards and judges authority claims against them.
- **A4.** Someone says "only what can be measured is real." Is this the Orange stage or a distortion of it? Explain. *Key:* a distortion (scientism / Orange made absolute and defensive), not the developmental achievement itself.

**M3 · Cognitive Dissonance Bridge**

- **A5.** In Kegan's terms, what does it mean for something to move from "subject" to "object"? *Key:* what you were embedded in and could not see becomes something you can examine and reflect on.
- **A6.** A friend meets a fact that clashes with a long-held belief and decides the source must be biased. What is the difference between that response and revising the frame? *Key:* dismissing reduces discomfort without learning; revising the frame changes how one knows and produces growth.

**M4 · Perspective-Taking Capacity**

- **A7.** What is a steel man argument? *Key:* the strongest possible version of a position you disagree with, not an easy-to-dismiss caricature.
- **A8.** Name the three AQAL perspectives (persons of the verb). *Key:* 1st person (I), 2nd person (You/We), 3rd person (It/Its).

**M5 · Authority & Autonomy Transition**

- **A9.** Name three things that can genuinely be lost when an Amber authority structure gives way. *Key:* any three of belonging, certainty, cosmic order, clear moral roles.
- **A10.** What is "repressive transcendence" in the Amber to Rational transition? *Key:* gaining the new by denying the real value of the old, i.e. treating Amber as merely primitive.

**M6 · Critical Thinking Foundations**

- **A11.** Name the four parts of the Toulmin model. *Key:* claim, evidence/grounds, warrant, qualifier.
- **A12.** "That claim can't be true, because accepting it would make life unbearable." Which fallacy is this, and why is it a fallacy? *Key:* argument from consequences; undesirability of an implication is not evidence the claim is false.

**M7 · Evidence Evaluation**

- **A13.** What does SIFT stand for? *Key:* Stop, Investigate the source, Find better coverage, Trace claims.
- **A14.** Ice cream sales and sunburn rise together in summer. Why can't we conclude that one causes the other? *Key:* a third variable (hot weather/sun) or other non-causal explanation; correlation alone does not establish causation.

## Set B

**M1 · Amber/Mythic Orientation**

- **B1.** Name three limitations associated with the Amber/Mythic stage. *Key:* any three of authority dependence, in-group/out-group rigidity, concrete-literal thinking (difficulty with ambiguity), externalized self-evaluation.
- **B2.** Why does the module name Amber's strengths before its limitations? *Key:* learners at or near Amber may disengage or feel pathologized if limits come first; strengths-first is respectful and supports growth.

**M2 · Rational/Orange Orientation**

- **B3.** Name three strengths of the Rational/Orange stage. *Key:* any three of evidence-based reasoning, individual autonomy, scientific method, institutional reform capacity, meritocratic fairness.
- **B4.** Name two shadows or limitations of Orange. *Key:* any two of meaning deficit/flatness, reductionism, extractive economics/ecological blindness, alienation from community.

**M3 · Cognitive Dissonance Bridge**

- **B5.** What is a "disorienting dilemma" (Mezirow)? *Key:* an experience that existing frames cannot interpret, triggering reflection on the assumptions behind the frame.
- **B6.** What does Kegan's "holding environment" provide during a transition? *Key:* enough support to tolerate disorientation without collapsing back or fragmenting.

**M4 · Perspective-Taking Capacity**

- **B7.** What does "postconventional" moral reasoning mean in Kohlberg's terms? *Key:* reasoning from self-authored principles (justice, dignity, autonomy) that stand apart from any group's approval or authority.
- **B8.** What does the Rational stage add to Amber's perspective-taking? *Key:* the capacity to hold one's own view as one perspective among several (third-person perspective), rather than judging others only from inside the in-group.

**M5 · Authority & Autonomy Transition**

- **B9.** What is the difference between Kegan's Stage 3 and Stage 4 in relation to authority? *Key:* Stage 3 is embedded in the authority structure; Stage 4 has an interior standpoint from which to evaluate it.
- **B10.** What three conditions does Kegan's model say a developmental transition needs? *Key:* confirmation, contradiction, continuity.

**M6 · Critical Thinking Foundations**

- **B11.** What is a warrant, and why make it explicit? *Key:* the bridge from evidence to claim (1); making it explicit shows whether it is true and shared (2).
- **B12.** "Either you trust the experts completely or you reject science." Which fallacy is this? *Key:* false dichotomy (a binary presented when more options exist).

**M7 · Evidence Evaluation**

- **B13.** What does a systematic review add beyond a single randomized controlled trial? *Key:* combines multiple independent studies by defined criteria, reducing the weight of any one study's limits.
- **B14.** What is the shift from Amber to Rational knowing, in terms of the question asked? *Key:* from "Who said it?" to "How well is this supported?" (additive, not a replacement of trust).
